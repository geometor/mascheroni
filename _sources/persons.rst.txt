.. rubric:: Prosopographical Register of Geometers, Translators, Commentators & Patrons in Compass-Only Geometry

Authors, Translators & Editors
------------------------------

.. _person-mascheroni:

Lorenzo Mascheroni (1750 – 1800)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Italian Mathematician, Poet & Professor of Algebra and Geometry at the University of Pavia*

Lorenzo Mascheroni (13 May 1750, Castagneta near Bergamo – 14 July 1800, Paris) was an Italian mathematician, poet, and rector of the University of Pavia. In 1790 he published *Adnotationes ad calculum integralem Euleri*, computing 32 decimal places of Euler's constant and introducing what is today known as the **Euler–Mascheroni constant** :math:`\gamma \approx 0.5772156649\dots`

In 1797 in Pavia, he published his crowning masterpiece, *La Geometria del Compasso*, dedicated in Italian hendecasyllabic verse to General :person:`Napoléon Bonaparte <bonaparte>`. Across twelve books and 213 propositions, Mascheroni proved constructively that every Euclidean problem solvable by ruler and compass can be executed with the **compass alone**, representing straight lines implicitly by pairs of points. Appointed delegate of the Cisalpine Republic to Paris in 1798 to assist in fixing the metric system, Mascheroni was stranded by renewed war and died in Paris in 1800.

.. _person-carette:

Antoine-Michel Carette (1772 – 1855)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*French Military Engineer Officer & Classical Translator*

Antoine-Michel Carette was an officer of military engineers (*officier du génie*) who produced the authoritative French translation of Mascheroni's treatise, *Géométrie du compas, par L. Mascheroni* (Paris: Duprat, 1798), within months of the Pavia *editio princeps*. Through Carette's lucid translation, Mascheroni's problem numbering and compass-only techniques entered the French mathematical mainstream at the École Polytechnique, directly inspiring the geometric work of Poncelet, Brianchon, Cauchy, and later :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>`.

.. _person-fazzari:

Gaetano Fazzari (1856 – 1935)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

*Italian Mathematician, Historian of Mathematics in Palermo & Founder of Il Pitagora*

Professor of mathematics at the Royal Technical Institute of Palermo and founder of the journal *Il Pitagora* (1895–1918). Fazzari published the definitive centenary critical edition of *La Geometria del Compasso* (Palermo: Era Nova, 1901). In his extensive historical preface (pp. v–xvi), Fazzari famously evaluated Mascheroni's constructions using :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>`'s *Géométrographie* (p. xi), demonstrating quantitatively that Mascheroni's compass-only solutions—such as the concentric fourth proportional (§ 93)—are often simpler in hand execution than traditional Euclidean straightedge constructions.

Historical Predecessors, Interlocutors & Modern Geometers
---------------------------------------------------------

.. _person-mohr:

**Georg Mohr** (1640 – 1697) — *Danish Mathematician & Author of Euclides Danicus (1672)*
   Published *Euclides Danicus* in Dutch and Danish at Amsterdam in 1672—125 years before Mascheroni—proving that all constructions of Euclid Books I–VI can be executed with compass alone. The treatise remained entirely overlooked as an optical work until :person:`Johannes Hjelmslev <hjelmslev>` rediscovered and republished it in 1928. The theorem is thus known today as the **Mohr–Mascheroni Theorem**.

.. _person-bonaparte:

**Napoléon Bonaparte** (1769 – 1821) — *General, French Statesman & Member of the Institut National*
   Met Lorenzo Mascheroni in Lombardy in 1797 during the Italian campaign and received the poetic dedication of *La Geometria del Compasso*. Upon returning to Paris in December 1797, Bonaparte astonished Laplace and Lagrange at the Institut National by demonstrating Mascheroni's compass-only circle quadrisection, provoking Laplace's celebrated compliment: *"Nous attendions tout de vous, général, excepté des leçons de géométrie."*

.. _person-adler:

**August Adler** (1863 – 1923) — *Czech-Austrian Geometer in Vienna and Prague*
   In *Zur Theorie der Mascheronischen Constructionen* (1890) and *Theorie der geometrischen Konstruktionen* (1906), gave the unified modern proof of the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem using **circular inversion** (:math:`5C_1 + 3C_3`), reducing line–line and line–circle intersections directly to circle–circle intersections.

.. _person-euclid:

**Euclid of Alexandria** (fl. c. 300 BCE) — *Alexandrian Mathematician & Author of the Elements*
   Author of the *Elements*, whose first three constructive postulates define Euclidean plane geometry. Mascheroni demonstrated that Postulates 1 and 2 (drawing and extending continuous straight lines) are redundant for determining all geometric points: Postulate 3 (compass circle sweep) alone suffices.

.. _person-steiner:

**Jakob Steiner** (1796 – 1863) — *Swiss Synthetic Projective Geometer & Professor in Berlin*
   Proved the dual minimal-instrument theorem in 1833 (*Die geometrischen Constructionen, ausgeführt mittelst der geraden Linie und Eines festen Kreises*): the straightedge *alone* suffices for all Euclidean constructions, provided a single fixed circle and its center are given in the plane.

.. _person-lemoine:

**Émile Michel Hyacinthe Lemoine** (1840 – 1912) — *French Civil Engineer, Modern Triangle Geometer & Creator of Géométrographie*
   Formulated *Géométrographie* in 1888–1902 (*Scientia* No. 18), providing the formal operation counts (:math:`m_1 C_1 + m_2 C_2 + m_3 C_3`), simplicity :math:`S`, and exactitude :math:`E` measuring Mascheroni's compass-only constructions.

.. _person-laplace:

**Pierre-Simon, Marquis de Laplace** (1749 – 1827) — *French Mathematician & Astronomer*
   Member of the Institut National present when General Bonaparte demonstrated Mascheroni's compass constructions in Paris in December 1797.

.. _person-lagrange:

**Joseph-Louis Lagrange** (1736 – 1813) — *Italian-born French Analyst & Academician*
   Examined Mascheroni's methods presented by Bonaparte at the Institut National in Paris and recognized the foundational constructive power of pure compass arcs.

.. _person-hjelmslev:

**Johannes Trolle Hjelmslev** (1873 – 1950) — *Danish Geometer & Historian of Mathematics in Copenhagen*
   Examined a 1672 Amsterdam copy of Georg Mohr's *Euclides Danicus* in 1928, establishing Mohr's historical priority for the compass-only theorem 125 years before Mascheroni.

.. _person-phi-architect:

**phi ARCHITECT** (contemporary) — *GEOMETOR Curator & Symbolic Modeler*
   Curator of the GEOMETOR Digital Scriptorium, implementing exact symbolic geometry models and synchronized Book Codices.
