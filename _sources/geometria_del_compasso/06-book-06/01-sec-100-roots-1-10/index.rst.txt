=============================================
§§ 100–103: Square Roots √1 through √10 & √36
=============================================

.. folio:: 0076
   :page: 51

**§ 100. Problem (Fig. 43, folio 0076, p. 51).** Construct easily the square roots of the integers from 1 to 10, taking :math:`AB = 1` as unit.

*Construction:* Draw unit circle :g:`( A B )` and step its six hexagon vertices :g:`B`, :g:`C`, :g:`D`, :g:`E`, :g:`d`, :g:`c`. With radius :math:`BD = \sqrt{3}` from :g:`B, E` locate :g:`a` and :g:`alpha` (:math:`Aa = \sqrt{2}`), and from :g:`D, d` locate :g:`V` on the extended diameter (:math:`AV = 2, BV = 3`). Mark quadrant point :g:`F` (:math:`BF = \sqrt{2}`) and intersect :math:`B(1) \cap F(1) =` :g:`T` (completing unit square :math:`ABTF`). Every integer square root from 1 to 10 now stands explicitly between pairs of already-marked points:

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   AB &= \sqrt{1}, & aV &= \sqrt{6}, \\
   Aa &= \sqrt{2}, & CV &= \sqrt{7}, \\
   BD &= \sqrt{3}, & a\alpha &= \sqrt{8}, \\
   BE &= \sqrt{4}, & BV &= \sqrt{9}, \\
   ET &= \sqrt{5}, & TV &= \sqrt{10}.
   \end{aligned}

**§§ 101–103 (Fig. 44, folio 0077, p. 52).** Subtracting each residue :math:`1, \dots, 10` from the squares :math:`16, 25, 36` via a right triangle yields every square root up to :math:`\sqrt{36}` (illustrated explicitly by :math:`\sqrt{29} = \sqrt{36 - 7}`).
