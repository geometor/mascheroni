================================
Summary: Dedication to Bonaparte
================================

.. rubric:: Historical Context and Mathematical Encounter (Pavia, 1797)

.. folio:: 0030

Encounter at the University of Pavia
-------------------------------------

In May 1796, General :person:`Napoléon Bonaparte <bonaparte>` entered Lombardy during the Italian Campaign. Following the armistice of Leoben and the Treaty of Campoformio (October 1797), Bonaparte visited the historic University of Pavia, where :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>` served as Professor of Higher Mathematics.

Trained as an artillery officer at the *École Militaire* in Paris under :person:`Pierre-Simon Laplace <laplace>`, Bonaparte was passionately interested in geometry. Mascheroni demonstrated to the young general his newly discovered geometric theory: that all Euclidean plane constructions could be executed with the compass alone, entirely dispensing with the straightedge.

The 1797 Editio Princeps
------------------------

Mascheroni dedicated his treatise to Bonaparte as *Bonaparte l'Italico* in fourteen hendecasyllabic verses (*endecasillabi sciolti*) celebrating both the military strategist and the acute geometric intellect. The volume was printed in Pavia by the heirs of Pietro Galeazzi in Year V of the French Republic (*Anno V della Repubblica Francese*, 1797).

Transmission to the Institut National
-------------------------------------

In December 1797, following his triumphant return to Paris, Bonaparte presented Mascheroni's problems to the *Institut de France* (*Classe des sciences mathématiques et physiques*), proposing them directly to Laplace and :person:`Joseph-Louis Lagrange <lagrange>`. This sparked immediate European recognition and led directly to :person:`Antoine-Michel Carette <carette>`'s authorized French translation, published in Paris in 1798.
