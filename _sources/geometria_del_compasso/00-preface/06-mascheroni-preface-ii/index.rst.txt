======================================================
Mascheroni's Preface: Architecture of the Twelve Books
======================================================

.. folio:: 0036
   :page: 7

In the concluding pages of his *Prefazione* (pp. 7–9, folio 0036–folio 0038), :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>` lays out the complete deductive architecture of his **twelve books** encompassing **280 numbered sections** (§§ 1–280) and **108 geometric figures** (*Fig. 1*–*Fig. 108*):

* **Book I (§§ 1–26):** Preliminaries, compass conventions, symmetry, and metric chord lemmas.
* **Book II (§§ 27–63):** Exact division of the circumference into 4, 8, 12, 24, 48, 5, 10, 20, 120, and 240 parts.
* **Book III (§§ 64–70):** Multiplication, bisection, trisection, and *n*-section of straight distances.
* **Book IV (§§ 71–85):** Addition and subtraction of distances, perpendiculars, parallels, and verification tests.
* **Book V (§§ 86–99):** Third, fourth, and mean proportionals, plus the Golden Section.
* **Book VI (§§ 100–109):** Exact square roots :math:`\sqrt{1}` through :math:`\sqrt{36}` and half-roots :math:`\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{1}` through :math:`\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{25}`.
* **Book VII (§§ 110–112):** Intersections of :term:`virtual lines <retta-virtuale>` with circles and with one another — completing the proof of full Euclidean equivalence.
* **Book VIII (§§ 113–123):** Angles and trigonometric lines (sine, cosine, tangent, secant).
* **Book IX (§§ 124–142):** Similar figures, area scaling, and regular polygons inscribed, circumscribed, or on a given side.
* **Book X (§§ 143–150):** Centers of circles and polygons, opening with :term:`Napoleon's Problem <problema-di-napoleone>` (§ 143).
* **Book XI (§§ 151–192):** Miscellaneous classical constructions — trigonometric proofs, inscribed squares, four-center ovals, the Kepler golden right triangle (§ 181), Platonic solid circumradii (§ 182), and symmetric circle packings.
* **Book XII (§§ 193–280):** High-precision compass approximations — sexagesimal degrees/minutes, French 400-grad decimal division, circle rectification (:math:`\pi/2`), circle squaring (:math:`\sqrt{\pi}`), sphere cubature, and cube duplication (:math:`\sqrt[3]{2}` to :math:`\sqrt[3]{8}`).
