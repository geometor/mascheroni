=========================================
Mascheroni's Preface: Purpose & Precision
=========================================

.. folio:: 0032
   :page: 3

In the first half of his *Prefazione* (pp. 3–6, folio 0032–folio 0035), :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>` explains why a geometry restricted strictly to the compass is not merely an abstract curiosity, but a practical instrument of superior precision for astronomers, navigators, and astronomical instrument makers (such as Ramsden, Bird, and Graham dividing mural quadrants and sextants).

Whereas straightedges warp, slide off-center, and require visual alignment along a pencil edge, two fine steel compass points locked at a fixed aperture transfer chords around a brass limb with far smaller mechanical error. Mascheroni therefore asks whether every problem of elementary geometry—defining points, dividing arcs, bisecting segments, constructing proportions, square roots, and intersections—can be accomplished using circular arcs alone.
