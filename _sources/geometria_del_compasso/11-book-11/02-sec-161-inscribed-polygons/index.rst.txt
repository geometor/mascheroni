§§ 161–169: Inscribed Squares, Octagons & Area Scaling

**161.** *Problem. In a given equilateral triangle* :math:`ABC` *(fig. 79), to inscribe a square* :math:`becd` *(§ 124).*

*Solution I.* If we wish to make use of the given sides of the triangle, with center :math:`A` and radius :math:`AB`, describe the semicircle :math:`BCDE` (§ 64).

With center :math:`E` and radius :math:`EC`, describe an arc cutting the given side :math:`AB` at :math:`e`. With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`Be`, describe an arc cutting the same side at :math:`b`. With centers :math:`b` and :math:`e` and radius :math:`eb`, describe two arcs cutting sides :math:`AC` at :math:`d` and :math:`CB` at :math:`c`. :math:`becd` will be the inscribed square.

.. picture:: 0115.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 79 (folio 0115, p. 90): Inscribing a square inside an equilateral triangle (§ 161)

*Demonstration.* Putting for brevity :math:`AB = 1`, we shall have :math:`Ee = EC = BD = \sqrt{3}` (§ 2); hence :math:`Be = BE - Ee = 2 - \sqrt{3} = Ab`; hence :math:`be = AB - 2Ab = 2\sqrt{3} - 3 = (2 - \sqrt{3})\sqrt{3} = ec`. Therefore :math:`Be : ec :: (2 - \sqrt{3}) : (2 - \sqrt{3})\sqrt{3} :: 1 : \sqrt{3}`.

But if :math:`AB` be supposed bisected at :math:`T` by the perpendicular :math:`CT`, we shall also have :math:`BT : TC :: \frac{1}{2} : \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}` (§ 104); that is, :math:`BT : TC :: 1 : \sqrt{3}`.

.. folio:: 0116
   :page: 91

Therefore :math:`ec` is parallel to :math:`TC` (2, Book VI), and hence perpendicular to :math:`AB` (27, Book I). The same will be demonstrated for :math:`db`. Hence :math:`becd` is the inscribed square.

*Solution II.* If, however, we do not wish to make use of the intersection of the given sides of triangle :math:`ABC`, but only the three extreme points :math:`A`, :math:`B`, and :math:`C` of the triangle are given and the four points :math:`b`, :math:`e`, :math:`c`, :math:`d` of the square to be inscribed must be found:

With center :math:`E`, as before, and radius :math:`EC`, mark an arc passing through :math:`e`, :math:`C`, and :math:`a`. With the same radius and center :math:`B`, mark an arc cutting the preceding one at :math:`a`. With radius :math:`AB` and center :math:`a`, cut semicircle :math:`BCDE` at :math:`H`. Make on it :math:`Ha = HI = IK`.

With radius :math:`aK` and center :math:`D`, describe an arc passing through :math:`e`. Point :math:`e` will be determined.

With radius :math:`Be` and center :math:`A`, draw an arc through :math:`b`. With radius :math:`Ce` and center :math:`C`, draw another arc cutting the last at :math:`b`. With radius :math:`be` and centers :math:`b` and :math:`C`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`d`. With the same radius :math:`be` and centers :math:`e` and :math:`C`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`c`. :math:`becd` will be the square sought.

Or, having completed circle :math:`BCDE\delta` and made :math:`AB = E\delta`, with radius :math:`aK` determined as above and centers :math:`D` and :math:`\delta`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`e`. Let the rest be done as above.

*Demonstration.* Point :math:`K` will here have been determined as in figure 9 (§ 32) by means of the same point :math:`a`, so that arc :math:`BK` will be a twenty-fourth part of the circumference. Now in figure 9, since :math:`BK = Bk = EM`, if points :math:`a`, :math:`K`, :math:`B`, :math:`k`, :math:`A`, :math:`M` be compared with points :math:`Q`, :math:`A`, :math:`R`, :math:`S`, :math:`p`, :math:`B` of figure 4,
from equation :math:`(AQ)^2 = (RQ)^2 - AS \cdot pQ` (§ 20) there will result for figure 9 the equation :math:`(aK)^2 = (Ba)^2 - Kk \cdot Aa`. Now :math:`Kk` is the chord of a twelfth part of the circumference. To find its value, supposing for brevity :math:`AB = 1`, we have the sine of :math:`BN = Kk = AX` (fig. 12) :math:`= \frac{1}{2}`, and twice its cosine, namely :math:`2NX = NO = BD = \sqrt{3}`:
hence if in the equation (§ 160) :math:`\mathrm{chord}\,x = \sqrt{2 - 2\cos x}` we substitute in place of :math:`x` the arc :math:`Kk`, and in place of :math:`2\cos x`, :math:`\sqrt{3}`, we shall have the line :math:`Kk = \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}`. Hence :math:`(aK)^2 = 3 - \left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\sqrt{2}` (§ 27) :math:`= 3 - (\sqrt{3} - 1) = 4 - \sqrt{3}`.

.. folio:: 0117
   :page: 92

Now if with centers :math:`D` and :math:`\delta` and radius :math:`aK` we mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`e`, point :math:`e` will lie on line :math:`BE` (§ 13), which will bisect :math:`D\delta` at point :math:`R` at right angles (§ 14), making :math:`RD = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}` (§ 104).
Therefore since :math:`(De)^2 = (DR)^2 + (eR)^2` (47, Book I), we shall have :math:`(eR)^2 = (De)^2 - (DR)^2 = 4 - \sqrt{3} - \frac{3}{4} = \frac{13 - 4\sqrt{3}}{4}`, and hence :math:`eR = \sqrt{3} - \frac{1}{2}` and :math:`eE = \sqrt{3}`. Therefore, by the demonstration of Solution I, point :math:`e` is one of the vertices of the square.
It will likewise be the same if it is found with centers :math:`D` and :math:`E` and the two radii :math:`aK` and :math:`BD`, as in the first part of this Solution II. Moreover, since the two triangles :math:`CAb`, :math:`CBe` have their sides equal to each other, angle :math:`CBe = CAb` (8, Book I) :math:`= CBA`. Hence point :math:`b` will lie on :math:`BA`, and will be another vertex of the square.
Finally, since in this Solution lines :math:`Cb`, :math:`Ce` are the same in position and magnitude as in the first Solution, and since in the first Solution :math:`Cd = bd`, :math:`Cc = ec` by reason of equilateral triangle :math:`Ccd` and the parallelism of :math:`cd` to :math:`BA` (2, Book VI), points :math:`d` and :math:`c` will also be determined in this Solution as in the first.

**162.** *Problem. In the square* :math:`ABLF`, *to inscribe an equilateral triangle (fig. 80) having one angle* :math:`B` *at an angle of the square.*

*Solution I.* With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`AB`, describe the semicircle :math:`BFE`, making :math:`FB = FE`; make also :math:`BE = BQ = EQ`.
If we wish to make use of the intersections of the given sides of the square, with center :math:`F` and radius :math:`FQ` describe an arc cutting two sides of the square at points :math:`M` and :math:`N`; points :math:`B`, :math:`M`, :math:`N` will be the vertices of the triangle sought.

.. folio:: 0118
   :page: 93

*Demonstration.* Since angle :math:`QAB` is right (§ 83), we shall have :math:`(BQ)^2 = (AB)^2 + (AQ)^2` (47, Book I),
and taking for brevity :math:`AB = 1`, we shall have :math:`4 = 1 + (AQ)^2`; hence :math:`AQ = \sqrt{3}`; :math:`FQ = AQ - AF = \sqrt{3} - 1 = FM = FN`. Therefore :math:`(FM)^2 = (FN)^2 = 4 - 2\sqrt{3}`, and thus :math:`(MN)^2 = (FM)^2 + (FN)^2 = 8 - 4\sqrt{3}`. Since moreover :math:`LM = LF - FM = 1 - (\sqrt{3} - 1) = 2 - \sqrt{3}`, we shall have :math:`(LM)^2 = 7 - 4\sqrt{3}`. Hence :math:`(BM)^2 = (BL)^2 + (LM)^2 = 8 - 4\sqrt{3} = (MN)^2`. Likewise :math:`(BN)^2 = (MN)^2` is shown. Therefore :math:`BM = MN = BN`.

*Solution II.* If only the four vertices :math:`A`, :math:`B`, :math:`L`, :math:`F` of the square be supposed given, having found point :math:`Q` as in Solution I, with center :math:`F` and radius :math:`FQ` describe the circumference :math:`QRSNM`, and having made :math:`FQ = QR = RS = SN`, with center :math:`B` and radius :math:`BN` cut this circumference at point :math:`M`. Points :math:`B`, :math:`N`, :math:`M` will be the vertices of the triangle sought.

*Demonstration.* Arc :math:`QRSN` is half the circumference (15, Book IV). Hence point :math:`N` lies on line :math:`QFA`, and is equally distant from :math:`F` as in Solution I; therefore it is the same. Point :math:`M` in Solution I is also found at the intersection of two arcs described with centers :math:`F` and :math:`B` and radii equal to :math:`FQ` and :math:`BN`, as in this one; hence it is the same. Hence, etc.

**163.** *Problem. In an equilateral triangle, of which the vertices* :math:`P`, :math:`Q`, :math:`R` *are given (fig. 81), to inscribe a regular hexagon.*

*Solution.* Divide distance :math:`QR` into three equal parts at points :math:`c` and :math:`d` (§ 68). With centers :math:`c` and :math:`d` and radius :math:`cd`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`A`. With the same radius and center :math:`A`, describe a circle, and make on its circumference :math:`dc = cB = BC = CD = DE`. Points :math:`B`, :math:`C`, :math:`D`, :math:`E`, :math:`d`, :math:`c` will be the vertices of the inscribed hexagon.

*Demonstration.* Triangle :math:`BcQ` has sides :math:`Bc`, :math:`Qc` equal to sides :math:`Ad`, :math:`cd` of triangle :math:`Adc`, and the contained angle equal by the parallelism of :math:`Bc`, :math:`Ad` (27, Book I). Therefore it is equal to it in every respect (4, Book I), and angle :math:`cQB = dcA = cQP`.

.. folio:: 0119
   :page: 94

Hence point :math:`B` lies on :math:`PQ`.
Likewise it is demonstrated that the other points :math:`C`, :math:`D`, :math:`E` lie on the sides of the proposed triangle. Hence, etc.

**164.** *Problem. In a given square* :math:`ABLF` *(fig. 82), to inscribe a regular octagon.*

*Solution I.* If we wish to make use of the intersection of the given sides of the square, with center :math:`A` and radius :math:`AB` describe the semicircle :math:`BCDE`, making :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE`.
Make :math:`BF = BQ`, :math:`EA = EQ`. With radius :math:`AQ` and center :math:`A`, cut two sides at :math:`b` and :math:`g`. With the same radius and centers :math:`B`, :math:`L`, and :math:`F`, cut the sides successively at points :math:`a`, :math:`d`, :math:`c`, :math:`f`, :math:`e`, :math:`h`. These will be the vertices of the octagon :math:`abcdefgh`.

*Solution II.* Having found point :math:`Q` as in Solution I, with radius :math:`AQ` and centers :math:`A` and :math:`B`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`O`. With the same radius and center :math:`A`, cut side :math:`AB` at :math:`b`. With center :math:`O` and radius :math:`Ob`, describe a circle cutting the sides of the square at the remaining points :math:`c`, :math:`d`, :math:`e`, :math:`f`, :math:`g`, :math:`h`, :math:`a`. These will be the vertices of the octagon.

*Solution III.* If the sides of the square were not given, but only the four vertices :math:`A`, :math:`B`, :math:`L`, :math:`F`, having found point :math:`Q` as in Solution I, make :math:`EC = EM`, :math:`BF = BM`. With radius :math:`AM` and center :math:`A`, describe an arc passing through :math:`e`, :math:`d`. With the same radius and center :math:`B`, describe an arc passing through :math:`f`, :math:`g`.
With the same radius and center :math:`L`, describe an arc passing through :math:`h`, :math:`a`. With the same radius and center :math:`F`, describe an arc passing through :math:`b`, :math:`c`. With radius :math:`AQ` and centers :math:`A`, :math:`B`, :math:`L`, :math:`F`, cut these arcs at points :math:`b`, :math:`g`, :math:`d`, :math:`a`, :math:`c`, :math:`f`, :math:`e`, :math:`h`. These will be the vertices of the octagon.

*Demonstration.* Supposing for brevity that :math:`AB = 1`, we shall have :math:`AM = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6}` (§ 104); :math:`AQ = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}`. Hence :math:`(Ad)^2 = (AM)^2 = \frac{6}{4} = 1 + \frac{1}{2} = (AB)^2 + (Bd)^2`, since :math:`(Bd)^2 = (AQ)^2 = \frac{1}{2}`. Therefore angle :math:`ABd` will be right (48, Book I), and hence point :math:`d` will lie on :math:`BL`.

.. folio:: 0120
   :page: 95

Likewise it will be shown that all the other points lie on the sides of the proposed square. We shall then have :math:`Ld = BL - Bd = 1 - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2} = Le`, and :math:`(de)^2 = (Ld)^2 + (Le)^2` (47, Book I) :math:`= 2(Ld)^2`, and hence :math:`de = Ld \times \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} - 1`. But we have :math:`cd = BL - Ld - Bc = BL - 2Ld = 1 - 2 + \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} - 1`.

Therefore :math:`cd = de`. Likewise it will be shown that all the sides of the octagon are equal to one another. Since moreover triangles :math:`Lde`, :math:`Bbc`, :math:`Aah`, :math:`Ffg` are equal to one another in every respect (4, Book I), their angles at points :math:`a`, :math:`b`, :math:`c`, :math:`d`, :math:`e`, :math:`f`, :math:`g`, :math:`h` will be equal; hence their supplements to two right angles, that is, the angles of the octagon, will also be equal (13, Book I).

   :alt: Archival Fig. 83 (folio 0120, p. 95): Master multi-circle figure for octagon doubling and tripling (§ 165)

**165.** *Problem. Given a regular octagon* :math:`ABhgfEGH` *(fig. 66), to find easily: 1st, the side of a regular octagon double in area; 2nd, the side of a triple octagon.*

*Solution.* With side :math:`AB` of the given octagon taken as radius, and center at :math:`F` and :math:`H`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`a`.

.. folio:: 0121
   :page: 96

There will be: 1st, :math:`af`, or :math:`aA`, the side of the double octagon; 2nd, :math:`aB`, or :math:`ag`, the side of the triple octagon.

*Demonstration.* If :math:`AB = 1` be supposed, we shall have :math:`aA = af = \sqrt{2}` (§ 139); :math:`aB = ag = \sqrt{3}` (§ 2).
But the areas of similar figures are in the duplicate ratio of homologous sides (20, Book VI). Hence :math:`aA` will be the side of a double octagon, and :math:`aB` of a triple one.

**166.** *Problem. In a circle of given radius* :math:`AB` *(fig. 83), to inscribe three circles touching it and touching one another.*

*Solution.* On the circumference of the given circle make :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE = Ed = dc`. With radius :math:`BD` and center :math:`B`, describe an arc passing through :math:`a`, :math:`p`, and :math:`\alpha`. With the same radius :math:`BD` and center :math:`E`, cut this arc at :math:`a` and :math:`\alpha`. With the same radius and centers :math:`C`, :math:`c`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`V`; and with centers :math:`D` and :math:`d`, two other arcs intersecting at :math:`v`.
With the same radius and centers :math:`V`, :math:`v`, describe two arcs passing through :math:`m` and :math:`n`. With radius :math:`AB` and centers :math:`a` and :math:`\alpha`, cut the circumference of the given circle at :math:`G`, :math:`H`, and at :math:`g`, :math:`h`. On that circumference, with the same radius, make :math:`AB = GL = HI = gl = hi`. Make :math:`Aa = BF`, :math:`IL = LY = IY = ly = iy`, :math:`Yy = Fm = Fn`, :math:`Dn = Dp`.
With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`mn`, describe circle :math:`PSRXQT`, and taking an arbitrary point :math:`P` on its circumference, make :math:`PA = PS = SR = RX = XQ = QT`. Finally, with centers :math:`P`, :math:`Q`, :math:`R` and radius :math:`pn`, describe three circles. These will be the ones sought.

*Demonstration.* Since :math:`IL` is the chord of a twelfth part of the circumference (§ 32), we shall have :math:`IL = \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}` (§ 160). And since the square of the diameter is :math:`(Li)^2 = (IL)^2 + (Ii)^2` (47, Book I; 31, Book III), we shall have :math:`4 = 2 - \sqrt{3} + (Ii)^2`; hence :math:`(Ii)^2 = 2 + \sqrt{3}` and :math:`Ii = \sqrt{2 + \sqrt{3}}`.
We shall then have (§ 20) :math:`(aI)^2 = (aB)^2 - Ii \cdot Aa = 3 - \sqrt{4 + 2\sqrt{3}} = 3 - (1 + \sqrt{3}) = 2 - \sqrt{3}`. Hence :math:`aI = IL = \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}`. By the same reasoning :math:`aL = LI`; hence :math:`aIYL` will be a rhombus, and we shall have :math:`(aY)^2 = 4(aI)^2 - (IL)^2 = 3(IL)^2` (§ 139). Hence :math:`aY = IL \cdot \sqrt{3}`.
But since points :math:`I` and :math:`L` are equally distant from :math:`A`, the three points :math:`a`, :math:`Y`, :math:`A` will lie on the same straight line (§ 13). Hence :math:`AY = Aa - aY = \sqrt{2} - \sqrt{6 - 3\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{2} - \left(3\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}} - \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = IL`.

.. folio:: 0122
   :page: 97

The same will be shown for :math:`Ay`.
Moreover, since point :math:`y` lies on :math:`a\alpha`, that is, on :math:`aA` (§ 13), we shall have :math:`Yy = 2AY`. Now points :math:`V`, :math:`B`, :math:`A`, :math:`E`, :math:`v` lie on the same straight line (§ 13). If it be supposed for a moment that points :math:`m`, :math:`n` lie on the same straight line, we shall have :math:`Am = AV - Vm = 2 - \sqrt{3}`.
For if points :math:`C`, :math:`c`, :math:`V`, :math:`B`, :math:`A`, :math:`E` be compared with points :math:`A`, :math:`B`, :math:`Q`, :math:`P`, :math:`p`, :math:`q` of Fig. 3, we shall have in this Figure 83, :math:`VB = AE` (§ 14), and hence :math:`AV = 2`. Moreover :math:`Vm = \sqrt{3}`. Likewise :math:`An = 2 - \sqrt{3}` will be demonstrated. Hence :math:`(Fm)^2 = (Am)^2 + (AF)^2` (47, Book I) :math:`= 7 - 4\sqrt{3} + 1 = 4(2 - \sqrt{3})`.
Hence :math:`Fm = 2\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}} = 2AY`. Now in the construction of the Figure we took :math:`Fm = 2\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{3}}`. Therefore point :math:`m` will lie on line :math:`VA`. The same is demonstrated of point :math:`n`. Therefore, since :math:`Am = 2 - \sqrt{3}`, we shall have :math:`mn = 4 - 2\sqrt{3}`.
Moreover, since triangles :math:`BpD`, :math:`vnD` have their sides equal to each other, angle :math:`Dvn = DBp` (8, Book I). But since angle :math:`Dvn`, which is the same as angle :math:`DvB`, equals angle :math:`DBV` (5, Book I), we shall have angle :math:`DBp = DBv`. Therefore point :math:`p` lies on line :math:`AE`.
Further we have :math:`Dn = Dp`, and likewise :math:`dn = dp` is demonstrated. Therefore, comparing points :math:`D`, :math:`d`, :math:`A`, :math:`n`, :math:`p`, :math:`E` of this Figure with points :math:`A`, :math:`B`, :math:`Q`, :math:`P`, :math:`p`, :math:`q` of Figure 3, we shall find in this Fig. 83 that :math:`pE = An = 2 - \sqrt{3}`. Hence :math:`pn = AE - 2An = 1 - 4 + 2\sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3} - 3`.
Moreover, since equilateral triangle :math:`PQR` is similar to equilateral triangle :math:`BDd`, and hence triangle :math:`ABD` is also similar to triangle :math:`APR` (20, Book VI), we shall have :math:`AB : BD :: AP : PR`. That is, :math:`1 : \sqrt{3} :: 4 - 2\sqrt{3} : PR`. Hence :math:`PR = 4\sqrt{3} - 6 = 2pn`.
Therefore, :math:`PR` being bisected at :math:`p`, point :math:`p` will lie equally on the two circumferences of the circles described with centers :math:`P` and :math:`R`, and :math:`p` will be the point of contact (12, Book III). Now add to line :math:`AR = mn = 4 - 2\sqrt{3}` the radius of the circle described with center :math:`R`, namely line :math:`Rr = np = 2\sqrt{3} - 3`;
we shall have :math:`Ar = 4 - 3 = 1 = AB`. Therefore point :math:`r` will lie equally on both circumferences, and the circle described with center :math:`R` will touch the given circle internally (11, Book III). The same will be shown of the others. Hence, etc.

**168.** *Problem. With center* :math:`A` *(fig. 83), to describe a circle touching externally the three circles inscribed by the preceding Problem (§ 166) in a given circle.*

*Solution.* Seek a third proportional to the two lines :math:`AB`, :math:`Am` (§ 86). With this taken as radius and center :math:`A`, describe a circle. It will be the one sought.

.. folio:: 0124
   :page: 99

*Demonstration.* Since :math:`AB = 1`, :math:`Am = 2 - \sqrt{3}`, the third proportional will be :math:`= 7 - 4\sqrt{3}`.
Now if from radius :math:`Ar = 1` we subtract the diameter :math:`qr` of the circle described with center :math:`R`, that is, if we subtract :math:`2np = 4\sqrt{3} - 6`, we shall obtain precisely :math:`7 - 4\sqrt{3}`. Therefore a circle described with center :math:`A` and that third proportional will touch this inscribed circle at point :math:`q` (12, Book III), and will likewise touch the other two circles.

**169.** *Problem. In a circle of given radius* :math:`AB` *(fig. 84), to inscribe four circles tangent to it and to one another.*

*Solution.* On the circumference of the given circle make :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE`. Then :math:`BD = Ba = Ea`; then :math:`Aa = BF = Bf`; then :math:`AB = FN = FO`; then :math:`BD = NP = OP`.
With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`aP`, describe the circle :math:`QRST`. Having taken an arbitrary point :math:`Q` on its circumference, make on it :math:`BQ = FR = ES = fT`. With centers :math:`Q`, :math:`R`, :math:`S`, :math:`T` and radius :math:`aF`, describe four circles. These will be the ones sought.

*Demonstration.* Since :math:`BF = FE = Ef = fB` (§ 27), we shall also have :math:`QR = RS = ST = TQ` (§ 93). Hence angle :math:`TAQ` will be right. Therefore :math:`(TQ)^2 = (AT)^2 + (AQ)^2 = 2(AT)^2`. Then taking :math:`AB = 1`, we shall also have :math:`FP = 1` (§ 166); hence :math:`AP = 2`. Also :math:`Aa = \sqrt{2}` (§ 27); hence :math:`aP = 2 - \sqrt{2}`; :math:`aF = \sqrt{2} - 1`.
Hence :math:`2(AT)^2 = 2(aP)^2 = (TQ)^2 = 2(2 - \sqrt{2})^2`, and :math:`TQ = (2 - \sqrt{2})\sqrt{2} = 2\sqrt{2} - 2 = 2(\sqrt{2} - 1) = 2aF`.

Therefore the distance between the two centers :math:`T` and :math:`Q` equals the sum of the radii of the two circles described with these centers. Hence they touch each other at the midpoint of :math:`TQ` at :math:`p`.
The same will be proved of any other two. Then let :math:`r` be the point where :math:`AR` produced cuts the circle described with center :math:`R`; we shall have :math:`Ar = AR + Rr = aP + aF = PF = 1 = AB`. Therefore point :math:`r` will lie on the circumference of the given circle.
Hence :math:`Ar`, passing through the two centers :math:`A` and :math:`R`, will be perpendicular to the tangent of both at point :math:`r`; hence they will touch each other (13 and 16, Book III). The same is demonstrated of the others.
