====================================================
§§ 186–192: Five-Square Cross & Seven-Hexagon Flower
====================================================

.. folio:: 0138
   :page: 113

**§ 186 (Fig. 96, folio 0138, p. 113).** In a circle of radius :math:`AB`, inscribe five equal squares forming a symmetric Greek cross (one concentric, four sharing its sides and touching the circumference). Since the outer vertices sit at :math:`(\pm\frac{1}{2}s, \pm\frac{3}{2}s)`, each square has exact side :math:`s = \frac{2}{\sqrt{10}}\,AB = \frac{\sqrt{10}}{5}\,AB`.

**§§ 187–192 (Fig. 100, folio 0139–folio 0144, pp. 114–119).** Concluding Book XI with geometric packings and rose tessellations—culminating in **§ 191 (Fig. 100, folio 0143)**: inscribing a **flower of seven equal regular hexagons** (one central, six surrounding) inside a given circle by compass alone.
