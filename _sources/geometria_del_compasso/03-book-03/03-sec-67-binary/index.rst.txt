=================================
§ 67: Repeated Binary Subdivision
=================================

.. folio:: 0059
   :page: 34

**67.** *Prob.* To continue subdividing into two equal parts by the simplest construction (fig. 18), :math:`AM` at :math:`N`, :math:`AN` at :math:`O`, etc., to infinity.

*Sol. I.* Having described the semicircumference :math:`BCDE` with radius :math:`AB` (§ 64), and with the same radius and center :math:`B` the indefinite arc :math:`P'CAp'`; with centers :math:`E` and :math:`B` and radius :math:`BE` the two arcs :math:`R'Q'P'Bp'q'r'`, :math:`PQRErqp`; with center :math:`E` and radius :math:`EC` the arc :math:`PCp`;
if with centers :math:`P'` and :math:`p'` and radius :math:`AB` two arcs be described, they will intersect at :math:`M` at the midpoint of :math:`AB` (Sol. V, § 66). If with the two centers :math:`P` and :math:`p` and radius :math:`PE` two arcs be described, they will also intersect at the same point :math:`M` (Sol. III, § 66).

Now make :math:`AP' = BQ' = Bq' = q'N = Q'N` (§ 11). Point :math:`N` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AM`.

Make :math:`AQ' = BR' = Br' = r'O = R'O`. Point :math:`O` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AN`.

Continuing by the same method, :math:`AO` would be divided into two equal parts, etc., to infinity.

*Dem.* Imagine a straight line :math:`P'A`, which bisects the base :math:`BE` of triangle :math:`P'BE` (§ 12). We shall have :math:`(BP')^2 + (P'E)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP')^2` (§ 26). Hence substituting the values of :math:`BP' = AB` and :math:`P'E = 2\,AB`, and subtracting :math:`2(AB)^2`, we obtain :math:`3(AB)^2 = 2(AP')^2`, and dividing by :math:`2`, :math:`(AP')^2 = (BQ')^2 = \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2`.
Then since :math:`N` lies on straight line :math:`BE` (§ 13), the isosceles triangle :math:`Q'BN` will be similar to the isosceles triangle :math:`Q'BE` by the common angle at :math:`B` (I.5 and 32; VI.4). Hence :math:`(BQ')^2 = BN \cdot BE` (VI.17). Comparing the two values of :math:`(BQ')^2`, we have :math:`\frac{3}{2}(AB)^2 = BN \cdot BE = 2\,BN \cdot AB`,

.. folio:: 0060
   :page: 35

and dividing by :math:`2\,AB`, we obtain :math:`\frac{3}{4}AB = BN`. Therefore :math:`AN = \frac{1}{4}AB`.

Likewise we shall have :math:`(BQ')^2 + (Q'E)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ')^2` (§ 26), hence :math:`\frac{3}{2}(AB)^2 + 4(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ')^2`, and reducing, :math:`\frac{7}{4}(AB)^2 = (AQ')^2 = (BR')^2`. But :math:`(BR')^2 = BO \cdot BE = 2\,AB \cdot BO`. Therefore :math:`\frac{7}{4}(AB)^2 = 2\,AB \cdot BO`, and hence :math:`\frac{7}{8}AB = BO`; and :math:`AO = \frac{1}{8}AB`; etc.

*Sol. II.* Make :math:`AP = EQ = Eq = qN = QN`. Point :math:`N` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AM`. Make :math:`AQ = ER = Er = rO = RO`. Point :math:`O` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AN`. Continuing by the same method, :math:`AO` would be bisected, and so on to infinity.

*Dem.* Since we have (§ 26) :math:`(PE)^2 + (PB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2`, and substituting the values :math:`(PE)^2 = (CE)^2 = 3(AB)^2` (XIII.12) (§ 2), and :math:`(PB)^2 = (BE)^2 = 4(AB)^2`, we get :math:`7(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2`. Hence taking away :math:`2(AB)^2` and dividing by :math:`2`, we obtain :math:`\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 = (AP)^2 = (EQ)^2`.
But :math:`(EQ)^2 = EN \cdot EB` because of the similar isosceles triangles :math:`EQN, EQB` (§ 13) (I.5 and 32; VI.4 and 17). Therefore :math:`\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 = EN \cdot EB = 2\,EN \cdot AB`; and dividing by :math:`2\,AB` gives :math:`\frac{5}{4}AB = EN`; and :math:`AN = \frac{1}{4}AB`.
Reasoning by the same method, we shall have :math:`(QE)^2 + (QB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ)^2`. Hence :math:`\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 + 4(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ)^2`. Hence also :math:`\frac{9}{4}(AB)^2 = (AQ)^2 = (ER)^2 = EO \cdot EB = 2\,AB \cdot EO`. Therefore dividing by :math:`2\,AB`, we obtain :math:`\frac{9}{8}AB = EO`; and :math:`AO = \frac{1}{8}AB`; etc.

*Sol. III.* With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`AB` having described the semicircumference :math:`BCDE` (§ 64) (fig. 19), with the same radius :math:`AB` and centers :math:`B` and :math:`E` having described the indefinite arcs :math:`CP, Dp`, and with the same centers :math:`B` and :math:`E` and radius :math:`BE` the arcs :math:`Epqr, BPQR`, point :math:`M` can be found by making :math:`PM = PB` and :math:`EM = Pp` (Sol. II, § 66).

Now make :math:`AP = BQ = QN = Eq`. Make also :math:`Qq = EN`. Point :math:`N` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AM`. Likewise make :math:`AQ = BR = RO = Er`. Make also :math:`Rr = EO`. Point :math:`O` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AN`. Etc.

*Dem.* Making the due substitutions in Fig. 5 (§ 23),

.. folio:: 0061
   :page: 36

we shall have :math:`Qq \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - (BQ)^2`. But :math:`BE = 2\,AB`; and :math:`(BQ)^2 = \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2` (see the demonstration of Sol. I). Therefore :math:`2\,Qq \cdot AB = 4(AB)^2 - \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2`, and dividing by :math:`2\,AB` and reducing, we have :math:`Qq = \frac{5}{4}AB`.
Therefore also :math:`EN = \frac{5}{4}AB`. Hence, :math:`AB` being the same in both Figures 18 and 19, the sides of the two triangles :math:`Q'NE` (Fig. 18) and :math:`QNE` (Fig. 19) will also be the same. Thus superposing the three points :math:`B, Q, E` of Fig. 19 upon the three :math:`B, Q', E` of 18, the points :math:`N` of the two Figures will also coincide. Therefore etc.

Likewise making the due substitutions in Fig. 5 (§ 23), we have :math:`Rr \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - (BR)^2`. But :math:`(BR)^2 = \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2` (Dem. of Sol. I); therefore :math:`Rr \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2`. Or substituting :math:`2\,AB` for :math:`BE`: :math:`2\,AB \cdot Rr = 4(AB)^2 - \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2`. Dividing by :math:`2\,AB` and reducing gives :math:`Rr = \frac{9}{8}AB = OE`.
Therefore, the points :math:`E, R, B` of this Fig. 19 coinciding with the points :math:`E, R', B` of Fig. 18, and here :math:`RO` and :math:`EO` being respectively equal to :math:`R'O, EO` of Fig. 18, point :math:`O` will also coincide. Hence :math:`O` will be at the midpoint of :math:`AN`. In the same manner it would be demonstrated thereafter to infinity.

Other ways of finding the same points could be used; but we shall pass to other divisions of :math:`AB` into a different number of parts.
