=============================================
§§ 71–75: Addition & Subtraction of Distances
=============================================

.. folio:: 0065
   :page: 40

**BOOK FOUR**

**OF THE ADDITION AND SUBTRACTION OF DISTANCES; OF THE POSITION OF PERPENDICULARS AND PARALLELS**

**71.** Adding or subtracting a distance from another given distance—which is so simple and easy to do with compass and straightedge by drawing an indefinite straight line through the two endpoints
of the first distance and adding or subtracting the second distance upon it with the compass (I.3)—is certainly not so ready and expeditious to perform with the compass alone; nor are these Problems proposed here as of great use,
but only that it may be seen that there can be no Problem of Elementary Geometry which cannot also be solved with the compass alone in the sense explained (§ I)—which will be demonstrated more rigorously in its place—and so that none of the Elements of this new Geometry may be wanting, according to our promise (§ 7).

**72.** *Prob.* From the distance :math:`AB` to cut off a distance equal to :math:`CD` (fig. 24).

.. picture:: 0065.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 24 (folio 0065, p. 40): Collinear addition and subtraction of distances AB ± CD

*Sol.* With radius :math:`CD` and center :math:`B` (if the distance is to be cut off on that side), describe a circle :math:`FGEH`. With center :math:`A` and any radius describe an arc cutting the circle at :math:`E` and :math:`F`. Bisect the arc :math:`EGF` (§ 60) at :math:`G`. Point :math:`G` will lie on the straight line :math:`BA`, and :math:`GA` will be the remainder.

*Dem.* Since the two triangles :math:`EBG, FBG` have equal sides,

.. folio:: 0066
   :page: 41

angle :math:`EBG = FBG` (I.8). Therefore angle :math:`EBG` will be equal to half the angle :math:`EBF`. Since the two triangles :math:`EBA, FBA` also have their sides mutually equal, it will be proved in the same way that angle :math:`EBA` is also half the angle :math:`EBF`.
Therefore angle :math:`EBG` is equal to :math:`EBA`. Hence point :math:`G` lies on :math:`BA`. But :math:`BG` is also equal to :math:`CD`. Therefore :math:`GA` will be the remainder of :math:`AB` after cutting off :math:`CD`.

**73.** *Prob.* To the distance :math:`AB` to add the distance :math:`CD` in a straight line (fig. 24).

*Sol.* With center :math:`B` (if the addition is to be made on that side) and radius :math:`CD`, describe the circle :math:`FGEH`. With center :math:`A` and any radius describe an arc cutting the circle at :math:`E` and :math:`F`. Bisect arc :math:`EHF` at :math:`H` (§ 60). Point :math:`H` will lie on the straight line :math:`AB`, and :math:`AH` will be the sum of the two distances :math:`AB, CD`.

*Dem.* By the equality of the sides among themselves in the two triangles :math:`EBH, FBH`, as also in the two triangles :math:`EBA, FBA`, we shall find (I.8) angle :math:`EBH = FBH`, and angle :math:`EBA = FBA`. Hence :math:`EBH + EBA = FBH + FBA`.
But the sum of all these four angles is equal to four right angles (I.13). Therefore their half, for example :math:`EBH + EBA`, will be equal to two right angles, and hence :math:`HBA` will be a straight line (I.14). Moreover :math:`BH = CD`. Therefore :math:`AH = AB + CD`.

**74.** *Prob.* Upon :math:`AB` from :math:`A` toward :math:`B` to lay off :math:`CD`, greater than :math:`AB` (fig. 25).

.. picture:: 0066.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 25 (folio 0066, p. 41): Laying off a larger distance CD along ray AB

*Sol.* With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`CD`, describe an indefinite arc :math:`LMN`, or an entire circle. With center :math:`B` and an arbitrary radius describe an arc cutting the first at the two points :math:`L` and :math:`N`. Bisect the arc :math:`LN` (§ 60) at :math:`M`. Then :math:`AM` will be the same :math:`CD` placed as desired.

*Dem.* By the method of the demonstrations of the two preceding Problems (§§ 72 and 73), it will be found that since angle :math:`LMA = NMA` (I.8) :math:`= \frac{1}{2} LMN`, and likewise :math:`LMB = NMB = \frac{1}{2} LMN`,

.. folio:: 0067
   :page: 42

:math:`LMA = LMB`, and hence :math:`ABM` is a straight line. Moreover it is equal to :math:`CD`. Therefore, etc.

**75.** *Obs.* If the arc described with center :math:`B` should cut the arc described with center :math:`A` at angles too acute—which happens when :math:`AB` is too small in comparison with :math:`CD`—to :math:`BA` add the equal :math:`AE` in a straight line, and cut the arc :math:`LMN` described with center :math:`A` by an arc described with center :math:`E` at :math:`L` and :math:`N`.
If in that case also the angles of the two arcs turn out too acute, triple, quadruple, etc. (§ 65) the straight line :math:`AB` from :math:`A` toward :math:`B`, until its last point taken as center of the second arc gives the angles of intersection at :math:`L` and :math:`N` nearer to a right angle. Do the same in like cases for §§ 72 and 73.
