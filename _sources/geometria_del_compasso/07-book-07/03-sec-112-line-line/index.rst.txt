=============================================================
§ 112: Intersection of Two Virtual Lines & Circular Inversion
=============================================================

.. folio:: 0086
   :page: 61

**§ 112. Problem (Figs. 49–50, folio 0086–folio 0088, pp. 61–63).** Given two :term:`virtual lines <retta-virtuale>` :math:`(A, B)` and :math:`(C, D)`, construct their exact intersection point :math:`S` by compass alone.

*Mascheroni's Construction:* Reflect :math:`C \to c` and :math:`D \to d` across :math:`(A, B)` (§ 78). Complete parallelogram :math:`cDdE` (§ 80) so that :math:`(E, c) \parallel (D, d)` and :math:`C, c, E` form a triangle similar to :math:`\triangle SCc`. Constructing the fourth proportional :math:`cE : cD :: CC' : CS` (§ 93) determines the exact radius :math:`CS`, and intersecting :math:`C(CS) \cap c(CS)` yields :math:`S`!

Modern Unification via Circular Inversion (:person:`August Adler <adler>`, 1890): In :cite:`adler-1890`, Adler proved that a single :term:`circular inversion <inversione>` across reference circle :g:`( O R )`—executed in just **3 circles** (:math:`5 C_1 + 3 C_3`, :math:`S = 8`): :g:`( P O )` :math:`\cap` :g:`( O R )` :math:`= \{`:g:`T1`, :g:`T2`:math:`\}`, followed by :g:`( T1 O )` :math:`\cap` :g:`( T2 O )` :math:`=` :g:`P'` satisfying :math:`OP \cdot OP' = R^2`—maps every straight line not through :g:`O` onto a circle through :g:`O`, reducing both line–circle and line–line intersections directly to circle–circle intersections!
