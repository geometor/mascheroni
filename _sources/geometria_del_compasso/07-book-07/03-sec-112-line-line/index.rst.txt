=============================================================
§ 112: Intersection of Two Virtual Lines & Circular Inversion
=============================================================

.. folio:: 0086
   :page: 61

**112. Problem.** Given two points :math:`A, B` of one straight line (figs. 49 and 50), and two points :math:`C, D` of another; to find the point :math:`S` where they intersect.

*Solution.* With the two points of one of the two lines, for example with

.. folio:: 0087
   :page: 62

points :math:`A` and :math:`B` taken as centers, and with the respective distances of these points :math:`AC, AD; BC, BD` from the two points of the other line :math:`C, D` taken as radii, describe four arcs, two of which intersect at :math:`C` and :math:`c`, and two at :math:`D` and :math:`d`.

Find the fourth point :math:`\delta` of the parallelogram :math:`CDd\delta` by making :math:`Dd = C\delta` and :math:`DC = d\delta` (§ 11).

Find the fourth proportional to the three :math:`c\delta, CD, Cc`. With this taken as radius, and with centers :math:`C` and :math:`c`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`S`. Then :math:`S` will be the point of intersection of the two straight lines :math:`AB, CD`.

*Demonstration.* The straight lines :math:`AB, Cc` will be perpendicular to one another (§§ 13, 14); so likewise :math:`AS, Cc`.
Hence point :math:`S` will lie on :math:`AB` itself. Then since :math:`AB`, or :math:`AS`, is perpendicular also to :math:`Dd` (§§ 13, 14), :math:`Dd` will be parallel to :math:`Cc` (I.29). But by the equal sides in the two triangles :math:`dC\delta, dCD`, the angles :math:`dC\delta, CdD` are equal (I.8). Hence :math:`C\delta, Dd` are also parallel (I.28). Therefore the points :math:`c, C, \delta` lie on the same straight line.
Now by reason of the two equal sides in the two triangles :math:`CBA, cBA`, the angles :math:`CBA, cBA` will be equal (I.8). For the same reason in triangles :math:`ABD, ABd` the angles :math:`ABD, ABd` are found equal. Therefore in Figure 49 the angle :math:`cBd`, which is the sum of the two :math:`cBA, ABd`, will equal the angle :math:`CBD`, the sum of the two :math:`CBA, ABD`.
Then in Figure 50 the angle :math:`cBd`, which is the difference of the two :math:`ABd, cBA`, will likewise equal the angle :math:`CBD`, which is the difference of the two :math:`ABD, CBA`. Thus in both Figures, in the two triangles :math:`cBd, CBD`, which have two
