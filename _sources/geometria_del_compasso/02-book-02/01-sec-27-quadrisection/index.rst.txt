============================================
§§ 27–29: Quadrisection of the Circumference
============================================

.. folio:: 0048
   :page: 19

**BOOK TWO**

**ON THE DIVISION OF THE CIRCUMFERENCE AND OF ARCS OF THE CIRCLE.**

**27.** *Prob.* To divide the circumference of the circle :g:`( A B )` (:math:`BDd`) into four equal parts (fig. 9).

.. picture:: 0048.g1.svg
   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Book II § 27 (Fig. 9): compass-only circle quadrisection BFEf

.. picture:: 0048.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 9 (folio 0048, p. 19): Quadrisection and multi-division of the circumference

*Sol.* On the same circumference, with radius :math:`AB = Bc = BC = CD = DE = Ed` using the first compass (§ 10, 8), we shall have :math:`dc =`

.. folio:: 0049
   :page: 20

:math:`cB = BA` (IV.15). Make :g:`/ B D /` :math:`= Ba = Ea` with the 2nd compass; and :g:`/ A a /` :math:`= BF = Bf` with the 3rd compass. The circumference will be divided into four equal parts :g:`/ B F /`, :g:`/ F E /`, :math:`Ff`, :g:`/ f B /`.

*Dem.* Since :g:`/ B E /` (:math:`BAE`) is a diameter (IV.15), and triangles :math:`aAB`, :math:`aAE` have all sides equal, their angles :math:`aAB`, :math:`aAE` are equal (I.8) and therefore right angles (I.13). Hence :math:`(aB)^2 = (AB)^2 + (aA)^2` (I.47); subtracting :math:`(AB)^2` from both sides gives :math:`(aB)^2 - (AB)^2 = (aA)^2`.
Let :math:`AB = 1` for brevity; then :math:`(aB)^2 = (BD)^2 = 3` (§ 2). Hence :math:`(aA)^2 = 3 - 1 = 2`, and therefore :math:`(BF)^2 = (aA)^2 = 2 = 1 + 1 = (AB)^2 + (AF)^2`. Thus in triangle :math:`FAB`, angle :math:`FAB` is right (I.48), and so also is angle :math:`FAE` (I.13).
Therefore arcs :g:`/ B F /`, :g:`/ F E /` are equal to each other, and quarters of the circle, as also arcs :math:`Bf`, :math:`fE`.

**28.** *Cor.* Since angles :math:`BAa`, :math:`BAF` are right, the three points :g:`A`, :g:`F`, :g:`a` lie on the same straight line.

**29.** We therefore already have the circumference divided into two equal parts, for example at points :g:`B` and :g:`E`; into three parts, as at points :g:`B`, :g:`D`, :math:`d` (IV.15); into four parts at :g:`B`, :g:`F`, :g:`E`, :g:`f` (§ 27); into six parts at :g:`B`, :g:`C`, :g:`D`, :g:`E`, :math:`d`, :math:`c` (IV.15).
