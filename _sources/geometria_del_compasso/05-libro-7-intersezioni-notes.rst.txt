Commentary & Notes: Libro VII — August Adler and the Inversive Paradigm
=======================================================================

.. rubric:: How Circle Inversion Transformed Compass Completeness

1. From Mascheroni's Exhaustive Cases to Adler's Group Action
-------------------------------------------------------------

In 1797, :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>` had to divide Book VII into dozens of casework propositions: lines through the center, lines external to the circle, secant chords, parallel lines, intersecting lines at acute angles, and obtuse lines.

In 1890, the Czech-Austrian geometer :person:`August Adler <adler>` (:cite:`adler-1890`) recognized that circular inversion acts as a conformal group involution on the extended plane:

* Every straight line is simply a circle passing through the point at infinity :math:`\infty`.
* Inverting through a point :math:`O` maps :math:`\infty` to :math:`O`, thus mapping every straight line not through :math:`O` to a circle passing through :math:`O`.
* Consequently, intersecting straight lines is mathematically identical to intersecting circles!
* Adler's paper eliminated the need for casework, proving the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem in less than three pages.

2. Relationship to Hilbert's Field Criteria
-------------------------------------------

In *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (1899, Chapter VII, :cite:`hilbert-1899`), :person:`David Hilbert <hilbert>` showed that ruler-and-compass geometry constructs exactly those points whose coordinates lie in the field formed by successive square-root extensions of the rationals (:math:`\mathbb{Q} \subset K_1 \subset K_2 \dots`).

Because the intersection of two circles :math:`(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r_1^2` and :math:`(x - c)^2 + (y - d)^2 = r_2^2` reduces algebraically to a linear equation (radical axis) and a quadratic equation, circle–circle intersections solve all quadratic equations. Mascheroni's Book VII proves constructively that the compass alone accesses the full quadratic field closure.
