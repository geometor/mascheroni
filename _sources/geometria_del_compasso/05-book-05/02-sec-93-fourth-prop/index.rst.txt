========================================
§§ 93–95: Concentric Fourth Proportional
========================================

.. folio:: 0073
   :page: 48

**93.** To find the fourth proportional to the three distances :math:`PQ, RS, TV` (fig. 39).

*Sol.* With a common center :math:`O`, and with the first two distances taken as radii, describe two circles—namely, with radius :math:`PQ` the circle :math:`BC`, and with radius :math:`RS` the circle :math:`DE`. With the third distance :math:`TV` taken as radius, and centered at some point :math:`B` of the first circumference, mark an arc cutting it at :math:`C`.
With an arbitrary radius centered at :math:`B`, mark an arc cutting the second circumference at :math:`D`. With the same radius :math:`BD` centered at :math:`C`, cut the same circumference at a nearby point :math:`E`. Then :math:`DE` will be the fourth proportional.

*Dem.* Because the sides in the two triangles :math:`COE, BOD` are mutually equal, the angle :math:`COE = BOD` (I.8). And taking away from both (or adding) the angle :math:`BOE`, we shall have the angle :math:`COB = EOD`.
Therefore the sum of the angles :math:`OCB, OBC` will also equal the sum of the angles :math:`OED, ODE` (I.32). But the two triangles :math:`COB, EOD` are isosceles. Therefore the two half-sums—that is, the base angles—will be equal (I.5). Hence the triangles will be similar (VI.4), and :math:`CO : DO :: CB : DE`, i.e., :math:`PQ : RS :: TV : DE`.

**94.** *Obs. I.* It will be advantageous to take the arbitrary radius :math:`BD` so that the angle :math:`BDO` turns out close to a right angle (§ 9), which can be done by eye.

**95.** *Obs. II.* If the third distance :math:`TV` could not be laid off as a chord in :math:`BC`—which will happen when :math:`TV` is greater than twice :math:`PQ`—we must double the two distances :math:`PQ, RS` (§ 64), and with them thus doubled describe the two circles :math:`BC, DE`, and do all the rest as above (§ 93).
If not even this sufficed, we would triple them, etc. It will also be advantageous to double or triple them, etc., when :math:`TV` could indeed be applied as a chord to the first circle, yet were nearly equal to its diameter; and this to avoid intersections at acute angles, and obtain others closer to a right angle.

This is proved from :math:`PQ : RS :: 2PQ : 2RS :: 3PQ : 3RS`, etc. Hence, having made as :math:`2PQ` to :math:`2RS` (or as :math:`3PQ` to :math:`3RS`, etc.), so :math:`BC` to :math:`DE`, it will always be as :math:`PQ` to :math:`RS`, so :math:`BC` to :math:`DE`; that is, as :math:`PQ` to :math:`RS`, so :math:`TV` to :math:`DE` (V.4).
