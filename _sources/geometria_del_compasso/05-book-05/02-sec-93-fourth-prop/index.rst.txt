========================================
§§ 93–95: Concentric Fourth Proportional
========================================

.. folio:: 0073
   :page: 48

**§ 93. Problem (Fig. 39, folio 0073, p. 48).** Given three distances :math:`a, b, c`, find the **fourth proportional** :math:`x = bc / a` (:math:`a : b :: c : x`) by the compass alone.

*Construction:* Around a common center :g:`A`, draw two concentric circles :g:`( A B )` of radius :math:`a` and :g:`( A D )` of radius :math:`b`. In circle :g:`( A B )`, lay off chord :g:`/ B C /` :math:`= c`. Choose an arbitrary compass opening :math:`r` (large enough to bridge the two circles) and sweep equal arcs :g:`( B D )` and :g:`( C E )` from :g:`B` and :g:`C` cutting the outer circle at :g:`D` and :g:`E` (with :eq:`/ B D / = / C E /`).

*Proof:* Triangles :math:`\triangle ABD` and :math:`\triangle ACE` have all three sides equal (:math:`AB = AC = a`, :math:`AD = AE = b`, :math:`BD = CE = r`), so :math:`\angle BAD = \angle CAE`, hence :math:`\angle BAC = \angle DAE`. The isosceles triangles :math:`\triangle ABC` and :math:`\triangle ADE` are therefore similar, yielding :math:`AB : AD :: BC : DE`, i.e., :g:`/ D E /` :math:`= \frac{bc}{a}` in just **4 circles** (:math:`S = 14`)!

**§§ 94–95.** Handles the case :math:`c > 2a` by subdividing :math:`c` or scaling :math:`a, b` proportionally. :person:`Gaetano Fazzari <fazzari>` (1901, p. XI) highlighted § 93 as a premier example where :term:`Mascheroni's concentric fourth proportional <quarta-proporzionale>` defeats Euclid VI.12 under :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>`'s Geometrography.
