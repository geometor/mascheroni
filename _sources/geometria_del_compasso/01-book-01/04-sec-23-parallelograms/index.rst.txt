===============================================
§§ 23–26: Parallelograms & Apollonius's Theorem
===============================================

.. folio:: 0046
   :page: 17

**23.** *Lemma.* If :math:`AB = AC = BD`, and :math:`AD = BC`; then (fig. 5) :math:`DC \cdot AB = (AB)^2 - (AD)^2`.

*Dem.* The two triangles :math:`ADB`, :math:`ACB`, having their sides respectively equal, will be equal (I.8 and 26).
Being moreover both placed upon the same base :math:`AB`, they will lie between the same parallels :math:`DC`, :math:`AB` (I.39). If therefore upon :math:`BA` we take :math:`BE = DC`, :math:`DE` will be equal and parallel to :math:`BC` (I.33), and equal also to :math:`DA`.
Hence the two isosceles triangles :math:`BDA`, :math:`DAE`, which have a common angle at :math:`A`, will be similar (I.5 and 32, and VI.4), and :math:`AB : AD :: AD : AE`; therefore :math:`AB \cdot AE = (AD)^2` (VI.17). We have moreover :math:`AB \cdot AE + AB \cdot BE = (AB)^2` (II.2).
Hence substituting :math:`(AD)^2` for :math:`AB \cdot AE`, and :math:`DC` for :math:`BE`, we have :math:`(AD)^2 + AB \cdot DC = (AB)^2`. And subtracting :math:`(AD)^2`, we shall have :math:`DC \cdot AB = (AB)^2 - (AD)^2`.

**24.** *Lemma.* If in the circle :math:`B\mu G` with radius :math:`AB` we erect at the center :math:`A` the perpendicular :math:`Ae` equal to the chord :math:`BG` of the arc :math:`B\mu G`; and with center :math:`e` and radius :math:`AB` we describe an arc cutting the circumference at :math:`\mu`; the arc :math:`B\mu` will be equal to half of the arc :math:`B\mu G`.

.. picture:: 0046.g2.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 6 (folio 0046, p. 17): Halving a circular arc with compass alone

*Dem.* By the equality of the sides of the two triangles :math:`ABG`, :math:`A\mu e` (fig. 6), angle :math:`GAB = A\mu e` (I.8). Let angle :math:`A\mu e` be bisected by the straight line :math:`\mu M`; since triangle :math:`\mu eA` is isosceles, angle :math:`\mu eM = \mu AM`; hence in the two triangles :math:`\mu eM`, :math:`\mu AM`, the other two angles being equal to each other, :math:`\mu Me = \mu MA` also (I.32, Coroll.);
therefore :math:`\mu M` is perpendicular to :math:`Ae` (I.13), and hence parallel to :math:`AB` (I.29), and angle :math:`M\mu A = \mu AB` (I.27); therefore :math:`\mu AB` will be half of angle :math:`GAB`, and hence also arc :math:`B\mu` half of arc :math:`B\mu G`.

**25.** If in the parallelogram :math:`ABMN` the diagonal :math:`MA` be equal to the opposite sides :math:`MB`, :math:`AN`; the square on the other diagonal :math:`BN` will be equal to the square on the first increased by the two squares on the other two sides.

.. picture:: 0047.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 7 (folio 0047, p. 18): Parallelogram diagonal sum of squares

*Dem.* Let :math:`AB` be bisected at :math:`m` (fig. 7) by the perpendicular :math:`Mm` (I.10, 11), and upon :math:`BA` produced let :math:`An = Bm` be taken; then :math:`mn = BA = MN`; and hence :math:`MNnm` is a parallelogram (I.33), and angle :math:`NnB` will be right (I.27).
Therefore :math:`(BN)^2 = (AB)^2 + (AN)^2 + 2AB \cdot An` (II.12). But :math:`An = \frac{1}{2} AB`. Therefore :math:`(NB)^2 = (AN)^2 + 2(AB)^2 = (AN)^2 + (AB)^2 + (MN)^2`.

**26.** If in any triangle :math:`BPE` the base :math:`BE` be cut into two equal parts at :math:`A`, and from the opposite angle :math:`P` the straight line :math:`PA` be drawn (fig. 8); the sum of the squares on the sides :math:`BP` and :math:`PE` will be equal to the sum of the equal squares on the two segments, increased by twice the square on :math:`AP`.

.. picture:: 0047.g2.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 8 (folio 0047, p. 18): Apollonius median identity

*Dem.* For if the perpendicular :math:`PR` be dropped upon the base :math:`BE`, :math:`(BP)^2 = (BA)^2 + (AP)^2 + 2BA \cdot AR` (II.12). Likewise :math:`(PE)^2 = (AE)^2 + (AP)^2 - 2AE \cdot AR` (II.13).
Taking therefore the sum of the values of the two squares :math:`(BP)^2` and :math:`(PE)^2`, since :math:`BA = AE`, we shall have :math:`(BP)^2 + (PE)^2 = (BA)^2 + (AE)^2 + 2(AP)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2`.
