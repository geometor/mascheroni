============================================================
§§ 267–272: Circle Rectification, Radian Arc & Squaring (√π)
============================================================

.. folio:: 0168
   :page: 143

**267. Problem.** In a circle (Fig. 101) of given radius :math:`AB`, to find a chord :math:`Bb` approximately equal to one quarter of the circumference.

*Sol.* In the circumference make :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE`. Then make :math:`BD = Ba = Ea`. With center :math:`C` and radius :math:`Ca` mark an arc cutting the circumference at :math:`b`. The chord :math:`Bb` will be the chord sought.

*Demonstration.* Supposing :math:`AB = 1`, set :math:`BC = A = 60^\circ` in equation (1); there will result :math:`A' = 43^\circ 33' \frac{286}{2005} = Cb`. The arc :math:`BCb` will therefore be :math:`103^\circ 33' \frac{286}{2005}`. Its half :math:`51^\circ 46' \frac{1145}{1005}` has for sine :math:`0.7855998`. The chord :math:`Bb` will therefore be :math:`1.5711996`. Now a quarter of the circumference is :math:`= 1.5707963`. The error is therefore about :math:`0.0004`.

**268.** According to the proportion of Archimedes, setting the radius :math:`= 1`, one finds a quarter of the circumference :math:`= \frac{11}{7} = 1.5714`. Therefore the construction of this problem (§ 267) gives a closer approximation. Since this construction is very simple, it will be preferable to use in practice rather than others that we might add, which would indeed give a greater theoretical approximation, but would be more complicated, and hence more liable to error.

**269. Problem.** In a circle (Fig. 102) of given radius :math:`AB`, to find the arc approximately equal to the same radius.

*Sol.* In the circumference :math:`BLCMFDOEd` make :math:`AB = BC`

:math:`= CD = DE = Ed`. Then make :math:`BD = Ba = Ea`. Then :math:`Aa = BF = Db = db = aL`. Then :math:`AB = FO`. Then finally :math:`bF = OM`. The arc :math:`LM` will be approximately equal to the radius.

*Demonstration.* If in equation (4) we introduce :math:`A' = 90^\circ`, of which arc :math:`aL` is chord, there will result :math:`BL = A = 20^\circ 42' \frac{786}{1721}`. Then since :math:`OM = bF` is chord of the fifth part of the circumference (§ 40), that is, of :math:`72^\circ`, and since arc :math:`FO = 60^\circ`, arc :math:`FM` will be :math:`= 12^\circ`. Hence arc :math:`LM = BF - BL - FM = 57^\circ 17' \frac{1035}{2721} = 57^\circ 17' 43''+`. Now the arc equal to the radius is, as is known, :math:`= 57^\circ 17' 44''`. Therefore, etc.

**270. Problem.** To find (Fig. 103) the side of a square that may be approximately equal in area to a circle of given radius :math:`AB`.

*Sol.* In the circumference :math:`BPCQDRE` make :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE`, and with the same radius :math:`BA`, and centers :math:`B` and :math:`E`, describe the two arcs :math:`ALc`, :math:`AMd`. With centers :math:`C` and :math:`D`, and radius :math:`DB`, describe the arcs :math:`cNM`, :math:`dNL`. Make :math:`AN = BP = PQ`; then :math:`LM = QR`. :math:`BR` will be the side sought.

   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Mascheroni Book XII § 269 (Fig. 103): Circle Squaring & Quadrature Arcs

*Demonstration.* If in the isosceles triangle :math:`CND` the base :math:`CD = 1` is supposed bisected at :math:`\mu`, we shall have :math:`(CN)^2 = (C\mu)^2 + (N\mu)^2`, that is (§ 2), :math:`3 = \frac{1}{4} + (N\mu)^2`; hence :math:`N\mu = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11}`. Next we have :math:`(CA)^2 = (C\mu)^2 + (A\mu)^2`, and hence :math:`A\mu = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}`. Therefore :math:`AN = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{11} - \sqrt{3}) = 0.7922869`, which is found to be the chord of an arc :math:`= 46^\circ 40' \frac{1272}{2071}`

   :page: 145

:math:`= BP = PQ`. Therefore the arc :math:`BQ` will be :math:`= 93^\circ 20' \frac{2544}{2671}`. Let the straight lines :math:`BL` bisected at :math:`n`, :math:`BD`, :math:`Dn`, :math:`BE`, and :math:`D\delta`, :math:`Ll`, :math:`Mm` perpendicular to the same :math:`BE` at :math:`\delta`, :math:`l`, and :math:`m` be drawn.

Since the angle :math:`DBE = 30^\circ` (III.20), we shall have :math:`\sin DBE = \frac{1}{2}`; :math:`\cos DBE = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}`. Then by Trigonometry :math:`\cos DBn = \frac{Bn}{DB}`; :math:`\sin DBn = \frac{Dn}{DB}`; that is, :math:`\cos DBn = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}`; and since :math:`Dn = \sqrt{(DB)^2 - (Bn)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11}`, :math:`\sin DBn = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33}`. Then (§ 154) :math:`\cos LBl = \cos(DBn - DBE) = \cos DBn \cdot \cos DBE + \sin DBn \cdot \sin DBE = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} + \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{12}(3 + \sqrt{33}) = \frac{Bl}{BL} = Bl` (since :math:`BL = BA = 1`).
Therefore :math:`Al = AB - Bl = \frac{1}{12}(9 - \sqrt{33})`, and hence :math:`lm = LM = 2Al = \frac{1}{6}(9 - \sqrt{33}) = 0.5425729`, which is found to be the chord of the arc :math:`= 31^\circ 28' \frac{2518}{2799} = QR`. The arc :math:`BQR` will therefore be :math:`= 124^\circ 49' \frac{6370}{7476}`. Its half :math:`62^\circ 24' \frac{6923}{7476}` has for sine :math:`0.8863283`, and therefore the chord of :math:`BQR` will be :math:`= 1.7726566`. Now setting the radius :math:`= 1`, we have the area of the circle :math:`= 3.1415926`, which has for logarithm :math:`0.4971499`. Half of this logarithm, that is :math:`0.2485749 = \log \sqrt{3.1415926}`, is found to be :math:`\log 1.772453`. We have therefore an error of about :math:`0.0002`, which gives a sufficient approximation.

**271.** Instead of :math:`AN`, one could have used :math:`dL` or :math:`cM`, which must be found equal to the same :math:`AN`.

*Demonstration.* If :math:`dL` be drawn, and :math:`Dp` to its midpoint at :math:`p`, we shall have :math:`\sin LDp = \frac{Lp}{DL}`. But the angle :math:`LDp = \frac{1}{2} LDd = \frac{1}{2}(BDd - BDL) = 30^\circ - BDn`. Therefore (§ 155) :math:`\sin LDp = \frac{1}{2}\cos BDn - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \sin BDn = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}`. Hence :math:`Lp = DL \cdot \sin LDp = \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{3}` and :math:`Ld = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} = AN`.

**272. Problem.** Given (Fig. 104) the side :math:`AB` of a square, to find the radius of a circle that may be approximately equal to it in area.

*Sol.* With radius :math:`AB`, center :math:`A`, describe the circumference :math:`BCFDLPMEd`. Make :math:`AB = BC = CD = Ed`. With center :math:`B` and radius :math:`BD` describe the arc :math:`dnNDa`. With the same radius and center :math:`E` cut this arc at :math:`a`. Make :math:`Aa = BF = Db = db`.
Then make :math:`AB = Dn = dN`. With center :math:`C` and radius :math:`CN`, cut the circumference at :math:`P`. Make :math:`Nn = PM`; then :math:`Fb = CL`. :math:`LM` will be the side sought.

*Demonstration.* If we suppose :math:`AB = 1`, the triangle :math:`BNd` having its sides respectively equal to the sides of triangle :math:`BDL` of Fig. 103, we shall have :math:`\sin \frac{1}{2} dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}`; :math:`\cos \frac{1}{2} dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33}` (§ 271). Hence :math:`\sin dBN = 2\sin \frac{1}{2} dBN \cdot \cos \frac{1}{2} dBN` (§ 155) :math:`= \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}`; :math:`\cos dBN = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 dBN} = \frac{5}{6}`. Since :math:`CBd` is a right angle (III.31), :math:`\cos CBN` will be :math:`= \sin dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}`. But from Trigonometry we have :math:`(CN)^2 = (BC)^2 + (BN)^2 - 2BC \cdot BN \cdot \cos CBN`. Therefore :math:`CN = \sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}} = \sqrt{4 - \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{33}} = 1.4440025`, which is found to be the chord of :math:`92^\circ 26' \frac{804}{2013} = CP`.
Since then :math:`\sin NBE = \frac{\frac{1}{2} Nd}{BN} = \sin(CBE - CBN) =` (§ 155) :math:`\sin 60^\circ \cos CBN - \cos 60^\circ \cdot \sin CBN = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{6}`; we shall have :math:`Nn = 2BN \times \sin NBE = \frac{1}{6}(\sqrt{11} - 5\sqrt{3}) = 0.2149367`, which is found to be the chord of :math:`12^\circ 20' \frac{946}{2892} = PM`.

The arc :math:`CPM` will therefore be :math:`= 104^\circ 46' \frac{4220}{5821}`, from which subtracting the arc :math:`CL`, which is a fifth part of the circumference (§ 40) :math:`= 72^\circ`, there will remain the arc :math:`LM = 32^\circ 46' \frac{4220}{5821}`, whose chord is found :math:`= 0.5643274`.

Now setting :math:`\pi` for the ratio of the circumference to the diameter, and :math:`R` for the radius of a circle, its area is :math:`= \pi R^2`, as is known. Making therefore :math:`\pi R^2 = 1`, which is the area of the square of radius :math:`AB = 1` to which the circle is desired equal, we shall have :math:`R = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi}}`, and :math:`\log R = -\frac{1}{2}\log \pi = -0.2485749 = -1 + 0.7514251 = \log 0.5641896`. We shall not therefore commit an error of :math:`0.0002`.
