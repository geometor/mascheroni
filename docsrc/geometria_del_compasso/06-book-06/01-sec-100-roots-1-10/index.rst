=============================================
§§ 100–103: Square Roots √1 through √10 & √36
=============================================

.. folio:: 0076
   :page: 51

**BOOK SIX**

**ON ROOTS**

**100. Problem.** To find easily the roots of the whole numbers from one to ten (Fig. 43), taking the distance :math:`AB` as unity.

*Solution.* With radius :math:`AB`, describe the circle :math:`BDd`; make :math:`AB = BC = CD = DE = Ed = dc` along its circumference. With centers :math:`B` and :math:`E`, and with radius :math:`BD`, mark arcs that intersect at :math:`a` and :math:`\alpha`.
With the same radius :math:`BD`, and with centers :math:`D` and :math:`d`, mark two arcs that intersect at :math:`V`. With radius :math:`Aa`, and with center :math:`B`, cut the circumference at :math:`F`. With centers :math:`B` and :math:`F`, and with radius :math:`AB`, mark two arcs that intersect at :math:`T`. We shall have

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   AB &= \sqrt{1} & aV &= \sqrt{6} \\
   Aa &= \sqrt{2} & CV &= \sqrt{7} \\
   BD &= \sqrt{3} & a\alpha &= \sqrt{8} \\
   BE &= \sqrt{4} & BV &= \sqrt{9} \\
   ET &= \sqrt{5} & TV &= \sqrt{10}
   \end{aligned}

*Demonstration.* It has been proved that :math:`(Aa)^2 = 2` (§ 27). Therefore :math:`Aa = \sqrt{2}`. It has been proved that :math:`BD = \sqrt{3}` (§ 2). We also have :math:`BE = 2 = \sqrt{4}`.

Then, since the two triangles :math:`BTA`, :math:`TAF` have their sides equal to each other, the angle :math:`BTA = TAF` (I.8). Hence :math:`BT` is parallel to :math:`FA` (I.28), and therefore :math:`BT` is also perpendicular to :math:`BA`, just like :math:`FA` (§ 27) (I.27).
Since point :math:`A` and point :math:`E` are equidistant from points :math:`D` and :math:`d`, and so likewise are point :math:`B` and point :math:`V`, the four points :math:`B, A, E, V` lie on the same straight line (§ 13), and :math:`EV = BA` (§ 14).
Therefore :math:`(ET)^2 = (TB)^2 + (BE)^2` (I.47) :math:`= (AB)^2 + 4(AB)^2 = 5`; hence :math:`ET = \sqrt{5}`. Likewise :math:`(aV)^2 = (Aa)^2 + (AV)^2`. But since :math:`EV = BA`, we also have :math:`AV = BE = 2AB`; hence :math:`(AV)^2 = 4(AB)^2 = 4`, and :math:`(Aa)^2 = 2` (§ 27).

Therefore :math:`(aV)^2 = 6`, and :math:`aV = \sqrt{6}`. Comparing points :math:`C, B, c, A, V` with points :math:`A, p, B, P, Q` of Fig. 3, and making the substitutions in the equation :math:`(AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + pQ \cdot PQ` (§ 18), we obtain :math:`(CV)^2 = (CB)^2 + BV \cdot AV = 1 + 3 \cdot 2 = 7`. Hence :math:`CV = \sqrt{7}`.
Since :math:`Aa = A\alpha` (§ 14), we also have :math:`(a\alpha)^2 = 4(Aa)^2 = 8`; hence :math:`a\alpha = \sqrt{8}`. Next :math:`BV = 3 = \sqrt{9}`. Finally, since :math:`(TV)^2 = (TB)^2 + (BV)^2 = 1 + 9 = 10`, we have :math:`TV = \sqrt{10}`.

**101. Problem.** By means of the roots found in the preceding problem (Fig. 44), to find the other roots of whole numbers from 10 to 36.

*Solution.* Subtract the number whose root is sought from the next greater square number, which will be 16, or 25, or 36. With the root of the remainder—which will be found in the list of § 100—taken as radius, and with center :math:`A`, describe the semicircle :math:`QLR` (§ 64).
With the root of the next greater square number taken as radius—which is found by the method of § 65—and with centers :math:`Q` and :math:`R`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`P`. Then :math:`AP` will be the required root.

For example, suppose we want the root of 29. Subtracted from 36, this leaves remainder 7. With radius :math:`CV = \sqrt{7}` (§ 100), describe the semicircle :math:`QLR`, and with centers :math:`Q` and :math:`R`, and with radius :math:`= 6`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`P`; then :math:`AP = \sqrt{29}`.

*Demonstration.* Since the angle :math:`PAQ` is right (§ 83), we have :math:`(PQ)^2 = (AQ)^2 + (AP)^2` (I.47). Hence subtracting :math:`(AQ)^2`, we obtain :math:`(PQ)^2 - (AQ)^2 = (AP)^2`. Now setting :math:`(PQ)^2 = 36` and :math:`(AQ)^2` successively equal to the whole numbers from unity up to 10,
we shall have :math:`(AP)^2` successively equal from 36 down to 25. Therefore for :math:`AP` we shall obtain successively the roots of these numbers. The root of 25 is 5, and is had from § 65. Setting :math:`(PQ)^2 = 25`, we obtain by the same method the roots from 25 down to 16,
and setting :math:`(PQ)^2 = 16`, we obtain the others from 16 down to 10.

In the cited example we have :math:`(PQ)^2 - (AQ)^2 = 36 - 7 = (AP)^2 = 29`. Therefore :math:`AP = \sqrt{29}`.

**102. Problem.** To find the roots of all whole numbers.

*Solution.* It is clear that by using the same method of § 101 with the roots already obtained, we can obtain other roots of higher numbers, and with these still others, and so successively to infinity. Thus we already have the means to obtain the roots of all whole numbers.

**103. Problem.** To find the root of any fractional number.

*Solution.* Find the root of the denominator (§ 102), then that of the numerator. Make as the first root is to the second, so unity is to a fourth proportional (§ 93). This will be the required root.

*Demonstration.* For if the denominator be :math:`d` and the numerator be :math:`n`, if we make :math:`\sqrt{d} : \sqrt{n} :: 1 : \text{fourth proportional}`, this fourth will be :math:`= \frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}}`. But :math:`\frac{\sqrt{n}}{\sqrt{d}} = \sqrt{\frac{n}{d}}`. Therefore, etc.
