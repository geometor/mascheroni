============================================================
§§ 108–109: Acute-Angle Refinement & Alternative Root Scheme
============================================================

.. folio:: 0084
   :page: 59

**108.** In the preceding construction, care was taken to employ as much as possible the first compass of opening :math:`= 1`, with which the circumference :math:`BDd` was described, which, preserving the fundamental opening, deserves more trust than the others.
Likewise, it was desired to employ only the three most notable first compasses (§ 107). This caused a few intersections of the arcs to turn out at somewhat acute angles, such as those of points :math:`S, s, O, o`, and even more those of points :math:`L` and :math:`l`. Whoever wishes to have all angles of intersection closer to a right angle may use the following

*Another construction of fig. 45.*

**109.** Having found the points :math:`M` and :math:`m` as in the solution of § 104, with radius :math:`BD` and centers :math:`M` and :math:`m`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`H`.

With radius :math:`AB` and center :math:`H`, describe an arc cutting the circumference at :math:`O` and :math:`o`. With the same radius, determine the semicircumferences :math:`OEs, oES` (§ 64).

With radius :math:`BE` and center :math:`H`, cut the circumference at :math:`L` and :math:`l`.

Let all the other points of the figure be found as in § 104.

We shall demonstrate that the points found by this construction are the same as those of the other.

Since it has been proved (§ 104) that :math:`BM = MR = Rm = mB`, and that the three points :math:`B, A, R` lie on the same straight line; and since :math:`MH = ME = mH = mE` by construction, :math:`H` will lie on the same straight line :math:`BAR` (§ 13), and we shall have :math:`HB = RE` (§ 14).
Therefore :math:`H` will be the same point as in the other construction. Then since :math:`HO, Ho` are equal in the two constructions, the points :math:`O, o` will also be the same. If we subtract the arc :math:`osE` from the two semicircumferences :math:`BsE, oES`, the remaining arcs :math:`Bo, ES` will be equal.
Hence :math:`ES = Bo = BO = AQ = RQ`. Therefore point :math:`S` will also be the same as before. Likewise so will point :math:`s`. Next, since the base :math:`HTR` of the triangle :math:`HLR` is bisected by :math:`LT`, we shall have (§ 26) :math:`(HL)^2 + (LR)^2 = 2(HT)^2 + 2(TL)^2`; that is, :math:`4 + (LR)^2 = 2 + 2(TL)^2`, or :math:`2 + (LR)^2 = 2(TL)^2`.
But since the base :math:`TR` of the

.. folio:: 0085
   :page: 60

triangle :math:`TLR` is also bisected at :math:`A` by the straight line :math:`LA`, we shall have (§ 26) :math:`(TL)^2 + (LR)^2 = 2(TA)^2 + 2(AL)^2`; and doubling, :math:`2(TL)^2 + 2(LR)^2 = 4(TA)^2 + 4(AL)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5`; hence subtracting :math:`2(LR)^2`, we have :math:`2(TL)^2 = 5 - 2(LR)^2`.
But above we found :math:`2(TL)^2 = 2 + (LR)^2`. Therefore :math:`5 - 2(LR)^2 = 2 + (LR)^2`. And subtracting 2 and adding :math:`2(LR)^2`, we have :math:`3 = 3(LR)^2`; hence :math:`1 = (LR)^2 = (AB)^2`. Therefore :math:`LR = AB` as in the first construction. The same will be proved of :math:`Rl`. The other points are determined as before. Therefore all the points of the figure are the same as before.
