============================================
§§ 27–29: Quadrisection of the Circumference
============================================

.. folio:: 0048
   :page: 19

**§ 27. Problem (Fig. 9).** Divide a given circumference (with known center :g:`A` and radius :g:`/ A B /`) into four equal parts using the compass alone.

*Construction:* Starting from :g:`B` on circle :g:`( A B )`, step three chords equal to radius :g:`/ A B /` to locate :g:`C`, :g:`D`, and the antipodal point :g:`E` (:math:`BC = CD = DE = AB`). Take the hexagon diagonal :g:`/ B D /` (:math:`= \sqrt{3}\,AB`) as compass radius, and from centers :g:`B` and :g:`E` sweep arcs :g:`( B D )` and :g:`( E C )` intersecting at :g:`a`. Then :g:`/ A a /` :math:`= \sqrt{3 - 1} = \sqrt{2}\,AB`, the exact side of the inscribed square! Finally, with radius :g:`/ A a /` from center :g:`B`, sweep arc :g:`( B F )` cutting the circle at :g:`F` and :g:`f`. The four vertices :g:`B`, :g:`F`, :g:`E`, :g:`f` divide the circumference into exact quadrants (:eq:`/ B F / = / F E / = / E f / = / f B /`).

**§§ 28–29.** Historical jewel: this is the celebrated construction General :person:`Napoléon Bonaparte <bonaparte>` presented to :person:`Laplace <laplace>` and :person:`Lagrange <lagrange>` in Paris in December 1797.
