=====================================================
§§ 40–44: Regular Pentagon, Decagon & 120-Part Circle
=====================================================

.. folio:: 0053
   :page: 24

**§§ 40–41. Problem (Fig. 12).** *Divide the circumference into 5 and 10 equal parts (inscribed regular pentagon and decagon) by the compass alone.*

*Construction:* Starting from circle :g:`( A B )` of unit radius :math:`AB = 1`, construct the hexagon vertices :g:`B`, :g:`C`, :g:`D`, :g:`E`, :g:`d` and quadrisection point :g:`a` (:math:`Aa = \sqrt{2}`, with :g:`F` at :math:`90^\circ`) as in § 27. Now set the compass to :math:`Aa = \sqrt{2}` and sweep arcs :g:`( D b )` and :g:`( d b )` from centers :g:`D` and :g:`d`, intersecting inside segment :g:`/ B E /` at point :g:`b`.

*Exact Algebraic Proof:* Since :math:`D = (\frac{1}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})` and :math:`d = (\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})`, the condition :math:`Db = \sqrt{2}` gives :math:`(x_b - \frac{1}{2})^2 + \frac{3}{4} = 2 \implies x_b = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2}`. Hence :g:`/ A b /` :math:`= \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{2} = \phi^{-1}` is the **exact side of the inscribed regular decagon**, and the hypotenuse :g:`/ F b /` :math:`= \sqrt{1 + \phi^{-2}} = \sqrt{\frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{2}}` is the **exact side of the inscribed regular pentagon**!

**§§ 42–44.** Combining the 5-fold, 8-fold, and 3-fold divisions immediately yields the **120-part division** (:math:`3^\circ`) on the same Fig. 12.
