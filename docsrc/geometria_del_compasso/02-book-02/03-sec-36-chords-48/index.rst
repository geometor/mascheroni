=========================================
§§ 36–39: Chord Lemmas & 48-Part Division
=========================================

.. folio:: 0050
   :page: 21

**36.** *Lemma.* If in the circle :math:`BGE` the radius be :math:`AB = 1`, and

.. folio:: 0051
   :page: 22

the arc :math:`BG` be an eighth part of the circumference, the square of its chord :math:`BG` will be :math:`(BG)^2 = 2 - \sqrt{2}` (fig. 10).

.. picture:: 0051.g1.svg
   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Mascheroni Book II § 36 (Fig. 10): Chord and Projection on Diameter

.. picture:: 0051.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 10 (folio 0051, p. 22): Half-angle chord relation

*Dem.* Upon the diameter :math:`BE` drop the perpendicular :math:`GP`. In the right triangle :math:`GPA`, because of the half-right angle :math:`GAP`, :math:`AGP` will also be half-right (I.32). Hence the sides :math:`GP, PA` will be equal (I.6). Furthermore, :math:`(AG)^2 = (PG)^2 + (AP)^2` (I.47). Therefore :math:`(AG)^2 = 2(AP)^2`; hence :math:`2(AG)^2 = 4(AP)^2`, or :math:`2 = (2AP)^2`.
Thus :math:`\sqrt{2} = 2AP`; :math:`AP = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}`; :math:`BP = AB - AP = 1 - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}`. Next, since angle :math:`BGE` is right (III.31), we have :math:`BP : BG :: BG : BE` (VI.8, 4); hence (VI.17) :math:`(GB)^2 = BP \cdot BE = 2BP`. Therefore :math:`(BG)^2 = 2 - \sqrt{2}`.

**37.** *Lemma.* Remaining as in § 36, the square of :math:`GE`, the chord of three eighth parts of the circumference, will be :math:`(GE)^2 = 2 + \sqrt{2}`.

*Dem.* Since :math:`(BE)^2 = (GE)^2 + (BG)^2` (I.47); while :math:`(BE)^2 = 4` and :math:`(BG)^2 = 2 - \sqrt{2}` (§ 36). Therefore :math:`4 = (GE)^2 + 2 - \sqrt{2}`, and subtracting :math:`2` and adding :math:`\sqrt{2}`, we get :math:`2 + \sqrt{2} = (GE)^2`.

**38.** *Prob.* The circumference having already been divided into twenty-four equal parts (§ 32), to subdivide it into forty-eight.

.. picture:: 0051.g2.svg
   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Mascheroni Book II § 36 (Fig. 11): Division of the Circumference into 48 Parts

.. picture:: 0051.g2.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 11 (folio 0051, p. 22): Exact 48-part division of the circle

*Sol.* Make :math:`aN = Be = Ee` (§ 11) with the 4th compass; and :math:`AB = e\mu = e\nu` with the 1st compass. Then :math:`K\mu, \mu N, M\nu, \nu O` (fig. 11) will be forty-eighth parts of the circumference.

*Dem.* If we conceive the straight lines :math:`Aa, Nn, aN, aB` drawn (§ 12); since the angle

.. folio:: 0052
   :page: 23

:math:`BAa` is right (§ 27), and angle :math:`BAN = BAn` (§ 31), and the three radii :math:`AN, AB, An` are equal, we shall have :math:`(aN)^2 = (aB)^2 - Nn \cdot Aa` (§ 20);
hence because :math:`aN = Be`, we shall also have :math:`(Be)^2 = (aB)^2 - Nn \cdot Aa`. Next, since triangles :math:`eAB, eAE` have their sides respectively equal, they are right-angled at :math:`A` (I.8 and 13), so :math:`(Be)^2 = (AB)^2 + (Ae)^2` (I.47). Therefore :math:`(AB)^2 + (Ae)^2 = (aB)^2 - Nn \cdot Aa`.
But :math:`(aB)^2 = (AB)^2 + (Aa)^2`. Hence :math:`(AB)^2 + (Ae)^2 = (AB)^2 + (Aa)^2 - Nn \cdot Aa`; subtracting :math:`(AB)^2` gives :math:`(Ae)^2 = (Aa)^2 - Nn\,Aa`. Now :math:`(Aa)^2 = 2` (§ 27), and :math:`Nn = 1`, since :math:`Nn` is the chord of a sixth part of the circumference (§ 31).
Therefore :math:`(Ae)^2 = 2 - \sqrt{2} =` the square of the chord of arc :math:`BG`, which is the eighth part of the circumference (§§ 30 and 36). Hence arc :math:`B\mu = \mu G` (§ 24). If from these arcs we subtract the equal arcs :math:`BK, NG` (§ 32), the arcs :math:`K\mu, \mu N` will be equal.
Since arc :math:`KN` is the twenty-fourth part of the circumference (§ 32), its halves, namely arcs :math:`K\mu, \mu N`, and for the same reason arcs :math:`M\nu, \nu O`, will be the forty-eighth part of the circumference.

**39.** Remaining as above, anyone who wished could, with the sole aid of the four compasses indicated above,
divide the entire circumference into forty-eight equal parts (§ 8) (fig. 11). For if with the first compass of opening :math:`= AB` we divide the circumference into six parts starting from point :math:`\mu`, the arcs :math:`IF, HO, mo, fl, ng` will be bisected. Dividing then the circumference into six parts starting from point :math:`\nu`,
the arcs :math:`oh, fi, nk, NG, FL` will remain divided. Dividing then the circumference into four parts with the third compass of opening equal to :math:`Aa` starting from point :math:`\mu`, the arcs :math:`LD, ic` will remain divided; starting then from point :math:`\nu`, the arcs :math:`IC, Id` will remain divided.
Subsequently dividing the circumference into six equal parts again with the first compass, but starting from the last points found with the third compass, all the remaining arcs will be bisected.

The demonstration is similar to that of § 32.
