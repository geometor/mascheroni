=============================================================
§§ 45–63: Golden Ratio Proof, 240-Part Circle & Arc Bisection
=============================================================

.. folio:: 0055
   :page: 26

**45.** Since by § 40

.. math::

   Xb + XF &= Fb = Xb + XA \\
   Ab &= Xb - XA \\
   \text{we have} \quad Fb \cdot Ab &= (Xb)^2 - (XA)^2 \\
   &= (XB)^2 - (XA)^2 = (AB)^2

that is, :math:`Fb \cdot Ab = (FA)^2`; hence :math:`Fb` will be divided at :math:`A` in extreme and mean ratio (VI.30).

**46.** Hence :math:`Fb \cdot Ab = (FA + Ab) \cdot Ab = (fA)^2 = (fA + Ab) \cdot Ab = fA \cdot Ab + (Ab)^2 = fA \cdot (fA - fb) + (Ab)^2 = (fA)^2 - fA \cdot fb + (Ab)^2`. Since therefore :math:`(fA)^2 = (fA)^2 - fA \cdot fb + (Ab)^2`, subtracting :math:`(fA)^2` and adding :math:`fA \cdot fb`, we obtain :math:`fA \cdot fb = (Ab)^2`. Therefore :math:`Af` is also divided at :math:`b` in extreme and mean ratio.

.. folio:: 0056
   :page: 27

**47.** If with center :math:`f` and radius :math:`bA` we cut the circumference at :math:`T`, we shall have :math:`Tf = Tb = bA`. For :math:`fA \cdot fb = (Ab)^2` (§ 46) :math:`= (Tf)^2`. Hence (VI.17) :math:`fA : fT :: fT : fb`. Therefore the two triangles :math:`fAT, fbT`, which have the common angle at :math:`f`, will have the sides about it proportional, and so (VI.6) will be similar. Thus triangle :math:`fbT` will also be isosceles, and :math:`Tb = Tf`.

**48.** The angle :math:`TbA = Tfb + bTf` (I.32) :math:`= Tbf + bAT`, and adding :math:`Tbf`, we have :math:`TbA + Tbf = 2\,Tbf + bAT`; but :math:`TbA + Tbf =` two right angles (I.13). Therefore :math:`2\,Tbf + bAT =` two right angles.
But :math:`Tbf = bAT + bTA` (I.32) :math:`= 2\,bAT` (I.5). Therefore :math:`2\,Tbf + bAT = 5\,bAT =` two right angles. Hence the angle :math:`bAT`, which is the same as the angle :math:`fAT`, will be a fifth of two right angles, and the arc :math:`fT` a tenth of the circumference.

**49.** If we take the chord :math:`ft = fT`, we shall also have :math:`bt = ft` (§ 47), and the two lines :math:`tT, bf` will bisect each other at right angles at a point :math:`y` (§ 14); and :math:`(Tf)^2 = (Ty)^2 + (fy)^2`; hence :math:`4(Tf)^2 = 4(Ab)^2 = 4(Ty)^2 + 4(fy)^2 = (Tt)^2 + (fb)^2`.
Hence :math:`(Tt)^2 = 4(Ab)^2 - (fb)^2`. But :math:`(fb)^2 = (fA - Ab)^2 = (fA)^2 - 2\,fA \cdot Ab + (Ab)^2`. Therefore :math:`(Tt)^2 = 3(Ab)^2 - (fA)^2 + 2\,fA \cdot Ab`. Now :math:`2\,fA \cdot Ab = 2\,fA \cdot (fA - fb) = 2(fA)^2 - 2\,fA \cdot fb = 2(fA)^2 - 2(Ab)^2`.
Therefore :math:`(Tt)^2 = 3(Ab)^2 - (fA)^2 + 2(fA)^2 - 2(Ab)^2 = (fA)^2 + (Ab)^2 = (BA)^2 + (Ab)^2 = (Bb)^2`; and hence :math:`Tt = Bb`. But :math:`Tt` is the chord of two tenths, that is, of a fifth part of the circumference. Therefore so also is :math:`Bb`. Hence

**50.** In the right-angled triangle :math:`ABb`, the square of the side of the pentagon is equal to the sum of the squares of the sides of the hexagon and of the decagon. This is XIII.10 of Euclid.

**51.** The sides of the right-angled triangle :math:`ABb` are chords of arcs that are in contraharmonic progression, since these arcs are :math:`\frac{1}{5}, \frac{1}{6}, \frac{1}{10}` of the circumference. We then find

.. math::

   \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{6} : \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{10} :: \frac{1}{10} : \frac{1}{5} .

**52.** Since :math:`fb : bA :: bA : Af` (§ 46), we also have :math:`fb : bA :: bA : AF`; and thus the diameter :math:`Ff` remains divided at points :math:`A` and :math:`b` into three parts in continued proportion.

**53.** *Prob.* To divide the circumference into twenty parts, that is, to find a twentieth part of it.
