Commentary & Notes: Libro III — The Architectural Core of Mascheroni's Theorem
==============================================================================

.. rubric:: Why Midpoint Bisection is the Gateway to Compass Completeness

1. Bisection Without Lines as the First Major Obstacle
------------------------------------------------------

In classical geometry, bisecting a line segment :math:`AB` (:cite:`euclid-heath`, Book I, Proposition 10) appears trivial: describe two equal circles :math:`A(AB)` and :math:`B(BA)` cutting at :math:`C` and :math:`D`, and draw the straight line :math:`CD` cutting :math:`AB` at its midpoint :math:`M`.

In compass-only geometry, however, the line :math:`CD` **cannot be drawn**, and :math:`AB` itself is not drawn on paper. Finding the exact point :math:`M` requires constructing the intersection of two circles that meet precisely at the midpoint on the invisible line between :math:`A` and :math:`B`.

2. The Genius of Solution V
---------------------------

Mascheroni presented four alternative solutions in § 66 before stating Solution V:

* Solutions I–IV use auxiliary equilateral triangles and perpendicular projections, requiring between 9 and 15 circles.
* **Solution V** achieves the bisection in just 7 circles (and only 5 distinct radii: :math:`AB, 2AB`), taking advantage of the collinear doubling point :math:`E`.
* Because :math:`E, A, B` are collinear, the symmetry across the line of centers guarantees that the intersecting arcs from :math:`P` and :math:`p` must close symmetrically upon the line :math:`AB`.
* As :person:`Gaetano Fazzari <fazzari>` emphasized, Solution V demonstrates that collinear points can be manipulated and generated with supreme mathematical rigor using circular metrics alone.
