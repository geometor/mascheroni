================================================
Book III § 64: Linear Distance Doubling
================================================

.. rubric:: Lorenzo Mascheroni (*The Geometry of the Compass*, Pavia 1797, § 64, p. 31)

.. folio:: 0057

§ 64. *Problem.* To double the distance :math:`AB` in a straight line.

*Solution.* With radius :math:`AB` and center :math:`B`, describe the circle or arc :math:`BCDE`. With the same radius and center :math:`A`, cut the circumference at :math:`C`; with center :math:`C`, cut at :math:`D`; with center :math:`D`, cut at :math:`E`. I say that the distance :math:`AE` is double :math:`AB`, and lies in a straight line with it.

.. picture:: /_static/figures/plates/fig_02_03.png
   :alt: Mascheroni Linear Doubling (Book III § 64)

*Demonstration.* Since the radii :math:`BC, CD, DE` and the chords :math:`BC, CD, DE` are all equal to radius :math:`AB`, each of the three triangles :math:`\triangle ABC, \triangle BCD, \triangle BDE` is equilateral. Each of the central angles :math:`\angle ABC, \angle CBD, \angle DBE` is therefore equal to one-third of two right angles, or :math:`60^\circ` (Euclid I.32).

The sum of the three angles is consequently equal to two right angles (:math:`180^\circ`). Therefore the line :math:`ABE` is a single continuous straight line (Euclid I.14).

Furthermore, since :math:`BE = AB`, the total distance :math:`AE = AB + BE = 2AB`. Which was to be constructed and demonstrated.
