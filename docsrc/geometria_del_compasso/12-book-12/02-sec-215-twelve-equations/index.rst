=======================================================
§§ 215–235: The Twelve Logarithmic Graduation Equations
=======================================================

.. folio:: 0152
   :page: 127

**215.** More limited are equations (5) and (6). For in (5) one cannot employ arcs :math:`B'` whose chord is less than :math:`bf`, nor greater than :math:`bF`; likewise in (6) all arcs :math:`E'` of chord less than :math:`eF` and greater than :math:`ef` are excluded.

**216.** We shall see hereafter the cases in which some of these values prove useful for the division of the circle, or for some other problem that cannot be solved except by approximation, choosing from the whole calculated list those that give the closest approach to the true value sought, and at the same time are solved with intersections of arcs less distant from the right angle.

**217.** Meanwhile, to obtain all possible advantages from the three points :math:`a`, :math:`b`, and :math:`e` without introducing new points, we shall derive from the

.. folio:: 0153
   :page: 128

three equations :math:`(A)`, :math:`(B)`, and :math:`(E)` six other equations so as to have new values of arcs and chords.

**218.** If the arc :math:`BZ` be a known arc, for instance one of those obtained by the Problems of the Second Book, and it be called :math:`B`; and if the distance :math:`bZ` be taken on the compass, and carried from :math:`a` to some other point :math:`Z'`, which determines another arc :math:`BZ' = A`, one can obtain from comparing the two equations :math:`(A)` and :math:`(B)` a new equation that makes it known.
For if in those two equations we set :math:`bZ = aZ'`; making in :math:`(A)` :math:`Zz = 2\sin A`, and in :math:`(B)` :math:`Zz = 2\sin B`, we shall have :math:`(aB)^2 - 2\sin A \cdot Aa = (bB)^2 + 2\sin B \cdot Ab`, or :math:`3 - 2\sin A \cdot \sqrt{2} = \frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{2} + \sin B (\sqrt{5} - 1)` (§ 199), and isolating :math:`\sin A`, we shall have :math:`\sin A = \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} - 2\sin B \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{4} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = \sin 54^\circ \sin 45^\circ - 2\sin B \cdot \sin 18^\circ \cdot \sin 45^\circ` (§ 210) :math:`= \frac{1}{2}(\cos 9^\circ + \sin 9^\circ) - \sin B \cdot (\cos 27^\circ - \sin 27^\circ)` (§ 159); hence (§ 156 and ff.)

.. math::

   (7) \quad \sin A = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \begin{aligned}
   &\cos 9^\circ + \sin 9^\circ \\
   + &\cos(B - 27^\circ) \\
   - &\sin(B - 27^\circ) \\
   - &\cos(B - 63^\circ) \\
   + &\sin(B - 63^\circ)
   \end{aligned} \right\}


**219.** Here too, not all arcs can be taken for :math:`B`, but only those that give a distance :math:`bZ` or :math:`bz` not less than :math:`aF`.

**220.** Now if an arc :math:`BZ` be known, which is called :math:`A`, and having taken the distance :math:`aZ` on the compass it be carried from :math:`b` to some point :math:`Z'` of the same circumference, which determines the arc :math:`BZ' = B`; this arc :math:`B`, and its chord if needed, will be known, if in the equation
:math:`\sin A = \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} - 2\sin B \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{4} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}}` (§ 218) we isolate :math:`\sin B`. Let this equation be multiplied by the factor :math:`2(\sqrt{5} + 1)\sqrt{2}`, and we shall have :math:`2\sin A \cdot (\sqrt{5} + 1)\sqrt{2} = 3 + \sqrt{5} - 4\sin B`; whence we shall have :math:`\sin B = \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{4} - 4\sin A \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = 2\sin^2 54^\circ - 4\sin A \sin 54^\circ \cdot \sin 45^\circ` (§ 210), and using as above the equations

.. folio:: 0154
   :page: 129

of §§ 156 and following, there will result finally

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   (8) \quad \sin B = 1 + \sin 18^\circ &- \sin(A + 9^\circ) + \cos(A + 9^\circ) \\
   &- \sin(A - 9^\circ) - \cos(A - 9^\circ).
   \end{aligned}


**221.** In this equation (8) one cannot employ for :math:`A` those arcs :math:`BZ` that give a distance :math:`aZ` greater than :math:`bF`.

**222.** Now if in equation :math:`(A)` (§ 197) we put :math:`Zz = 2\sin A`, and in equation :math:`(E)` another :math:`Zz = 2\sin E`; putting moreover in these two equations :math:`(aZ)^2 = (eZ)^2`, we shall have after substituting the values (§ 199) :math:`3 - 2\sin A \cdot \sqrt{2} = 3 - \sqrt{2} - 2\sin E \cdot \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}`; :math:`\sin A = \frac{1}{2} + 2\sin E \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{1}{2} + 2\sin E \sin 22^\circ 30' \sin 45^\circ = \frac{1}{2} + \sin E \cos 22^\circ 30' - \sin E \sin 22^\circ 30'` (§ 159). Hence finally (§§ 157 and 158)

.. math::

   (9) \quad \sin A = \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \begin{aligned}
   + &\sin(E + 22^\circ 30') \\
   + &\cos(E + 22^\circ 30') \\
   + &\sin(E - 22^\circ 30') \\
   - &\cos(E - 22^\circ 30')
   \end{aligned} \right\}


**223.** This ninth equation can also be prepared in another way, so that the calculation may turn out easier by means of the artificial sines and cosines, which are usually found in the tables more easily from ten to ten seconds. For since (§ 222)

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \sin A &= \frac{1}{2} + 2\sin E \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = \left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{2}}} + \sin E\right) \frac{\sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{2}}}{2} \\
   &= \left(\frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} + \sin E\right) \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} : \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} = (\sin 67^\circ 30' + \sin E) \frac{\sin 22^\circ 30'}{\sin 45^\circ} \quad (\text{§ } 37).
   \end{aligned}

And setting :math:`67^\circ 30' = p`; :math:`E = q`; we shall have

.. math::

   [9] \quad \sin A = \frac{\sin \frac{67^\circ 30' + E}{2} \cos \frac{67^\circ 30' - E}{2}}{\cos 22^\circ 30'}.

Which equation will be easy to calculate by means of the logarithms of sines and cosines.

**224.** If therefore we take on the compass a distance from point :math:`e` to some point :math:`Z`, endpoint of a known arc :math:`BZ = E`, and carry it from :math:`a` to some other point :math:`Z'` of the circumference, we shall know the new arc :math:`BZ' = A` by means of equation (9), or of [9]. These will have their limits,

.. folio:: 0155
   :page: 130

since the arc :math:`BZ` must be such that :math:`eZ` is not less than :math:`aF`.

**225.** Since from § 222 we have :math:`2\sin A \cdot \sqrt{2} = \sqrt{2} + 2\sin E \cdot \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}`: if we multiply this equation by :math:`\sqrt{\frac{2 + \sqrt{2}}{2}}`, we shall have :math:`2\sin A \cdot \sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}} + 2\sin E`, and hence :math:`\sin E = 2\sin A \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} - \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} = 2\sin A \cdot \sin 67^\circ 30' - \sin 67^\circ 30'` (§ 37); hence (§ 159)

.. math::

   (10) \quad \sin E = \cos(A - 67^\circ 30') - \cos(A + 67^\circ 30') - \sin 67^\circ 30'.

**226.** This tenth equation can also be prepared differently for the use of logarithms. For since :math:`\sin E = (\sin A - \frac{1}{2}) \cdot 2\frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} = [\sin A + \sin(-30^\circ)] 2\sin 67^\circ 30'`, setting :math:`A = p; -30^\circ = q`, we shall have (§ 157)

.. math::

   [10] \quad \sin E = 4\sin\frac{A - 30^\circ}{2} \cos\frac{A + 30^\circ}{2} \sin 67^\circ 30'.


**227.** It is clear that all values of the arc :math:`BZ`, or :math:`Bz = \pm A`, which give :math:`aZ` greater than :math:`ef`, cannot be introduced into the tenth equation, which will thus receive its limits.

**228.** Finally, two other equations can be obtained from comparing equation :math:`(B)` with :math:`(E)`. For if one takes a distance from :math:`e` to some point :math:`Z` of the circumference that is the endpoint of a known arc :math:`BZ = E`,
and with this distance :math:`eZ` taken as radius, and with center at :math:`b`, one marks an arc cutting the circumference at some other point :math:`Z'`, determining the arc :math:`BZ' = B`; this arc will be known by means of its sine in the following manner. Setting in equation :math:`(B)` (§ 197) :math:`Zz = 2\sin B` and in equation :math:`(E)` :math:`Zz = 2\sin E`, and setting in them :math:`bZ = eZ`, we shall have after substituting the values (§ 199) :math:`\frac{5 - \sqrt{5}}{2} + \sin B (\sqrt{5} - 1) = 3 - \sqrt{2} - 2\sin E \cdot \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}`; whence is obtained :math:`2\sin B \cdot (\sqrt{5} - 1) = \sqrt{5} + 1 - 2\sqrt{2} - 4\sin E \cdot \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}`; and multiplying both sides by :math:`\frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{8}`, we shall have

.. folio:: 0156
   :page: 131

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \sin B &= \frac{3 + \sqrt{5}}{4} - \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} - 4\sin E \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}}{2} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4} \\
   &= 2\sin^2 54^\circ - 2\sin 54^\circ \sin 45^\circ - 4\sin E \cdot \sin 22^\circ 30' \sin 54^\circ \quad (\text{§ } 210);
   \end{aligned}

and hence (§ 159) :math:`\sin B = 1 - \cos 108^\circ - \cos 9^\circ - \sin 9^\circ - 2\sin E (\cos 31^\circ 30' - \cos 76^\circ 30')`, and hence (§ 157)

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   (11) \quad \sin B = 1 &+ \sin 18^\circ - \cos 9^\circ - \sin 9^\circ - \sin(E + 31^\circ 30') \\
   &- \sin(E - 31^\circ 30') + \sin(E + 76^\circ 30') + \sin(E - 76^\circ 30').
   \end{aligned}

**229.** This equation (11) too will have limits at both ends. For the minimum distance :math:`eF = 1 - \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}` is less than the distance :math:`bf = 1 - \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{5} - 1)`, and the maximum :math:`ef` is greater than :math:`bF`.

**230.** If the equation

.. math::

   2\sin B \cdot (\sqrt{5} - 1) = \sqrt{5} + 1 - 2\sqrt{2} - 4\sin E \cdot \sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}

found above in § 228 be multiplied by :math:`\frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{4\sqrt{2}}`, we shall have

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   \sin E &= 2\frac{\sqrt{5} + 1}{4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} - \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} \\
   &\phantom{=} - 4\sin B \cdot \frac{(\sqrt{5} - 1)}{4} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}}{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} \\
   &= 2\sin 54^\circ \sin 67^\circ 30' \sin 45^\circ - \sin 67^\circ 30' - 4\sin B \cdot \sin 18^\circ \sin 67^\circ 30' \sin 45^\circ \\
   &= \sin 67^\circ 30'(\cos 9^\circ + \sin 9^\circ) - \sin 67^\circ 30' - 2\sin B \sin 67^\circ 30'(\cos 27^\circ - \sin 27^\circ) \\
   &= \frac{1}{2}(\sin 76^\circ 30' - \cos 76^\circ 30' + \sin 58^\circ 30' + \cos 58^\circ 30') - \sin 67^\circ 30' \\
   &\phantom{=} - \sin B \cdot (\sin 85^\circ 30' + \sin 40^\circ 30' - \cos 40^\circ 30' - \cos 85^\circ 30');
   \end{aligned}

whence we have finally

.. math::

   \begin{aligned}
   (12) \quad \sin E &= \frac{1}{2} \left( \begin{aligned}
   &\sin 76^\circ 30' - \cos 76^\circ 30' \\
   + &\sin 58^\circ 30' + \cos 58^\circ 30'
   \end{aligned} \right) - \sin 67^\circ 30' \\
   &\phantom{=} - \frac{1}{2} \left\{ \begin{aligned}
   &\cos(85^\circ 30' - B) + \sin(85^\circ 30' - B) \\
   - &\cos(85^\circ 30' + B) - \sin(85^\circ 30' + B) \\
   + &\cos(40^\circ 30' - B) + \sin(40^\circ 30' - B) \\
   - &\cos(40^\circ 30' + B) - \sin(40^\circ 30' + B)
   \end{aligned} \right\}
   \end{aligned}

Which equation has no limits.

**231.** If by chance any of the values found with these twelve equations should at first sight prove close

.. folio:: 0157
   :page: 132

to some useful value sought in the solution of problems, we shall have the advantage of reaching our aim by a very simple way.
For we shall have to employ no other points taken outside the circumference than the three alone so many times remarked, :math:`a`, :math:`b`, and :math:`e`, and on the circumference some of those that serve for the exact division of the circumference by means of the solutions of the problems of Book II. Let us therefore pass now to giving various examples of them.

**232.** And first we shall see how the circle may be divided in the ancient manner into degrees and minutes, without the error of seconds. For this purpose (Fig. 12) we shall suppose that the circumference of circle :math:`BDd` is divided into two hundred forty parts (§§ 57, 58), each of which, like :math:`P\delta`, contains :math:`1^\circ 30'`,
and that the positive numbering begins from :math:`B` toward :math:`F`, and a like negative numbering goes from :math:`B` toward :math:`f`, and let the points :math:`Z` and :math:`Z'` be arbitrary points, which have for the present no other condition than that they lie between :math:`B` and :math:`F` on the circumference; and likewise the points :math:`z` and :math:`z'` between :math:`B` and :math:`f`.
This we do to avoid too many figures, which would be needed if one were replicated for every problem. Moreover, the minuteness of the divisions would scarcely allow them to be seen even in figures much larger than Fig. 12.

**233. Problem.** To find the arc of an ancient degree, that is, of :math:`1^\circ`, without the error of half a second (Fig. 12).

*Sol. I.* Let the arc :math:`Bz = -55^\circ 30'` (§ 232). Take on the compass the distance :math:`bz`, and with center at :math:`e` mark an arc cutting the circumference at a point :math:`Z`. The arc :math:`BZ` will be :math:`52^\circ 59' \frac{1739}{1751}`, that is, of :math:`53^\circ`, lacking only twenty-five third-minutes. Then we have in the divisions from :math:`B` toward :math:`F` made by Problem § 42 the arc of :math:`54^\circ = \frac{18}{120}`. Thus we shall also have the arc :math:`54^\circ - 53^\circ = 1^\circ` with the desired approximation.

*Demonstration.* If in equation (12) we set :math:`B = -55^\circ 30'`, there results at the end of the calculation :math:`\sin E = 0.7986343 = \sin 52^\circ 59' \frac{1739}{1751}`.

*Sol. II.* Let the arc :math:`BZ = 10^\circ 30'`. With the distance :math:`bZ` taken as radius, and with center :math:`a`, describe an arc cutting the circumference at another point :math:`Z'`. The arc :math:`BZ'` will be :math:`= 29^\circ 29'`

.. folio:: 0158
   :page: 133

:math:`\frac{2511}{2532}`, that is :math:`= 29^\circ 30'` without the error of half a second.
Then in the divisions of § 58 one finds the arc of :math:`28^\circ 30' = \frac{19}{240}` of the circumference. Therefore we shall have the difference of the two arcs equal to :math:`1^\circ` with the desired approximation.

*Demonstration.* If in equation (7) we set :math:`B = 10^\circ 30'`, there will result at the end of the calculation :math:`\sin A = 0.4924215 = \sin 29^\circ 29' \frac{2511}{2532}`. The difference of the arcs will therefore fall short of the value of one degree by :math:`29'''`.

**234.** This second solution is less close than the first by 5 third-minutes, but the intersections of the arcs are made in this one at an angle closer to the right angle.

**235.** Having the arc of :math:`1^\circ 30'` (§ 226), and having found the arc of :math:`1^\circ` (§ 227), one will also have by subtraction the arc of :math:`30'`, or half a degree, and hence one will have a means of dividing the whole circumference into half-degrees without accumulating errors, and without any point being distant from its true place by half a second.
