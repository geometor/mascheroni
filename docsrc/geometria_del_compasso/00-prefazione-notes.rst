Commentary & Notes: Mohr, Mascheroni, Bonaparte & Fazzari
=========================================================

.. rubric:: Historical Lineage, Priority, and Reception of La Geometria del Compasso

1. Georg Mohr (1672) and the Priority of Euclides Danicus
---------------------------------------------------------

In 1672, the Danish mathematician :person:`Georg Mohr <mohr>` published *Euclides Danicus* (:cite:`mohr-1672`) in Dutch (*Euclides Danicus, Bestaende udi Too Deele*) at Amsterdam. In Part I of that work, Mohr systematically demonstrated that all 53 problems of Books I–VI of :person:`Euclid <euclid>`'s *Elements* can be constructed with the compass alone.

Because Mohr's booklet had an obscure Dutch/Danish printing and was cataloged among practical perspective works, it remained entirely unknown to eighteenth-century European scholars, including Mascheroni, Lagrange, Laplace, and Gauss. Only in 1928 did :person:`Johannes Hjelmslev <hjelmslev>` examine a copy found by a student in a Copenhagen antiquarian bookshop, establishing Mohr's complete 125-year priority. The theorem is therefore rightfully designated today as the **Mohr–Mascheroni Theorem**.

2. Mascheroni's Independent Creation and Methodological Superiority
-------------------------------------------------------------------

While Mohr preceded Mascheroni chronologically, :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>`'s *La Geometria del Compasso* (:cite:`mascheroni-1797`) stands as an independent, vastly more comprehensive constructive masterpiece:

* Mascheroni created a systematic synthetic calculus across twelve books, providing multiple constructive pathways for each problem (e.g., five distinct solutions for bisecting a segment in § 66).
* Mascheroni introduced the explicit representation of straight lines by point-pairs :math:`(A, B)`, formulating modern projective and metric coordinates without continuous curves.
* Through :person:`Antoine-Michel Carette <carette>`'s 1798 French translation (:cite:`carette-1798`), Mascheroni directly influenced nineteenth-century synthetic geometry, leading to :person:`Jakob Steiner <steiner>`'s 1833 dual fixed-circle straightedge geometry (:cite:`steiner-1833`) and :person:`August Adler <adler>`'s 1890 inversion theory (:cite:`adler-1890`).

3. Gaetano Fazzari and Émile Lemoine's Géométrographie
------------------------------------------------------

In his 1901 centenary Palermo edition (:cite:`fazzari-1901`), :person:`Gaetano Fazzari <fazzari>` brought Mascheroni into direct dialogue with the quantitative science of :person:`Émile Lemoine <lemoine>`'s *Géométrographie* (:cite:`lemoine-1902`).

Fazzari observed that critics who had labeled compass-only geometry as a mere "mathematical curiosity" overlooked its genuine operational economy: by dispensing with straightedge alignment operations (:math:`R_1, R_2`), many Mascheroni constructions actually achieve lower simplicity :math:`S` and superior drafting exactitude :math:`E` than standard ruler-and-compass procedures taught in Euclidean schools.
