**118. Problem.** To bisect the angle :math:`BAC` (fig. 54) given only by the three points :math:`B, A, C` in which :math:`A` is unequally distant from :math:`B` and from :math:`C`.

*Solution.* With center :math:`A` and radius :math:`AB` let arc :math:`BMD` be described. Make :math:`CB = CD`. Bisect arc :math:`BMD` at :math:`M` (§ 60). Bisect arc :math:`BM` at :math:`N`. Then angle :math:`BAN` will be half of angle :math:`BAC`.

*Demonstration.* Since angle :math:`BAD` is double :math:`BAC` (§ 116) and likewise double :math:`BAM` (VI.33), angle :math:`BAC` will be the same as :math:`BAM`. But angle :math:`BAN` is half of :math:`BAM`. Therefore, etc.

**119. Problem.** Given the arc :math:`BC` (fig. 55) described with center :math:`A`, to find its sine, cosine, tangent, and secant.

*Solution.* On the circumference described with radius :math:`AB`, make :math:`BC = Bc`. Bisect :math:`Cc` at :math:`M` (§ 66). Then :math:`CM` will be the sine, and :math:`MA` the cosine.

With center :math:`M` and radius :math:`MA` describe an arc cutting the circumference, if it can, at :math:`D` and :math:`d`. Determine the semicircumference :math:`dD\delta` (§ 64). By the same § add to :math:`BA` its equal :math:`BV`.
With centers :math:`A` and :math:`V`, and radius :math:`D\delta`, describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`S`. Then :math:`BS` will be the tangent, and :math:`SA` the secant.

*Demonstration.* Line :math:`BA` cuts :math:`Cc` at right angles at its midpoint :math:`M` (§ 14). Hence :math:`CM` is the sine and :math:`MA` is the cosine of arc :math:`BC`. Line :math:`SB` is perpendicular to :math:`AB` (§ 83).
Line :math:`D\delta` is the third proportional to :math:`AM, AC` (§ 87); hence also its equal :math:`AS`. Therefore in the two right triangles :math:`AMC, ABS` we have the proportion :math:`AM : AC :: AB : AS`, and by inversion (V.4) :math:`AC : AM :: AS : AB`. Hence (V.35) :math:`(AC)^2 : (AM)^2 :: (AS)^2 : (AB)^2`. Substituting for :math:`(AC)^2` and :math:`(AS)^2` their values drawn from I.47, we shall have :math:`(AM)^2 + (MC)^2 : (AM)^2 :: (AB)^2 + (BS)^2 : (AB)^2`.

   :alt: Archival Fig. 55 (folio 0090, p. 65): Constructing sine, cosine, tangent, and secant of arc BC (§ 119)

Hence (V.17) :math:`(MC)^2 : (AM)^2 :: (BS)^2 : (AB)^2`. Hence (V.34) :math:`MC : AM :: BS : AB`. Therefore the sides :math:`MC, BS` are also proportional. We shall therefore have angle :math:`MCA` the same as :math:`BSA` (VI.5). Hence the points :math:`A, C, S` will lie on the same straight line, and :math:`BS` will be the tangent and :math:`SA` the secant of arc :math:`BC`.

If the circle described with center :math:`M` and radius :math:`MA` should not cut the circumference, or should cut it at too acute angles, one should have recourse to § 89 or to §§ 90 and 91.

*Another Solution for finding the tangent and the secant.* Determine the semicircumference :math:`BCE` (fig. 56) (§ 64). With radius :math:`BC` and center :math:`E`, cut this at :math:`Q`.
With radius :math:`CQ` and centers :math:`A` and :math:`B`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`V`. With the same radius :math:`CQ` and center :math:`V`, cut the circumference at :math:`e`. With radius :math:`Ee` and centers :math:`A` and :math:`B`, mark two arcs intersecting at :math:`m`. With the same radius :math:`Ee` and center :math:`m`, describe the semicircumference :math:`ABS` (§ 64). Then :math:`SB` will be the tangent, and :math:`SA` the secant.

.. picture:: 0091.g1.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 56 (folio 0091, p. 66): Finding an arc from its given sine (§ 120)

*Demonstration.* Let the arc :math:`BCF` be equal to the quadrant :math:`= FE`; since :math:`BC = QE`, we shall also have :math:`CF = FQ`. Moreover, by the trigonometric definitions, the sine of arc :math:`CF` will be the same thing as the cosine of arc :math:`BC`.
Then the chord of arc :math:`CFQ`, double arc :math:`CF`, will be double the sine of arc :math:`CF`, that is, of the cosine of arc :math:`BC`. This chord is :math:`CQ = AV`. We also have (§ 22) :math:`AV \cdot Ee = (AB)^2`. Since the straight line :math:`AmS` (§ 64) :math:`= 2\,Ee` and :math:`AV = 2\cos BC`, we shall have :math:`2\,Ee \cdot \cos BC = (AB)^2 = AS \cdot \cos BC`.
Hence :math:`\cos BC : AB :: AB : AS` (VI.17). Therefore :math:`AS` will be the third proportional to the cosine and the radius; and hence it will equal the secant, according to the trigonometric demonstrations. Moreover angle :math:`ABS` is right (III.31). Therefore :math:`AS` will be the secant in its position, and hence :math:`BS` the tangent.

**121. Problem.** Given the cosine :math:`ma` (fig. 57) of an arc of a given radius :math:`AB`, to find this arc.

*Solution.* Having described the circle :math:`CcK` with radius :math:`AB`, find a double of :math:`ma` (§ 64). With center at some point :math:`c` of the circumference and this double taken as radius, cut the circumference at :math:`K`. Determine the semicircumference :math:`KcC` (§ 64). Bisect arc :math:`Cc` at :math:`B` (§ 60). Then :math:`BC` will be the arc sought.

*Demonstration.* Let the other chord :math:`Cc` be drawn. It will be bisected at right angles at :math:`M` by radius :math:`AB` (§ 14). But angle :math:`CcK` is also right (III.31), being in the semicircle :math:`CcK`. Therefore the two triangles :math:`CMA, CcK`, having a common angle at :math:`C` and the other right, are equiangular to one another (I.32),
and we have (VI.4) :math:`CM : Cc :: MA : cK`. But :math:`CM` is half of :math:`Cc`. Hence :math:`MA = \frac{1}{2} cK = ma`. Now :math:`MA` is the cosine of arc :math:`BC`. Therefore, etc.

**122. Problem.** Given the tangent :math:`sb` of an arc of a given radius :math:`AB` (fig. 55), to find this arc.

*Solution.* Having described the circle :math:`BC\delta` with radius :math:`AB`, erect at :math:`B` perpendicular to :math:`AB` the line :math:`BS = bs` (§ 76). Find the point :math:`C` where :math:`SA` cuts the circumference (§ 111). Then :math:`BC` will be the arc sought.

This Solution needs no demonstration.

**123. Problem.** Given the secant :math:`sa` of an arc of a given radius :math:`AB` (fig. 55), to find this arc.

*Solution.* Having described the circumference :math:`BC\delta` with radius :math:`AB`, and laid off on :math:`AB` its equal :math:`BV` (§ 64), with centers :math:`A` and :math:`V` and radius :math:`sa` describe two arcs intersecting at :math:`S`. Find the point :math:`C` where :math:`SA` cuts the circumference (§ 111). Then :math:`BC` will be the arc sought.

*Demonstration.* Line :math:`SB` will be perpendicular to :math:`BA` (§ 83); hence it will be the tangent of arc :math:`BC`, and therefore :math:`SA` the secant.
