§§ 86–92: Third Proportional & Iterative Doubling
=================================================

.. folio:: 0071
   :page: 46

**BOOK FIVE**

**OF PROPORTIONAL DISTANCES**

**86.** *Prob.* To find a third proportional to the two distances :math:`Qp, MN` (fig. 35), of which the first :math:`Qp` is greater than the second :math:`MN`.

.. picture:: 0071.g2.svg
   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Mascheroni Book V § 86 (Fig. 35): Third Proportional (Case 1)

.. picture:: 0071.g2.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 35 (folio 0071, p. 46): Third proportional when first term exceeds half the second (§ 86)

*Sol.* With center :math:`Q` and radius :math:`Qp`, describe an indefinite arc :math:`ApB`. With center :math:`p` and radius :math:`MN`, describe the semicircumference :math:`BAS`. Then :math:`AS` will be the third proportional.

*Dem.* By the Lemma of § 22, :math:`AS \cdot pQ = (Ap)^2`. Therefore :math:`AS \cdot pQ = (MN)^2`. Hence (VI.17) :math:`pQ : MN :: MN : AS`.

**87.** To find a third proportional to the two distances :math:`Qp, MN` (fig. 36), of which the first is less than the second, yet greater than half of it.

*Obs.* We shall perceive that :math:`Qp` is greater than half of :math:`MN` if the two circles described with centers :math:`Q` and :math:`p` (which are the endpoints of the first distance) and with radii :math:`Qp` and :math:`MN` (which are the two given distances) intersect one another as in the figure.

**88.** If the circle :math:`pbQ'` described with center :math:`Q` and radius :math:`Qp` should not intersect at any point with the circle described with center :math:`p` and radius :math:`MN`, as in fig. 37, the following problem will serve.

**89.** *Prob.* To find a third proportional to the two distances (fig. 37) :math:`Qp, MN`, of which the first is less than half the second.

.. picture:: 0072.g2.svg
   :alt: Calibrated 5-layer vector diagram of Mascheroni Book V § 88 (Fig. 37): Repeated Third Proportional

.. picture:: 0072.g2.png
   :alt: Archival Fig. 37 (folio 0072, p. 47): Third proportional by doubling Q to Q' (§ 89)

*Sol.* With center :math:`p` and radius :math:`MN`, describe an indefinite arc :math:`BAS`. With center :math:`Q` and radius :math:`Qp`, describe the semicircumference :math:`pbQ'` (§ 64). With center :math:`Q'` and radius :math:`Q'p`, describe an indefinite arc.
If this arc cuts the arc :math:`BAS` at two points :math:`B` and :math:`A`, determine the semicircumference :math:`BAS'` (§ 64). By the method of the same § 64, add to :math:`AS'` in a straight line an equal :math:`S'S`. Then :math:`AS` will be the required third proportional.

*Dem.* We have (§ 22) :math:`AS' \cdot pQ' = (Ap)^2 = (MN)^2`. But :math:`pQ' = 2pQ`. Therefore :math:`2AS' \cdot pQ = (MN)^2`; that is, :math:`AS \cdot pQ = (MN)^2`. Hence (I.17) :math:`pQ : MN :: MN : AS`.

**90.** If not even the arc :math:`pcQ''` described with center :math:`Q'` (fig. 38) and radius :math:`Q'p` should cut the arc :math:`BAS` described with center :math:`p` and radius :math:`MN`, determine the semicircumference :math:`pcQ''`; with center :math:`Q''` and radius

.. folio:: 0073
   :page: 48

:math:`Q''p` describe an indefinite arc. If this cuts the arc :math:`BAS` at two points :math:`A` and :math:`B`, determine the semicircumference :math:`BAS''` (§ 64). Quadruple :math:`AS''` (§ 65), and let :math:`AS = 4AS''`. This will be the required third proportional.

*Dem.* Because we have (§ 22) :math:`AS' \cdot pQ'' = (Ap)^2 = (MN)^2`. Hence :math:`4AS' \cdot pQ = (MN)^2 = AS \cdot pQ`. Hence (VI.17) :math:`pQ : MN :: MN : AS`.

**91.** In the same manner we would proceed further if not even the distance :math:`Q''p` were greater than half of :math:`MN`; namely, we would take a distance double that, and octuple of :math:`Qp`, and octuple the :math:`AS''` that would be determined thereby.
This octuple distance of :math:`AS'` would be the required third proportional; and so on.

The demonstration is like the preceding ones.

**92.** Even in the case where the first distance :math:`Qp` should indeed be greater than half the second :math:`MN`, but by a little, it will be advantageous to double it, so that the intersections of the two circles may be made at angles not so acute, but closer to a right angle (§ 9).
