=============================================
§§ 15–22: Isosceles & Chord Metric Identities
=============================================

.. folio:: 0044
   :page: 15

**15.** *Cor.* Therefore :math:`(QM)^2 = (AQ)^2 - (AM)^2` (I.47).

**16.** *Lemma.* The same things remaining as in § 13, :math:`(AQ)^2 = (AP)^2 + (PQ)^2 + Pp \cdot PQ`.

*Dem.* Since :math:`(AQ)^2 = (AP)^2 + (PQ)^2 + 2MP \cdot PQ` (II.12). But :math:`2MP = Pp` (§ 14). Therefore etc.

**17.** *Lemma.* Likewise :math:`(AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + (pQ)^2 - pP \cdot pQ`.

*Dem.* Since :math:`(AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + (pQ)^2 - 2pM \cdot pQ` (II.13). But :math:`2pM = pP` (§ 14). Therefore etc.

**18.** *Cor. I.* Since :math:`pQ = pP + PQ`, :math:`(pQ)^2 = pP \cdot pQ + PQ \cdot pQ` (II.2);
hence subtracting :math:`pP \cdot pQ` we have :math:`(pQ)^2 - pP \cdot pQ = PQ \cdot pQ`. And substituting this value into the value of :math:`(AQ)^2` of § 17, we shall have :math:`(AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + pQ \cdot PQ`. Whence subtracting :math:`(Ap)^2` on both sides, there arises :math:`(AQ)^2 - (Ap)^2 = pQ \cdot PQ`.
If now we perform the multiplication of :math:`AQ + Ap` by :math:`AQ - Ap`, we shall find :math:`(AQ + Ap)(AQ - Ap) = (AQ)^2 - (Ap)^2`. Hence we shall have :math:`(AQ + Ap)(AQ - Ap) = pQ \cdot PQ`. Whence by VI.16 is deduced the analogy

.. math::

   pQ : AQ + Ap :: AQ - Ap : PQ,

or substituting :math:`AP` in place of :math:`Ap`, and inverting alternately,

.. math::

   PQ : AQ + AP :: AQ - AP : pQ

By these two analogies is expressed the celebrated

*Theor.* In any triangle, any side is to the sum of the other two as their difference is to the difference or to the sum of the segments which the perpendicular drawn from the opposite angle makes upon that side, according as it falls inside or outside the triangle.

**19.** *Cor. II.* If :math:`AQ = pQ`; taking away on both sides the two

.. folio:: 0045
   :page: 16

equal terms :math:`(AQ)^2`, :math:`(pQ)^2`, and adding on both sides :math:`pP \cdot pQ`, there will result :math:`pP \cdot pQ = (Ap)^2`.

**20.** *Lemma.* The same things remaining (§ 13), if angle :math:`RpQ` be right; let then angle :math:`RpS = RpA`, and :math:`pS = pR = pA`; :math:`AS` will be parallel and equal to :math:`Pp`, and :math:`(AQ)^2 = (RQ)^2 - AS \cdot pQ`.

   :alt: Archival Fig. 3 (folio 0045, p. 16): Collinear cevian and Stewart-type distance identity

*Dem.* Since if from the two right angles :math:`RpQ`, :math:`Rpq` (fig. 4) we subtract the two equals :math:`RpA`, :math:`RpS`, the angles :math:`ApP`, :math:`Spq` will remain equal. But :math:`ApP = APp` (I.5). Hence :math:`Spq = APq`. Therefore :math:`AP`, :math:`Sp` are parallel (I.29).
But they are also equal by construction. Therefore the two :math:`AS`, :math:`Pp` are equal and parallel (I.33).

We have moreover :math:`(RQ)^2 = (Rp)^2 + (pQ)^2` (I.47) :math:`= (Ap)^2 + (pQ)^2`. And by Lemma § 17, :math:`(AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + (pQ)^2 - pP \cdot pQ`. Therefore :math:`(AQ)^2 = (RQ)^2 - pP \cdot pQ`, or :math:`= (RQ)^2 - AS \cdot pQ`.

**21.** *Lemma.* The same things remaining (§§ 13 and 20), :math:`(SQ)^2 = (RQ)^2 + AS \cdot pQ` (fig. 4).

*Dem.* Since if we make :math:`ST = Sp`; :math:`pT = pP` (§ 1); in the two triangles :math:`SpT`, :math:`APp` the angles :math:`SpT`, :math:`APp` will be found equal to each other (I.8); and hence :math:`PpT` a straight line (I.27).
Then :math:`(SQ)^2 = (pS)^2 + (pQ)^2 + pQ \cdot pT` (§ 16). But :math:`(pS)^2 + (pQ)^2 = (pR)^2 + (pQ)^2 = (RQ)^2`; and :math:`pT = pP = AS`. Therefore :math:`(SQ)^2 = (RQ)^2 + AS \cdot pQ`.

From the two preceding Lemmas it follows as a Corollary that :math:`(AQ)^2 + (SQ)^2 = 2(RQ)^2`.

**22.** *Lemma.* If :math:`AQ = pQ = BQ` (fig. 4); and :math:`Ap = pB = pS`, :math:`pS` being on the continuation of :math:`Bp`; then :math:`AS \cdot pQ = (Ap)^2`.

*Dem.* Since the isosceles triangles :math:`AQp`, :math:`BQp` have their sides equal to one another, angle :math:`QpA = QpB` (I.8).
Moreover angle :math:`ApB`, which is the sum of the two, is also equal to the sum of the two angles :math:`SAp`, :math:`ASp` (I.32), which being equal

.. folio:: 0046
   :page: 17

to one another because triangle :math:`ApS` is isosceles (I.5), each of them will be equal to angle :math:`ApQ = pAQ` (I.5).
Therefore triangle :math:`pAS` is similar to triangle :math:`QpA` (I.32, VI.4); and hence :math:`pQ : Ap :: Ap : AS`, and :math:`AS \cdot pQ = (Ap)^2` (VI.17).
