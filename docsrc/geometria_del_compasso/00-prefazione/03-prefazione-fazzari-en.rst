============================================
Historical Preface by Gaetano Fazzari (1901)
============================================

.. rubric:: Gaetano Fazzari (*The Geometry of the Compass*, Palermo 1901, pp. iii–xvi)

.. folio:: 0016

On the first centenary of the death of :person:`Lorenzo Mascheroni <mascheroni>`, the distinguished mathematician and historian of science :person:`Gaetano Fazzari <fazzari>`, founder of the mathematical journal *Il Pitagora*, published in Palermo a centenary edition of *La Geometria del Compasso*.

From Fazzari's celebrated *Historical Preface* (:cite:`fazzari-1901`, pp. xi–xii):

.. epigraph::

   « If his demonstrations, with their continuous invocation of the propositions of Euclid—which was then the sole elementary text of geometry—seem at times cumbersome and might today be simplified, his constructions remain constantly elegant, and are often simpler than those ordinarily executed using both straightedge and compass. Of this the reader can easily persuade himself by employing, for example, the method of the Géométrographie of :person:`Lemoine <lemoine>` to determine the simplicity and exactitude of a geometric construction. »

And further on the theoretical significance of Mascheroni's treatise (p. xii):

.. epigraph::

   « Mascheroni was not content merely to show that every construction problem of elementary geometry could be solved with the compass alone; he provided general, fertile methods for transforming straightedge-and-compass constructions into constructions by the compass alone. Hence his treatise possesses not merely a historical interest, but constitutes an organic and vital manual that may still be studied with profit by anyone desiring to master the elegance of pure geometric constructions. »

Fazzari chronicles Mascheroni's life: from his professorship at Pavia to his delegation to Paris in 1798 on the international commission for the establishment of the metric system (*Commission des poids et mesures*), and his sudden death in Paris in 1800.
