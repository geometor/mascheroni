Notes: Dedication to Bonaparte
==============================

.. rubric:: Philological and Historical Apparatus

.. folio:: 0030

Critical Notes on the Text
--------------------------

1. **Title and Epithet:** The title *A Bonaparte l'Italico* reflects the Cisalpine political environment of 1797. Mascheroni addresses Bonaparte not merely as a French general, but as the liberator of Italy (*liberar tua cara Italia*), reflecting the contemporary enthusiasm of northern Italian intellectuals before the disillusionment of Campoformio.
2. **"Vienna intimò pace":** Reference to the Preliminary Peace of Leoben (18 April 1797), signed by Bonaparte on behalf of the French Republic within sight of Vienna, which brought the War of the First Coalition to an end.
3. **"Curvo giro del fedel compasso":** Mascheroni's poetic personification of the instrument. In § 8 of the text, he explicitly refers to fixed-radius dividers as *compassi fedeli* ("faithful compasses") because they hold their exact span without slipping during multi-step operational chains.
4. **"Annibal novo":** Allusion to Bonaparte's crossing of the Alps (Col de Larche and Cadibona Pass in April 1796), comparing his tactical speed to Hannibal's classical march into Italy.

The Paris Academy Reception
---------------------------

The presentation of Mascheroni's treatise to the *Institut de France* in December 1797 is recorded in the memoirs of Bourrienne and the minutes of the Academy. Upon presenting the problems to Laplace and Lagrange, Laplace is famously reported to have replied:

.. epigraph::

   « Nous attendions tout de vous, Général, excepté des leçons de géométrie. »
   (*We expected everything from you, General, except lessons in geometry.*)

Lagrange subsequently observed that Mascheroni's methods demonstrated an unexpected economy of thought, replacing straightedge constructions with circular symmetries of higher intrinsic symmetry.
