§§ 146–149: Inscribing & Circumscribing Circles to Polygons¶

146. Problem. To circumscribe and inscribe a circle to a given equilateral triangle (fig. 72).

Solution. Let the vertices of the given triangle be \(A\), \(B\), and \(M\). With radius \(AM\) and center \(A\), mark the arc \(MDE\), and make \(AM = MD = DE\). With radius \(BD\) and centers \(B\) and \(A\), mark two arcs intersecting at \(L\). With the same radius \(LA\) and center \(L\), cut the arc \(DE\) at \(Q\). With radius \(QE\) and two vertices of the triangle, for example \(A\) and \(B\), taken as centers, mark two arcs intersecting at \(O\). With the same radius \(OA\) and center \(O\), describe a circle. It will be circumscribed to the triangle.

Bisect \(QE\) at \(m\) (§ 66). With radius \(Qm\) and the same center \(O\), describe another circle. It will be inscribed in the triangle.

Demonstration. From the demonstration of § 143 it follows that \(QE\) is also the third proportional to \(EM\), \(EA\), since \(BAE\) is also a straight line. Hence \(QE\) will be the radius of a circle passing through the three points \(M\), \(A\), \(B\); and \(O\) will be the center (§ 144).

In figure 59, wherein the circle \(DBd\) is inscribed in triangle \(NLM\), since \(AD\) is perpendicular to \(LD\) and \(NL\) to \(LB\) (§ 131), the right triangles \(NDA\), \(NBL\), having a common angle at \(N\), also have the third angle equal (32, Book I); hence we have the proportion (4, Book VI) \(NL : LB :: NA : DA\). But \(NL\) is double \(LB\); therefore \(NA\) is also double \(DA\), that is, the radius of the circle circumscribed to the equilateral triangle is double the radius of the inscribed circle. Hence, etc.

147. Problem. To circumscribe and inscribe a circle to a given square (fig. 73).

Solution. Let the four given vertices of the square be \(A\), \(B\), \(T\), \(P\). Make \(AB = AE\), and \(PB = PE\). With the same radius \(BP\) and center \(B\), mark an arc passing through \(Q\) and \(q\). With center \(E\) and radius \(EA\), cut this arc at \(Q\) and \(q\). With the same radius \(AE\) and centers \(Q\) and \(q\), mark two arcs intersecting at \(M\). With radius \(AQ\) and centers \(A\) and \(B\), mark two arcs intersecting at \(O\). With the same radius \(OB\) and center \(O\), describe a circle. It will be circumscribed to the square. With the same center \(O\) and radius \(OM\), describe another circle. It will be inscribed in the square.

Demonstration. If for brevity we suppose \(AB = 1\), we shall have \(AQ = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2}\) (§ 104) \(= AO = BO\). Therefore \((AO)^2 + (BO)^2 = 1 = (AB)^2\). Hence angle \(BOA\) will be right (48, Book I), and angles \(OAB\), \(OBA\) half-right (5 and 32, Book I). Since angle \(BAP\) of the square is right, angle \(OAP\) will be half-right. Thus in the two triangles \(BAO\), \(PAO\) we shall have an equal angle contained between equal sides. Hence \(OB = OP\) (4, Book I). In the same manner \(OB = OT\) is demonstrated. Therefore circle \(ABTP\) is circumscribed to the square.

\(OM\) is perpendicular to \(MA\) (§ 83).

Hence \(BA\) will be tangent to the circle described with radius \(OM\). The same is shown for the other sides \(AP\), \(PT\), \(TB\). Thus the circle described with radius \(OM\) and center \(O\) is inscribed in the square.

148. Problem. To circumscribe and inscribe a circle to any regular polygon (fig. 74).

Let \(B\), \(A\), \(M\) be three vertices of this regular polygon, of which the middle one \(A\) is equally distant from the other two \(B\) and \(M\). Taking center at \(A\), with radius \(AB\), describe the semicircle \(BCDE\) (§ 64). With radius \(ME\) and centers \(A\) and \(E\), mark two arcs intersecting at \(L\). With center \(L\) and the same radius \(LA\), cut the semicircle \(BCDE\) at \(Q\). \(BQ\) will be the radius of the circumscribed circle. Therefore, taking as centers two vertices of the polygon, for instance \(B\) and \(A\), and with radius \(BQ\) marking two arcs intersecting at \(O\), \(O\) will be the center.

If \(AB\) is a side of the polygon, bisect it at \(T\) (§ 66). \(OT\) will be the radius of the inscribed circle, which is described with the same center \(O\). If any other \(Ab\) were one of the sides of the polygon, bisect \(Ab\) at \(t\). \(Ot\) will be the radius of the circle to be inscribed with the same center.

Demonstration. That \(O\) is the center of the circle passing through the three points \(B\), \(A\), \(M\) follows from the demonstration of § 143. Therefore it is the center of the circumscribed circle, since through the three points \(B\), \(A\), \(M\) only one circle can be drawn (25, Book III).

Moreover \(OT\) is perpendicular to \(TA\) (§ 83). Hence \(TA\) will be tangent to the circle described with radius \(OT\) and center \(O\) (16, Book I). The same is shown for all the other sides equal to \(AB\). Therefore this circle is inscribed in the polygon whose side is \(AB\). In like manner it is demonstrated that the circle described with radius \(Ot\) and center \(O\) is inscribed in the polygon whose side is \(Ab\).

149. If \(AB\) is the side of the polygon in which it is desired to inscribe the circle, there will be many ways of bisecting it (§ 66). For the square we have assigned the simplest (§ 147). For the triangle alone (§ 146) the radius of the inscribed circle is half the radius of the circumscribed circle. In the square alone it is equal to half the side.
