Commentary & Notes: Libro III — The Architectural Core of Mascheroni’s Theorem¶

Why Midpoint Bisection is the Gateway to Compass Completeness

1. Bisection Without Lines as the First Major Obstacle¶

In classical geometry, bisecting a line segment \(AB\) (euclid-heath, Book I, Proposition 10) appears trivial: describe two equal circles \(A(AB)\) and \(B(BA)\) cutting at \(C\) and \(D\), and draw the straight line \(CD\) cutting \(AB\) at its midpoint \(M\).

In compass-only geometry, however, the line \(CD\) cannot be drawn, and \(AB\) itself is not drawn on paper. Finding the exact point \(M\) requires constructing the intersection of two circles that meet precisely at the midpoint on the invisible line between \(A\) and \(B\).

2. The Genius of Solution V¶

Mascheroni presented four alternative solutions in § 66 before stating Solution V:

  • Solutions I–IV use auxiliary equilateral triangles and perpendicular projections, requiring between 9 and 15 circles.

  • Solution V achieves the bisection in just 7 circles (and only 5 distinct radii: \(AB, 2AB\)), taking advantage of the collinear doubling point \(E\).

  • Because \(E, A, B\) are collinear, the symmetry across the line of centers guarantees that the intersecting arcs from \(P\) and \(p\) must close symmetrically upon the line \(AB\).

  • As Gaetano Fazzari emphasized, Solution V demonstrates that collinear points can be manipulated and generated with supreme mathematical rigor using circular metrics alone.
