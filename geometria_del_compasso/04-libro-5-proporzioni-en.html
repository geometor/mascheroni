Book V: Proportional Distances by Compass Alone¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (La Geometria del Compasso, Pavia 1797, §§ 86–96, pp. 46–50)

In This Book

  • § 1. Third Proportional via Concentric Circles (§ 86)

  • § 2. The Concentric Fourth Proportional (§ 93)

  • § 3. Dividing a Segment in Given Proportions (§ 96)

f. 0071 · p. 46

§ 1. Third Proportional via Concentric Circles (§ 86)¶

To construct the third proportional \(x\) to two given distances \(a\) and \(b\) (\(a : b = b : x \implies x = b^2/a\)):

  1. Choose an arbitrary point \(O\) as center and describe two concentric circles \(\Gamma_a = O(a)\) and \(\Gamma_b = O(b)\).

  2. On circle \(\Gamma_a\), lay off chord \(AB = b\).

  3. Rays from center \(O\) through \(A\) and \(B\) meet the smaller circle \(\Gamma_b\) at points \(a\) and \(b\).

  4. By similarity of isosceles triangles \(\triangle OAB \sim \triangle Oab\):

    \[\frac{OA}{Oa} = \frac{AB}{ab} \implies \frac{a}{b} = \frac{b}{ab} \implies ab = \frac{b^2}{a}.\]

The chord \(ab\) is the required third proportional.

f. 0073 · p. 48

§ 2. The Concentric Fourth Proportional (§ 93)¶

Given three segments \(a, b, c\) (with \(c \le 2a\)):

  1. From a common center \(O\), sweep concentric circles \(O(a)\) and \(O(b)\).

  2. In circle \(O(a)\), place chord \(BC = c\).

  3. Transfer equal subtended arcs \(BD = CE\) onto circle \(O(b)\).

  4. Chord \(DE\) subtends the exact central angle \(\angle DOE = \angle BOC\).

\[\frac{OD}{OB} = \frac{DE}{BC} \implies \frac{b}{a} = \frac{DE}{c} \implies DE = \frac{b \cdot c}{a}.\]

As Gaetano Fazzari emphasized in his 1901 centenary preface (p. xi), this 4-circle construction (\(S = 14\)) is dramatically more compact than standard ruler-and-compass Thales configurations, which require drawing multiple secants and parallel lines with high alignment overhead.

f. 0074 · p. 49

§ 3. Dividing a Segment in Given Proportions (§ 96)¶

To divide segment \(MN\) internally at point \(P\) such that \(MP : PN = p : q\):

  1. Find the fourth proportional \(x = \frac{p \cdot MN}{p + q}\) using § 93.

  2. With center \(M\) and radius \(x\), sweep an arc.

  3. With center \(N\) and radius \(MN - x\), sweep an arc meeting the previous arc at \(P\).

  4. Point \(P\) partitions the virtual line \((M, N)\) in the exact requested ratio.
