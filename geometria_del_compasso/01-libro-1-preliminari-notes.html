Commentary & Notes: Libro I — The Epistemology of Instrument Redundancy¶

The Foundational Shift from Continuous Lines to Point-Pairs

1. Why Euclid Postulated the Straightedge¶

In classical antiquity, Euclid grounded geometry in the physical mechanics of the drafting table: drawing an actual graphite or ink line along a straightedge creates an immediate visual representation of continuous linear space.

However, as Lorenzo Mascheroni recognized, the straight line in synthetic proofs functions almost exclusively as an incidence carrier between points:

  • We draw a line \(AB\) to determine its intersection with another line \(CD\), or to project a perpendicular from \(P\).

  • The physical continuum of points along the line segment between \(A\) and \(B\) is rarely used; only specific landmark points (midpoints, harmonic conjugates, section cuts) enter the chain of demonstration.

  • By demonstrating that all such target points can be located by circular intersections alone, Mascheroni revealed that continuous straight lines are logically redundant for constructive plane geometry.

2. Physical Precision: The Advantage of the Fixed Compass¶

Draftsmen and instrument makers in eighteenth-century Pavia and Milan had long known that straightedges introduce unavoidable mechanical errors:

  • Any ruler edge has microscopic warp, bevel irregularities, and parallex errors when guiding a drawing pen.

  • A well-machined steel compass with a locking screw maintains a fixed radius with exceptional mechanical stability.

  • In Lemoine’s exactitude metric \(E = m_1 + m_2\), compass arcs avoid the double alignment \(2R_1\) required each time a straightedge must be brought into simultaneous contact with two points on paper.
