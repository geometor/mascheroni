Book I: Preliminaries¶

Libro Primo: Preliminare (§§ 1–26)

Foundations of compass-only geometry (§§ 1–26, pp. 11–18, folios 0040–0047): equilateral circle intersections, point-pair line representation, orthogonal symmetry lemmas, isosceles metric equations, and parallelogram / Apollonius median identities.

§§ 1–5: Principles & Fundamental Compass Operations

§ 1. Lorenzo Mascheroni establishes the sole constructive primitive of the treatise: given two points A and E, the compass sweeps circles AE and EA of radius AE, intersecting at D and d so that . Every straight line in the treatise is treated purely as a virtual line determined by two constructed points—never drawn by a ruler.

§§ 6–14: Compass Notation & Symmetry Lemmas

§§ 6–10. Mascheroni introduces compact verbal notation for compass sweeps («col centro A, raggio AB si tagli l’arco in C», «si faccia ad AB = BC = CD») and establishes how circles of distinct apertures transfer distances without disturbing previously marked loci.

§§ 15–22: Isosceles & Chord Metric Identities

§§ 15–20 (Fig. 3). Mascheroni derives exact quadratic distance identities for a point \(Q\) collinear with the base \(pP\) of an isosceles triangle \(ApP\): \((AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 + pQ \cdot PQ\) (external) and \((AQ)^2 = (Ap)^2 - pQ \cdot pP\) (internal). These algebraic identities—direct compass-age counterparts of Stewart’s theorem—allow Mascheroni to compute exact radical lengths such as \(\sqrt{7}\) in Book VI (§ 100) and exact trig-chord equations in Book XI.

§§ 23–26: Parallelograms & Apollonius’s Theorem

§§ 23–25 (Figs. 5–6). Mascheroni establishes the metric laws of symmetric trapezoids (\(\pi\tau \cdot BD = (BD)^2 - (B\pi)^2\), § 23) and parallelograms (sum of squares of the diagonals equals sum of squares of the four sides, § 25).
