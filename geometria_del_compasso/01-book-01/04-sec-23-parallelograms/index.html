§§ 23–26: Parallelograms & Apollonius’s Theorem¶

23. Lemma. If \(AB = AC = BD\), and \(AD = BC\); then (fig. 5) \(DC \cdot AB = (AB)^2 - (AD)^2\).

Dem. The two triangles \(ADB\), \(ACB\), having their sides respectively equal, will be equal (I.8 and 26). Being moreover both placed upon the same base \(AB\), they will lie between the same parallels \(DC\), \(AB\) (I.39). If therefore upon \(BA\) we take \(BE = DC\), \(DE\) will be equal and parallel to \(BC\) (I.33), and equal also to \(DA\). Hence the two isosceles triangles \(BDA\), \(DAE\), which have a common angle at \(A\), will be similar (I.5 and 32, and VI.4), and \(AB : AD :: AD : AE\); therefore \(AB \cdot AE = (AD)^2\) (VI.17). We have moreover \(AB \cdot AE + AB \cdot BE = (AB)^2\) (II.2). Hence substituting \((AD)^2\) for \(AB \cdot AE\), and \(DC\) for \(BE\), we have \((AD)^2 + AB \cdot DC = (AB)^2\). And subtracting \((AD)^2\), we shall have \(DC \cdot AB = (AB)^2 - (AD)^2\).

24. Lemma. If in the circle \(B\mu G\) with radius \(AB\) we erect at the center \(A\) the perpendicular \(Ae\) equal to the chord \(BG\) of the arc \(B\mu G\); and with center \(e\) and radius \(AB\) we describe an arc cutting the circumference at \(\mu\); the arc \(B\mu\) will be equal to half of the arc \(B\mu G\).

Dem. By the equality of the sides of the two triangles \(ABG\), \(A\mu e\) (fig. 6), angle \(GAB = A\mu e\) (I.8). Let angle \(A\mu e\) be bisected by the straight line \(\mu M\); since triangle \(\mu eA\) is isosceles, angle \(\mu eM = \mu AM\); hence in the two triangles \(\mu eM\), \(\mu AM\), the other two angles being equal to each other, \(\mu Me = \mu MA\) also (I.32, Coroll.); therefore \(\mu M\) is perpendicular to \(Ae\) (I.13), and hence parallel to \(AB\) (I.29), and angle \(M\mu A = \mu AB\) (I.27); therefore \(\mu AB\) will be half of angle \(GAB\), and hence also arc \(B\mu\) half of arc \(B\mu G\).

25. If in the parallelogram \(ABMN\) the diagonal \(MA\) be equal to the opposite sides \(MB\), \(AN\); the square on the other diagonal \(BN\) will be equal to the square on the first increased by the two squares on the other two sides.

Dem. Let \(AB\) be bisected at \(m\) (fig. 7) by the perpendicular \(Mm\) (I.10, 11), and upon \(BA\) produced let \(An = Bm\) be taken; then \(mn = BA = MN\); and hence \(MNnm\) is a parallelogram (I.33), and angle \(NnB\) will be right (I.27). Therefore \((BN)^2 = (AB)^2 + (AN)^2 + 2AB \cdot An\) (II.12). But \(An = \frac{1}{2} AB\). Therefore \((NB)^2 = (AN)^2 + 2(AB)^2 = (AN)^2 + (AB)^2 + (MN)^2\).

26. If in any triangle \(BPE\) the base \(BE\) be cut into two equal parts at \(A\), and from the opposite angle \(P\) the straight line \(PA\) be drawn (fig. 8); the sum of the squares on the sides \(BP\) and \(PE\) will be equal to the sum of the equal squares on the two segments, increased by twice the square on \(AP\).

Dem. For if the perpendicular \(PR\) be dropped upon the base \(BE\), \((BP)^2 = (BA)^2 + (AP)^2 + 2BA \cdot AR\) (II.12). Likewise \((PE)^2 = (AE)^2 + (AP)^2 - 2AE \cdot AR\) (II.13). Taking therefore the sum of the values of the two squares \((BP)^2\) and \((PE)^2\), since \(BA = AE\), we shall have \((BP)^2 + (PE)^2 = (BA)^2 + (AE)^2 + 2(AP)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2\).
