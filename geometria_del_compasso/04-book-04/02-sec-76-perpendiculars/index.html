§§ 76–77: Erecting a Perpendicular at an Endpoint¶

76. Prob. Given the two points \(A\) and \(B\), to find a point \(H\) such that the straight line \(BH\) is perpendicular to \(AB\) at \(B\) and equal to a given distance \(CD\) (fig. 26).

Sol. With center \(B\) and distance \(CD\) as radius, describe a circle \(FEHG\). With center \(A\) and distance \(AB\) as radius, describe an arc cutting the circle at \(F\) and \(E\). Determine the semicircumference \(FEG\) (§ 64). Bisect the arc \(GE\) at \(H\) (§ 60). Then \(H\) will be the point sought.

Dem. By the similarity of the two triangles \(GEB, EBA\) (§ 22), angle \(GEB = EBA\), and hence \(GE\) is parallel to \(BA\) (I.28). But \(BH\), which bisects the angle \(GBE\), is perpendicular to the chord \(GE\) (I.9, 11, 12). Therefore it is also perpendicular to \(BA\) (I.28). Moreover \(BH = CD\). Hence \(H\) is the point sought.

If \(AB\) should be too much less than \(CD\), double or triple it, etc. (§§ 64, 65).

77. Prob. Given the two points \(A\) and \(B\), to find a point \(D\) such that \(DA\) is perpendicular to \(AB\) (fig. 27).

Sol. Taking an arbitrary radius (for example \(AB\) itself),

with it and with the two centers \(A\) and \(B\), describe two arcs intersecting at \(C\). With this same radius and center \(C\), describe the semicircumference \(BAD\) (§ 64). Then \(D\) will be the point sought.

Dem. The angle \(DAB\) is in a semicircle. Therefore it is a right angle (III.31).
