Book IV: Perpendiculars & Parallels by Compass Alone¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (La Geometria del Compasso, Pavia 1797, §§ 76–80, pp. 40–45)

In This Book

  • § 1. Erecting a Perpendicular at a Given Point (§§ 76–77)

  • § 2. Dropping a Perpendicular & Finding its Foot (§§ 78–79)

  • § 3. Constructing a Parallel Through an External Point (§ 80)

f. 0067 · p. 42

§ 1. Erecting a Perpendicular at a Given Point (§§ 76–77)¶

To erect a perpendicular \(DA\) to line \((A, B)\) at point \(A\):

  1. Choose an arbitrary radius \(r > \frac{1}{2}AB\). Sweep arcs from \(A\) and \(B\) intersecting at an auxiliary vertex \(C\).

  2. With center \(C\) and radius \(CA\), double diameter \(BC\) across \(C\) to point \(D\) via collinear doubling (Book III, § 64).

  3. By Euclid III.31 (an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle), \(\angle BAD = 90^\circ\). The virtual line \((D, A)\) is strictly perpendicular to \((A, B)\).

Operation Count: \(6C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 9, E = 6\), 3 circles, 0 lines).

f. 0068 · p. 43

§ 2. Dropping a Perpendicular & Finding its Foot (§§ 78–79)¶

Given line \((A, B)\) and an external point \(D\):

  1. Sweep arc \(A(AD)\) and arc \(B(BD)\). Their opposite intersection \(E\) is the exact orthogonal reflection of \(D\) across line \((A, B)\).

  2. The virtual line \((D, E)\) represents the perpendicular dropped from \(D\) onto \((A, B)\) (\(4C_1 + 2C_3, S = 6\)).

  3. Bisecting the vertical segment \(DE\) via Book III, § 66 (Solution V) yields point \(M\), the exact orthogonal projection (foot of the perpendicular) of \(D\) onto \((A, B)\)!

f. 0068 · p. 43

§ 3. Constructing a Parallel Through an External Point (§ 80)¶

Given line \((A, B)\) and an external point \(C\), determine a point \(D\) such that \((C, D) \parallel (A, B)\):

  1. With center \(B\) and radius equal to distance \(AC\), sweep arc \(B(AC)\).

  2. With center \(C\) and radius equal to distance \(AB\), sweep arc \(C(AB)\).

  3. Let \(D\) be their intersection on the appropriate side of \(C\).

Geometric Proof: In quadrilateral \(ABDC\), the opposite sides are equal by construction: \(AB = CD\) and \(AC = BD\). By Euclid I.34, \(ABDC\) is a parallelogram. Therefore, the virtual line \((C, D)\) is parallel to \((A, B)\).

Operation Count: \(5C_1 + 2C_3\) (\(S = 7, E = 5\), 2 circles, 0 lines).

This matches or beats the best ruler-and-compass parallel constructions (such as Tarry’s \(S = 9\)), completely dispensing with straightedge alignment!
