Book III: Collinear Multiplication & Division of Distances¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (La Geometria del Compasso, Pavia 1797, §§ 64–68, pp. 31–36)

In This Book

  • § 1. Collinear Doubling of Distance AB (§ 64)

  • § 2. Bisecting an Un-Drawn Segment AB (§ 66, Solution V)

  • § 3. Repeated Subdivision and Trisection (§§ 67–68)

f. 0057 · p. 31

§ 1. Collinear Doubling of Distance AB (§ 64)¶

To produce a point \(E\) collinear with two given points \(A\) and \(B\) such that \(AE = 2\,AB\) without a ruler:

  1. With center \(A\) and radius \(AB\), sweep the circle \(A(AB)\).

  2. Stepping the radius \(AB\) three consecutive times around the circumference from \(B\) generates points \(C, D, E\).

  3. Because each equilateral chord subtends \(60^\circ\), the three chords span an exact semicircle of \(180^\circ\). Hence \(E\) lies on the diameter opposite \(B\), placing \(E, A, B\) in strict collinearity with \(EB = 2\,AB\).

Operation Count: \(5C_1 + 4C_3\) (\(S = 9, E = 5\), 4 circles, 0 lines).

f. 0058 · p. 32

§ 2. Bisecting an Un-Drawn Segment AB (§ 66, Solution V)¶

Mascheroni provides five solutions for segment bisection. Solution V is celebrated as the most elegant:

  1. Double segment \(BA\) past \(A\) to point \(E\) via § 64 (\(BE = 2\,BA\)).

  2. With center \(E\) and radius \(EB = 2\,AB\), describe arc \(PBp\).

  3. With center \(B\) and radius \(BA\), sweep circle \(B(BA)\) cutting the previous arc at \(P\) and \(p\).

  4. With centers \(P\) and \(p\) and radius \(BA\), sweep two arcs intersecting at point \(M\).

  5. Point \(M\) is the exact midpoint of segment \(AB\)!

Exact Algebraic Verification: Taking \(A = (0, 0)\) and \(B = (1, 0)\): * \(E = (-1, 0)\), so \(EB = 2\). * Circle \(E(EB)\): \((x + 1)^2 + y^2 = 4\). * Circle \(B(BA)\): \((x - 1)^2 + y^2 = 1\). * Radicall-plane intersection: \(4x = 3 \implies x = \frac{3}{4}\), yielding \(P, p = \left(\frac{3}{4}, \pm \frac{\sqrt{15}}{4}\right)\). * Circles centered at \(P, p\) of radius \(1\): \(\left(x - \frac{3}{4}\right)^2 + \frac{15}{16} = 1 \implies \left(x - \frac{3}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{16}\). * Roots: \(x = \frac{3}{4} \pm \frac{1}{4} \implies x_1 = \frac{1}{2}, x_2 = 1\). * The root \(x = \frac{1}{2}\) lies precisely midway between \(A(0, 0)\) and \(B(1, 0)\).

Operation Count: \(10C_1 + 7C_3\) (\(S = 17, E = 10\), 7 circles, 0 lines).

f. 0060 · p. 34

§ 3. Repeated Subdivision and Trisection (§§ 67–68)¶

  • § 67. Repeated Bisection: Applying Solution V recursively to \(AM\) yields the quarter point \(N\), eighth point \(O\), and any dyadic fraction \(k/2^n \cdot AB\).

  • § 68. Trisection: Combining collinear doubling (§ 64) with proportional distance transfer determines the exact third \(\frac{1}{3} AB\) with compass alone.
