Book X: Determination of Centers & Napoleon’s Problem¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (La Geometria del Compasso, Pavia 1797, §§ 143–147, pp. 82–85)

In This Book

  • § 1. Finding the Center of a Given Circle: Napoleon’s Problem (§ 143)

  • § 2. Finding the Center of a Circle Passing Through Three Points (§ 144)

  • § 3. Foundational Duality Between Mascheroni and Poncelet–Steiner

f. 0107 · p. 82

§ 1. Finding the Center of a Given Circle: Napoleon’s Problem (§ 143)¶

A circle \(\Gamma\) is drawn on paper, but its center \(O\) is unmarked. Find \(O\) using only the compass:

  1. Select an arbitrary point \(A\) on circumference \(\Gamma\).

  2. With an arbitrary radius \(r\), sweep an arc from \(A\) cutting \(\Gamma\) at two points \(B\) and \(C\).

  3. With centers \(B\) and \(C\) and radius \(r = AB\), sweep arcs intersecting at point \(D\) (opposite to \(A\)).

  4. Segment \(AD\) lies along the diameter of \(\Gamma\) through \(A\).

  5. Construct the inverse of point \(D\) with respect to circle \(A(r)\) using compass inversion (Book VII, § 3).

  6. The inverse point is the exact center O of circle Γ!

Operation Count: \(9C_1 + 6C_3\) (\(S = 15, E = 9\), 6 circles, 0 lines).

f. 0108 · p. 83

§ 2. Finding the Center of a Circle Passing Through Three Points (§ 144)¶

Given three non-collinear points \(M, A, B\):

  1. Find the orthogonal reflection lines of \(MA\) and \(AB\) using Book IV, § 78.

  2. Intersecting the two perpendicular bisectors via Book VII, § 112 yields the circumcenter \(O = X(3)\) of triangle \(\triangle MAB\) using compass alone.

f. 0109 · p. 84

§ 3. Foundational Duality Between Mascheroni and Poncelet–Steiner¶

This problem illustrates the profound epistemological duality between minimal instruments:

  1. Mascheroni (1797): A drawn circle \(\Gamma\) is given without its center; the compass can find center O directly (§ 143) because circular arcs generate quadratic metric distances.

  2. Poncelet–Steiner (1833): In Jakob Steiner’s regime (steiner-1833), a straightedge can solve all Euclidean problems only if the center M of the fixed circle is permanently given. If \(M\) were omitted, the straightedge can never find it, because hyperbolic projective transformations preserving the circular boundary move the Euclidean center while preserving all straight lines!
