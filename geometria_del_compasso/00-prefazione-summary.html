Summary: The Scope and Axiomatic Foundation of Compass-Only Geometry¶

Structural Overview of Mascheroni’s 1797 Treatise and Operational Principles

1. The Postulate of the Single Instrument¶

In classical Euclidean geometry (euclid-heath), constructive power rests upon three postulates:

  • Postulate 1: To draw a straight line from any point to any point.

  • Postulate 2: To produce a finite straight line continuously in a straight line.

  • Postulate 3: To describe a circle with any center and radius.

Lorenzo Mascheroni proved that Postulates 1 and 2 can be entirely eliminated for all point-determination problems in plane geometry. The geometer requires only Postulate 3.

2. The Virtual Line Principle¶

Because a continuous straight line cannot be traced without a straightedge, Mascheroni introduces the fundamental concept of the virtual line:

  • Any straight line \(L\) is determined and represented by two distinct points \((A, B)\).

  • A third point \(C\) is said to lie on line \((A, B)\) if its distance from \(A\) and \(B\) satisfies the collinear triangle-inequality degenerate limit \(AB + BC = AC\) or \(AC + CB = AB\).

  • All operations on straight lines—drawing parallels, erecting perpendiculars, reflecting across lines, and intersecting lines with circles or with other lines—are executed purely by intersecting circular arcs.
