§§ 108–109: Acute-Angle Refinement & Alternative Root Scheme¶

f. 0084 · p. 59

108. In the preceding construction, care was taken to employ as much as possible the first compass of opening \(= 1\), with which the circumference \(BDd\) was described, which, preserving the fundamental opening, deserves more trust than the others. Likewise, it was desired to employ only the three most notable first compasses (§ 107). This caused a few intersections of the arcs to turn out at somewhat acute angles, such as those of points \(S, s, O, o\), and even more those of points \(L\) and \(l\). Whoever wishes to have all angles of intersection closer to a right angle may use the following

Another construction of fig. 45.

109. Having found the points \(M\) and \(m\) as in the solution of § 104, with radius \(BD\) and centers \(M\) and \(m\), describe two arcs intersecting at \(H\).

With radius \(AB\) and center \(H\), describe an arc cutting the circumference at \(O\) and \(o\). With the same radius, determine the semicircumferences \(OEs, oES\) (§ 64).

With radius \(BE\) and center \(H\), cut the circumference at \(L\) and \(l\).

Let all the other points of the figure be found as in § 104.

We shall demonstrate that the points found by this construction are the same as those of the other.

Since it has been proved (§ 104) that \(BM = MR = Rm = mB\), and that the three points \(B, A, R\) lie on the same straight line; and since \(MH = ME = mH = mE\) by construction, \(H\) will lie on the same straight line \(BAR\) (§ 13), and we shall have \(HB = RE\) (§ 14). Therefore \(H\) will be the same point as in the other construction. Then since \(HO, Ho\) are equal in the two constructions, the points \(O, o\) will also be the same. If we subtract the arc \(osE\) from the two semicircumferences \(BsE, oES\), the remaining arcs \(Bo, ES\) will be equal. Hence \(ES = Bo = BO = AQ = RQ\). Therefore point \(S\) will also be the same as before. Likewise so will point \(s\). Next, since the base \(HTR\) of the triangle \(HLR\) is bisected by \(LT\), we shall have (§ 26) \((HL)^2 + (LR)^2 = 2(HT)^2 + 2(TL)^2\); that is, \(4 + (LR)^2 = 2 + 2(TL)^2\), or \(2 + (LR)^2 = 2(TL)^2\). But since the base \(TR\) of the

f. 0085 · p. 60

triangle \(TLR\) is also bisected at \(A\) by the straight line \(LA\), we shall have (§ 26) \((TL)^2 + (LR)^2 = 2(TA)^2 + 2(AL)^2\); and doubling, \(2(TL)^2 + 2(LR)^2 = 4(TA)^2 + 4(AL)^2 = 1 + 4 = 5\); hence subtracting \(2(LR)^2\), we have \(2(TL)^2 = 5 - 2(LR)^2\). But above we found \(2(TL)^2 = 2 + (LR)^2\). Therefore \(5 - 2(LR)^2 = 2 + (LR)^2\). And subtracting 2 and adding \(2(LR)^2\), we have \(3 = 3(LR)^2\); hence \(1 = (LR)^2 = (AB)^2\). Therefore \(LR = AB\) as in the first construction. The same will be proved of \(Rl\). The other points are determined as before. Therefore all the points of the figure are the same as before.
