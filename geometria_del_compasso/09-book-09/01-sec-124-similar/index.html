§§ 124–127: Similar Figures & Area Ratios¶

BOOK NINTH — OF SIMILAR FIGURES AND REGULAR POLYGONS

124. Notice. When we say: to construct a figure or a polygon, we shall mean: to find all those points that suffice to determine the position and magnitude of those straight lines which must be drawn to construct the polygon entirely.

125. Problem. Upon a given side \(ab\) (fig. 51) to construct a triangle similar to a given triangle \(ABC\).

Solution. See § 115.

126. Problem. To construct a figure similar to a given one (fig. 58) \(ABCEFD\), having a given ratio of area to it.

Solution. Let it be desired, for example, that the new figure have two fifths of the area of the given figure. With a radius \(AB\), as large as may conveniently be taken, construct figure 43 (§ 100). Taking from that figure \(Aa = \sqrt{2}\) as radius, and with center \(O\) (fig. 58), describe the smaller circle, on whose circumference lies point \(v\). Taking from fig. 43 \(ET = \sqrt{5}\) as radius, with the same center \(O\) describe the

f. 0094 · p. 69

greater circle, on whose circumference lies point \(V\), which is marked in such a place with respect to point \(v\) that \(Vv\) turns out nearly tangent to the inner circle at \(v\).

Suppose the given figure divided into so many triangles \(ABC\), \(ACD\), \(CDE\), \(EDF\), imagining lines \(AC\), \(CD\), etc.

Assign numbers \(1, 2, 3, 4, 5\), etc. to the distances \(AB\), \(AC\), \(BC\), \(CD\), etc., which measure the sides of these triangles.

Apply these distances \(1, 2, 3\), etc. successively as chords to so many successive arcs of circle \(V\), namely \(AB\) from \(V\) to \(1\); \(AC\) from \(1\) to \(2\); \(BC\) from \(2\) to \(3\), etc. to the end; which is done by taking distance \(AB\) as radius, and with center \(V\), cutting this same circumference with an arc at \(1\), etc.

With distance \(Vv\) taken as radius, take center successively at points \(1, 2, 3, 4\), etc., and describe arcs cutting the smaller circumference successively at \(1, 2, 3, 4\), etc. to the end.

The chords of the inner circle, namely the distances from \(v\) to \(1\), from \(1\) to \(2\), from \(2\) to \(3\), from \(3\) to \(4\), etc., will be the sides \(ab\), \(ac\), \(bc\), \(cd\), etc. of the new figure, which will be constructed triangle by triangle. With the distance from \(v\) to \(1\) mark the two points \(ab\). With centers \(a\) and \(b\), and with the second and third distances taken from the inner circle (the second is from 1 to 2; the third from 2 to 3), mark two arcs intersecting at \(c\). With centers \(c\) and \(a\), and with the fourth and fifth distances taken from the inner circle (the fourth is from 3 to 4, the fifth from 4 to 5), mark two arcs intersecting at \(d\). In the same manner points \(e\) and \(f\) will be found, and the figure will be constructed.

Demonstration. The chords of the inner circle stand to the respective chords of the outer circle as radius \(Ov\) stands to radius \(OV\) (§ 93), that is, as \(\sqrt{2}\) to \(\sqrt{5}\). Therefore all sides of the triangles of figure \(abcefd\) stand in the same ratio to the respective sides of the triangles of figure \(ABCEFD\). Hence the triangles of the two figures are equiangular and similar to one another (5, Book VI, def. 1). Therefore these same two polygonal figures are similar (20, Book VI), and since the proportion, or ratio of the areas of polygons, is the duplicate of the ratio of the sides (ibid.), the area of polygon \(abcefd\) will be to the area \(ABCEFD\) as \(2\) to \(5\). Thus

the area of the lesser figure will equal two fifths of the greater.

From this example one sees what must be done, whatever other ratio may be desired for the area of the figure to be constructed to the given one. Two circumferences \(v\) and \(V\) shall be described, whose radii shall stand to one another in the ratio of the roots of the numbers forming the ratio of the areas. On the circumference corresponding to the given figure, place successively as chords the sides of the triangles into which the given figure is supposed divided. By means of these, with the indicated method, proportional chords will be found on the other circumference. These will be the respective sides of the triangles of the new figure.

Since the ratio of the half roots and that of the whole roots is the same, fig. 45 (§ 104) will serve in many cases. For the other cases see §§ 101, 102, and 103.

One chooses moreover a radius \(AB\) as large as may conveniently be taken (fig. 44 and 45), so that the two circles may better contain the lengths of the chords to be applied to them, and so that the intersections arising therefrom may make angles nearer to a right angle.

127. If the ratio were given between the sides \(AB\) and \(ab\) of the figures themselves, or between any other two straight lines; in this ratio one would choose the two radii \(OV\), \(Ov\) as large as possible.
