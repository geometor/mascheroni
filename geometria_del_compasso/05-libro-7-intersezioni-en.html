Book VII: Intersection of Lines and Circles by Compass Alone¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (La Geometria del Compasso, Pavia 1797, §§ 110–112, pp. 60–63)

In This Book

  • § 1. Intersecting a Virtual Line with a Circle (§§ 110–111)

  • § 2. Intersecting Two Virtual Lines (§ 112)

  • § 3. August Adler’s Inversion Functor (1890)

f. 0085 · p. 60

§ 1. Intersecting a Virtual Line with a Circle (§§ 110–111)¶

To find the points where a virtual line \((P, Q)\) intersects circle \(\Gamma = A(R)\) without drawing the line:

  1. Reflect the circle center \(A\) across line \((P, Q)\) using Book IV, § 78: sweep arcs \(P(PA)\) and \(Q(QA)\) meeting at \(A'\).

  2. With center \(A'\) and the original radius \(R\), describe the reflected circle \(\Gamma' = A'(R)\).

  3. Circles \(\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma'\) intersect at two points \(X\) and \(Y\).

  4. Because \((P, Q)\) is the perpendicular bisector (line of symmetry) between centers \(A\) and \(A'\), every intersection of \(\Gamma\) and \(\Gamma'\) must lie on line \((P, Q)\).

  5. Therefore, \(X\) and \(Y\) are the exact intersections of the line and the circle!

Operation Count: \(7C_1 + 3C_3\) (\(S = 10, E = 7\), 3 circles, 0 lines).

f. 0086 · p. 61

§ 2. Intersecting Two Virtual Lines (§ 112)¶

Given two virtual lines \((A, B)\) and \((C, D)\), determine their point of intersection \(S\):

  1. Reflect points \(C\) and \(D\) across line \((A, B)\) to obtain \(c\) and \(d\).

  2. Construct the fourth vertex \(\delta\) of parallelogram \(CDd\delta\) using Book IV, § 80.

  3. Using the concentric fourth proportional (Book V, § 93), compute radius:

    \[CS = \frac{CD \cdot Cc}{c\delta}.\]

  4. Arcs of radius \(CS\) centered at \(C\) and \(c\) intersect at point \(S\). Point \(S\) lies simultaneously on \((C, D)\) and on the axis of symmetry \((A, B)\), yielding the exact meet of the two lines!

f. 0088 · p. 63

§ 3. August Adler’s Inversion Functor (1890)¶

In 1890, August Adler (adler-1890) established the modern conceptual completion of the Mohr–Mascheroni theorem via circular inversion (lemoine-1902, Problem XXXVIII: \(5C_1 + 3C_3, S = 8\)):

  • Under circular inversion with respect to reference circle \(\Gamma(O, R)\), every straight line not passing through \(O\) transforms into a circle passing through \(O\).

  • Consequently, the problem of intersecting two straight lines \(L_1 \cap L_2\) transforms into intersecting two circles passing through \(O\).

  • The circles intersect at \(O\) and at an inverse point \(S'\). Inverting \(S'\) back through \(\Gamma\) recovers the intersection \(S\)!

  • This unified functor provides the ultimate algebraic proof that compass geometry is isomorphic to ruler-and-compass geometry over the Euclidean quadratic closure.
