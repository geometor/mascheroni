§§ 267–272: Circle Rectification, Radian Arc & Squaring (√π)¶

267. Problem. In a circle (Fig. 101) of given radius \(AB\), to find a chord \(Bb\) approximately equal to one quarter of the circumference.

Sol. In the circumference make \(AB = BC = CD = DE\). Then make \(BD = Ba = Ea\). With center \(C\) and radius \(Ca\) mark an arc cutting the circumference at \(b\). The chord \(Bb\) will be the chord sought.

Demonstration. Supposing \(AB = 1\), set \(BC = A = 60^\circ\) in equation (1); there will result \(A' = 43^\circ 33' \frac{286}{2005} = Cb\). The arc \(BCb\) will therefore be \(103^\circ 33' \frac{286}{2005}\). Its half \(51^\circ 46' \frac{1145}{1005}\) has for sine \(0.7855998\). The chord \(Bb\) will therefore be \(1.5711996\). Now a quarter of the circumference is \(= 1.5707963\). The error is therefore about \(0.0004\).

268. According to the proportion of Archimedes, setting the radius \(= 1\), one finds a quarter of the circumference \(= \frac{11}{7} = 1.5714\). Therefore the construction of this problem (§ 267) gives a closer approximation. Since this construction is very simple, it will be preferable to use in practice rather than others that we might add, which would indeed give a greater theoretical approximation, but would be more complicated, and hence more liable to error.

269. Problem. In a circle (Fig. 102) of given radius \(AB\), to find the arc approximately equal to the same radius.

Sol. In the circumference \(BLCMFDOEd\) make \(AB = BC\)

\(= CD = DE = Ed\). Then make \(BD = Ba = Ea\). Then \(Aa = BF = Db = db = aL\). Then \(AB = FO\). Then finally \(bF = OM\). The arc \(LM\) will be approximately equal to the radius.

Demonstration. If in equation (4) we introduce \(A' = 90^\circ\), of which arc \(aL\) is chord, there will result \(BL = A = 20^\circ 42' \frac{786}{1721}\). Then since \(OM = bF\) is chord of the fifth part of the circumference (§ 40), that is, of \(72^\circ\), and since arc \(FO = 60^\circ\), arc \(FM\) will be \(= 12^\circ\). Hence arc \(LM = BF - BL - FM = 57^\circ 17' \frac{1035}{2721} = 57^\circ 17' 43''+\). Now the arc equal to the radius is, as is known, \(= 57^\circ 17' 44''\). Therefore, etc.

270. Problem. To find (Fig. 103) the side of a square that may be approximately equal in area to a circle of given radius \(AB\).

Sol. In the circumference \(BPCQDRE\) make \(AB = BC = CD = DE\), and with the same radius \(BA\), and centers \(B\) and \(E\), describe the two arcs \(ALc\), \(AMd\). With centers \(C\) and \(D\), and radius \(DB\), describe the arcs \(cNM\), \(dNL\). Make \(AN = BP = PQ\); then \(LM = QR\). \(BR\) will be the side sought.

Demonstration. If in the isosceles triangle \(CND\) the base \(CD = 1\) is supposed bisected at \(\mu\), we shall have \((CN)^2 = (C\mu)^2 + (N\mu)^2\), that is (§ 2), \(3 = \frac{1}{4} + (N\mu)^2\); hence \(N\mu = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11}\). Next we have \((CA)^2 = (C\mu)^2 + (A\mu)^2\), and hence \(A\mu = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}\). Therefore \(AN = \frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{11} - \sqrt{3}) = 0.7922869\), which is found to be the chord of an arc \(= 46^\circ 40' \frac{1272}{2071}\)

\(= BP = PQ\). Therefore the arc \(BQ\) will be \(= 93^\circ 20' \frac{2544}{2671}\). Let the straight lines \(BL\) bisected at \(n\), \(BD\), \(Dn\), \(BE\), and \(D\delta\), \(Ll\), \(Mm\) perpendicular to the same \(BE\) at \(\delta\), \(l\), and \(m\) be drawn.

Since the angle \(DBE = 30^\circ\) (III.20), we shall have \(\sin DBE = \frac{1}{2}\); \(\cos DBE = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}\). Then by Trigonometry \(\cos DBn = \frac{Bn}{DB}\); \(\sin DBn = \frac{Dn}{DB}\); that is, \(\cos DBn = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}\); and since \(Dn = \sqrt{(DB)^2 - (Bn)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11}\), \(\sin DBn = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33}\). Then (§ 154) \(\cos LBl = \cos(DBn - DBE) = \cos DBn \cdot \cos DBE + \sin DBn \cdot \sin DBE = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} + \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{12}(3 + \sqrt{33}) = \frac{Bl}{BL} = Bl\) (since \(BL = BA = 1\)). Therefore \(Al = AB - Bl = \frac{1}{12}(9 - \sqrt{33})\), and hence \(lm = LM = 2Al = \frac{1}{6}(9 - \sqrt{33}) = 0.5425729\), which is found to be the chord of the arc \(= 31^\circ 28' \frac{2518}{2799} = QR\). The arc \(BQR\) will therefore be \(= 124^\circ 49' \frac{6370}{7476}\). Its half \(62^\circ 24' \frac{6923}{7476}\) has for sine \(0.8863283\), and therefore the chord of \(BQR\) will be \(= 1.7726566\). Now setting the radius \(= 1\), we have the area of the circle \(= 3.1415926\), which has for logarithm \(0.4971499\). Half of this logarithm, that is \(0.2485749 = \log \sqrt{3.1415926}\), is found to be \(\log 1.772453\). We have therefore an error of about \(0.0002\), which gives a sufficient approximation.

271. Instead of \(AN\), one could have used \(dL\) or \(cM\), which must be found equal to the same \(AN\).

Demonstration. If \(dL\) be drawn, and \(Dp\) to its midpoint at \(p\), we shall have \(\sin LDp = \frac{Lp}{DL}\). But the angle \(LDp = \frac{1}{2} LDd = \frac{1}{2}(BDd - BDL) = 30^\circ - BDn\). Therefore (§ 155) \(\sin LDp = \frac{1}{2}\cos BDn - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \sin BDn = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}\). Hence \(Lp = DL \cdot \sin LDp = \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{4}\sqrt{3}\) and \(Ld = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} = AN\).

272. Problem. Given (Fig. 104) the side \(AB\) of a square, to find the radius of a circle that may be approximately equal to it in area.

Sol. With radius \(AB\), center \(A\), describe the circumference \(BCFDLPMEd\). Make \(AB = BC = CD = Ed\). With center \(B\) and radius \(BD\) describe the arc \(dnNDa\). With the same radius and center \(E\) cut this arc at \(a\). Make \(Aa = BF = Db = db\). Then make \(AB = Dn = dN\). With center \(C\) and radius \(CN\), cut the circumference at \(P\). Make \(Nn = PM\); then \(Fb = CL\). \(LM\) will be the side sought.

Demonstration. If we suppose \(AB = 1\), the triangle \(BNd\) having its sides respectively equal to the sides of triangle \(BDL\) of Fig. 103, we shall have \(\sin \frac{1}{2} dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{3}\); \(\cos \frac{1}{2} dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{33}\) (§ 271). Hence \(\sin dBN = 2\sin \frac{1}{2} dBN \cdot \cos \frac{1}{2} dBN\) (§ 155) \(= \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}\); \(\cos dBN = \sqrt{1 - \sin^2 dBN} = \frac{5}{6}\). Since \(CBd\) is a right angle (III.31), \(\cos CBN\) will be \(= \sin dBN = \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}\). But from Trigonometry we have \((CN)^2 = (BC)^2 + (BN)^2 - 2BC \cdot BN \cdot \cos CBN\). Therefore \(CN = \sqrt{4 - 2\sqrt{3} \cdot \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11}} = \sqrt{4 - \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{33}} = 1.4440025\), which is found to be the chord of \(92^\circ 26' \frac{804}{2013} = CP\). Since then \(\sin NBE = \frac{\frac{1}{2} Nd}{BN} = \sin(CBE - CBN) =\) (§ 155) \(\sin 60^\circ \cos CBN - \cos 60^\circ \cdot \sin CBN = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{6}\sqrt{11} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{5}{6}\); we shall have \(Nn = 2BN \times \sin NBE = \frac{1}{6}(\sqrt{11} - 5\sqrt{3}) = 0.2149367\), which is found to be the chord of \(12^\circ 20' \frac{946}{2892} = PM\).

The arc \(CPM\) will therefore be \(= 104^\circ 46' \frac{4220}{5821}\), from which subtracting the arc \(CL\), which is a fifth part of the circumference (§ 40) \(= 72^\circ\), there will remain the arc \(LM = 32^\circ 46' \frac{4220}{5821}\), whose chord is found \(= 0.5643274\).

Now setting \(\pi\) for the ratio of the circumference to the diameter, and \(R\) for the radius of a circle, its area is \(= \pi R^2\), as is known. Making therefore \(\pi R^2 = 1\), which is the area of the square of radius \(AB = 1\) to which the circle is desired equal, we shall have \(R = \sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi}}\), and \(\log R = -\frac{1}{2}\log \pi = -0.2485749 = -1 + 0.7514251 = \log 0.5641896\). We shall not therefore commit an error of \(0.0002\).
