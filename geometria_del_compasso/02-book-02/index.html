Book II: Division of the Circumference¶

Libro Secondo: Della divisione della circonferenza e degli archi del cerchio (§§ 27–63)

Exact compass-only division of the circumference and circular arcs (§§ 27–63, pp. 19–30, folios 0048–0056 + documented scan lacuna pp. 28–31): opening with the iconic quadrisection of the circle (§ 27) and culminating in the regular pentagon, decagon, Golden Ratio, and 240-part division using only three compass apertures.

§§ 27–29: Quadrisection of the Circumference

§ 27. Problem (Fig. 9). Divide a given circumference (with known center A and radius AB) into four equal parts using the compass alone.

English · Italian · Summary · Notes

§§ 30–35: Division into 8, 12, and 24 Parts

§§ 30–32. Building directly on Fig. 9 (folio 0048), Mascheroni divides the circumference into 8 equal parts (\(45^\circ\)) by sweeping unit radius \(AB = 1\) from the quadrisection auxiliary point \(a\) (cutting the circle at \(G\) and \(H\), since \(AG = 1, Aa = \sqrt{2}, aG = 1 \implies \angle A a G = 45^\circ\)), into 12 equal parts (\(30^\circ\)) by stepping unit radius from \(F\) and \(f\), and into 24 equal parts (\(15^\circ\)) by combining the 8-part and 12-part divisions (\(\gcd(8,12)\) yields \(45^\circ - 30^\circ = 15^\circ\)).

English · Italian · Summary · Notes

§§ 36–39: Chord Lemmas & 48-Part Division

§§ 36–37 (Fig. 10). Mascheroni proves the exact radical half-chord relations \(\sin 67^\circ 30' = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2 + \sqrt{2}}\) and \(\sin 22^\circ 30' = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{2 - \sqrt{2}}\), showing how nested square roots arise directly from repeated compass intersections on a single circle.

§§ 40–44: Regular Pentagon, Decagon & 120-Part Circle

§§ 40–41. Problem (Fig. 12). Divide the circumference into 5 and 10 equal parts (inscribed regular pentagon and decagon) by the compass alone.

English · Italian · Summary · Notes

§§ 45–63: Golden Ratio Proof, 240-Part Circle & Arc Bisection

§§ 45–52 (pp. 26–27, folio 0055–folio 0056). Mascheroni proves rigorously that point \(b\) of Fig. 12 divides radius \(AB\) in extreme and mean ratio (\(AB : Ab :: Ab : Bb\), § 46), verifies Euclid Book XIII Prop. 10 (\((\text{pentagon})^2 = (\text{hexagon})^2 + (\text{decagon})^2\), § 50), and extends the exact divisions to 20, 60, and 120 parts.
