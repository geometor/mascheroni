Book III § 68: Distance Trisection in a Straight Line¶

Lorenzo Mascheroni (The Geometry of the Compass, Pavia 1797, § 68, pp. 36–37)

  1. Problem. To divide the distance \(AB\) into three equal parts.

Solution. With radius \(AB\) and center \(B\) (fig. 20), describe circle \(BCDE\), and set \(AB = BC = CD = DE\) (§ 10). Then with center \(E\) and radius \(EC\), describe the indefinite arc \(QCPq\). With center \(A\) and radius \(AB\), cut this arc in \(P\) and \(p\). With centers \(P\) and \(p\) and with the same radius \(AB\), describe two arcs intersecting in \(M\). The distance \(BM\) will be one-third of \(AB\). If then with center \(M\) and the same radius \(MB\) an arc be described cutting \(AB\) in \(N\) toward \(A\), the distance \(AB\) will be divided into three equal parts at points \(M\) and \(N\).

Demonstration. The point \(M\) will lie on line \(BE\) (§ 13). Since \(BC = CD = AB\), we have \(BD = \sqrt{3} AB\) (§ 2); and since \(BE = 2 AB\) (§ 64), in the right triangle \(\triangle BCE\) inscribed in the semicircle (Euclid III.31):

\[(EC)^2 = (BE)^2 - (BC)^2 = 4(AB)^2 - (AB)^2 = 3(AB)^2.\]

Hence \(EP = EC = \sqrt{3} AB\).

Now the two isosceles triangles \(\triangle PBE\) and \(\triangle PBM\) are similar, having their base angle at \(B\) in common (Euclid I.5, I.32; VI.4).

Therefore:

\[BE : BP = BP : BM.\]

Consequently \(BE \cdot BM = (BP)^2\). With \(E\) placed at distance \(3 AB\) along the line:

\[3 AB \cdot BM = (AB)^2.\]

Dividing by \(AB\), we obtain \(3 BM = AB\), or \(BM = \frac{1}{3} AB\). Thus \(BM\) is one-third of \(AB\), and \(N\) marks the second trisection point.
