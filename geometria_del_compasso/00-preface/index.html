Preface & Dedication to Bonaparte¶

Prefazione e Dedica a Bonaparte

Frontispiece portrait, Palermo centenary title page (1901), Gaetano Fazzari’s biographical and geometrographic preface (pp. III–XVI), Lorenzo Mascheroni’s poetic dedication to General Napoléon Bonaparte (p. 1), and Mascheroni’s own preface laying out the compass-only program across all twelve books (pp. 3–9).

Portrait & Centenary Title Page

The archival witness digitized for this GEOMETOR Scriptorium edition is the centenary reprint edited by Gaetano Fazzari, La Geometria del Compasso (Nuova edizione) (Palermo: Premiata Casa Editrice «Era Nova», 1901; Harvard Science Center Library copy Math 5409.01), reproducing Lorenzo Mascheroni’s original 1797 Pavia treatise (Presso gli Eredi di Pietro Galeazzi, Anno V) together with all twelve books, sections §§ 1–280, and 108 inline chalkboard figures.

Fazzari’s Biographical Preface (pp. III–IX)

In the opening half of his historical preface (Lorenzo Mascheroni e le sue opere matematiche, pp. III–IX), Gaetano Fazzari traces Lorenzo Mascheroni’s formation in Bergamo (born 13 May 1750 at Castagneta), his early literary studies, his appointment to the chair of algebra and geometry at the University of Pavia (1786), and his seminal Adnotationes ad calculum integralem Euleri (1790), which introduced the Euler–Mascheroni constant \(\gamma\) to thirty-two decimal places.

English · Italian · Summary · Notes

Fazzari on Geometrography & Legacy (pp. X–XVI)

Continuing his centenary introduction (pp. X–XVI), Gaetano Fazzari recounts General Napoléon Bonaparte’s celebrated exchange in Paris with Pierre-Simon Laplace and Joseph-Louis Lagrange, where Bonaparte posed Mascheroni’s compass-only circle quadrisection (§ 27) and center-finding (§ 143) problems, prompting Laplace’s famous quip: «Nous attendions tout de vous, général, excepté des leçons de géométrie.»

Mascheroni’s Preface: Purpose & Precision

In the first half of his Prefazione (pp. 3–6, folio 0032–folio 0035), Lorenzo Mascheroni explains why a geometry restricted strictly to the compass is not merely an abstract curiosity, but a practical instrument of superior precision for astronomers, navigators, and astronomical instrument makers (such as Ramsden, Bird, and Graham dividing mural quadrants and sextants).
