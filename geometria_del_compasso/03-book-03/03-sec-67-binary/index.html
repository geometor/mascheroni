§ 67: Repeated Binary Subdivision¶

67. Prob. To continue subdividing into two equal parts by the simplest construction (fig. 18), \(AM\) at \(N\), \(AN\) at \(O\), etc., to infinity.

Sol. I. Having described the semicircumference \(BCDE\) with radius \(AB\) (§ 64), and with the same radius and center \(B\) the indefinite arc \(P'CAp'\); with centers \(E\) and \(B\) and radius \(BE\) the two arcs \(R'Q'P'Bp'q'r'\), \(PQRErqp\); with center \(E\) and radius \(EC\) the arc \(PCp\); if with centers \(P'\) and \(p'\) and radius \(AB\) two arcs be described, they will intersect at \(M\) at the midpoint of \(AB\) (Sol. V, § 66). If with the two centers \(P\) and \(p\) and radius \(PE\) two arcs be described, they will also intersect at the same point \(M\) (Sol. III, § 66).

Now make \(AP' = BQ' = Bq' = q'N = Q'N\) (§ 11). Point \(N\) will be at the midpoint of \(AM\).

Make \(AQ' = BR' = Br' = r'O = R'O\). Point \(O\) will be at the midpoint of \(AN\).

Continuing by the same method, \(AO\) would be divided into two equal parts, etc., to infinity.

Dem. Imagine a straight line \(P'A\), which bisects the base \(BE\) of triangle \(P'BE\) (§ 12). We shall have \((BP')^2 + (P'E)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP')^2\) (§ 26). Hence substituting the values of \(BP' = AB\) and \(P'E = 2\,AB\), and subtracting \(2(AB)^2\), we obtain \(3(AB)^2 = 2(AP')^2\), and dividing by \(2\), \((AP')^2 = (BQ')^2 = \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2\). Then since \(N\) lies on straight line \(BE\) (§ 13), the isosceles triangle \(Q'BN\) will be similar to the isosceles triangle \(Q'BE\) by the common angle at \(B\) (I.5 and 32; VI.4). Hence \((BQ')^2 = BN \cdot BE\) (VI.17). Comparing the two values of \((BQ')^2\), we have \(\frac{3}{2}(AB)^2 = BN \cdot BE = 2\,BN \cdot AB\),

f. 0060 · p. 35

and dividing by \(2\,AB\), we obtain \(\frac{3}{4}AB = BN\). Therefore \(AN = \frac{1}{4}AB\).

Likewise we shall have \((BQ')^2 + (Q'E)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ')^2\) (§ 26), hence \(\frac{3}{2}(AB)^2 + 4(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ')^2\), and reducing, \(\frac{7}{4}(AB)^2 = (AQ')^2 = (BR')^2\). But \((BR')^2 = BO \cdot BE = 2\,AB \cdot BO\). Therefore \(\frac{7}{4}(AB)^2 = 2\,AB \cdot BO\), and hence \(\frac{7}{8}AB = BO\); and \(AO = \frac{1}{8}AB\); etc.

Sol. II. Make \(AP = EQ = Eq = qN = QN\). Point \(N\) will be at the midpoint of \(AM\). Make \(AQ = ER = Er = rO = RO\). Point \(O\) will be at the midpoint of \(AN\). Continuing by the same method, \(AO\) would be bisected, and so on to infinity.

Dem. Since we have (§ 26) \((PE)^2 + (PB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2\), and substituting the values \((PE)^2 = (CE)^2 = 3(AB)^2\) (XIII.12) (§ 2), and \((PB)^2 = (BE)^2 = 4(AB)^2\), we get \(7(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AP)^2\). Hence taking away \(2(AB)^2\) and dividing by \(2\), we obtain \(\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 = (AP)^2 = (EQ)^2\). But \((EQ)^2 = EN \cdot EB\) because of the similar isosceles triangles \(EQN, EQB\) (§ 13) (I.5 and 32; VI.4 and 17). Therefore \(\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 = EN \cdot EB = 2\,EN \cdot AB\); and dividing by \(2\,AB\) gives \(\frac{5}{4}AB = EN\); and \(AN = \frac{1}{4}AB\). Reasoning by the same method, we shall have \((QE)^2 + (QB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ)^2\). Hence \(\frac{5}{2}(AB)^2 + 4(AB)^2 = 2(AB)^2 + 2(AQ)^2\). Hence also \(\frac{9}{4}(AB)^2 = (AQ)^2 = (ER)^2 = EO \cdot EB = 2\,AB \cdot EO\). Therefore dividing by \(2\,AB\), we obtain \(\frac{9}{8}AB = EO\); and \(AO = \frac{1}{8}AB\); etc.

Sol. III. With center \(A\) and radius \(AB\) having described the semicircumference \(BCDE\) (§ 64) (fig. 19), with the same radius \(AB\) and centers \(B\) and \(E\) having described the indefinite arcs \(CP, Dp\), and with the same centers \(B\) and \(E\) and radius \(BE\) the arcs \(Epqr, BPQR\), point \(M\) can be found by making \(PM = PB\) and \(EM = Pp\) (Sol. II, § 66).

Now make \(AP = BQ = QN = Eq\). Make also \(Qq = EN\). Point \(N\) will be at the midpoint of \(AM\). Likewise make \(AQ = BR = RO = Er\). Make also \(Rr = EO\). Point \(O\) will be at the midpoint of \(AN\). Etc.

Dem. Making the due substitutions in Fig. 5 (§ 23),

f. 0061 · p. 36

we shall have \(Qq \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - (BQ)^2\). But \(BE = 2\,AB\); and \((BQ)^2 = \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2\) (see the demonstration of Sol. I). Therefore \(2\,Qq \cdot AB = 4(AB)^2 - \frac{3}{2}(AB)^2\), and dividing by \(2\,AB\) and reducing, we have \(Qq = \frac{5}{4}AB\). Therefore also \(EN = \frac{5}{4}AB\). Hence, \(AB\) being the same in both Figures 18 and 19, the sides of the two triangles \(Q'NE\) (Fig. 18) and \(QNE\) (Fig. 19) will also be the same. Thus superposing the three points \(B, Q, E\) of Fig. 19 upon the three \(B, Q', E\) of 18, the points \(N\) of the two Figures will also coincide. Therefore etc.

Likewise making the due substitutions in Fig. 5 (§ 23), we have \(Rr \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - (BR)^2\). But \((BR)^2 = \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2\) (Dem. of Sol. I); therefore \(Rr \cdot BE = (BE)^2 - \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2\). Or substituting \(2\,AB\) for \(BE\): \(2\,AB \cdot Rr = 4(AB)^2 - \frac{7}{4}(AB)^2\). Dividing by \(2\,AB\) and reducing gives \(Rr = \frac{9}{8}AB = OE\). Therefore, the points \(E, R, B\) of this Fig. 19 coinciding with the points \(E, R', B\) of Fig. 18, and here \(RO\) and \(EO\) being respectively equal to \(R'O, EO\) of Fig. 18, point \(O\) will also coincide. Hence \(O\) will be at the midpoint of \(AN\). In the same manner it would be demonstrated thereafter to infinity.

Other ways of finding the same points could be used; but we shall pass to other divisions of \(AB\) into a different number of parts.
